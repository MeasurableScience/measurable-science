# RM3100 Fluxgate Magnetometer

## Introduction

The RM3100 represents the latest generation of magnetic sensors used throughout the **Measurable Science** project.

It was introduced after the WT901 when greater magnetic sensitivity became necessary for detecting weak magnetic field variations.

---

# Why RM3100?

The magnetometer integrated into the WT901 is an excellent general-purpose sensor and was used for the majority of the project's early measurements.

As the research progressed, experiments required higher magnetic sensitivity and lower noise than a standard MEMS magnetometer could provide.

For this reason, the RM3100 was introduced.

---

# A Revolution in Accessibility

Only a few years ago, magnetic instruments with performance comparable to modern fluxgate sensors were typically available only to universities, government laboratories and industrial research facilities.

Such instruments often cost **thousands of US dollars**, making them inaccessible to most independent researchers.

Today, RM3100-based modules can be purchased for only **a few tens of US dollars**, with many versions readily available through suppliers such as AliExpress.

This dramatic reduction in cost has made high-quality geomagnetic measurements available to students, hobbyists and independent researchers around the world.

Scientific experiments that once required institutional laboratories can now be performed from a home laboratory or in the field using affordable equipment.

---

# Connection

The original measurements presented in this project were performed using the **TTL version** of the RM3100 module.

For most users, the TTL version is recommended because it is:

- inexpensive,
- easy to connect,
- simple to configure,
- compatible with common USB-to-TTL adapters.

Many manufacturers also offer **RS-485** versions.

RS-485 is primarily intended for industrial applications where multiple devices share the same communication bus or where very long cable lengths are required.

For the vast majority of laboratory and field measurements described in this project, these advantages are unnecessary.

Therefore, the **TTL version is recommended** for anyone wishing to reproduce the experiments presented in this repository.
