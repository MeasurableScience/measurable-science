# Models

Every scientific model begins with an observation.

The **Measurable Science** project began with a simple question.

**How is it possible that civilizations separated by thousands of years and thousands of kilometres repeatedly created remarkably similar geometric structures?**

Among the examples that motivated this research were:

- the **Samarra Bowl**, with its central swastika surrounded by a fourfold rotating geometry;
- the traditional **Serbian cross** surrounded by four "C" symbols;
- the **Tibetan mandala** with four entrances arranged around a central structure;
- the **Irish four-leaf clover** and other Celtic symbols based on fourfold rotational symmetry;
- the **fleur-de-lis** symbols appearing on numerous historical maps;
- the **pre-Christian cross** found throughout many ancient cultures;
- the **Rupes Nigra** model and the recurring geometry of the four central islands shown on historical maps.

Each of these symbols belongs to a different civilization, a different historical period and often a completely different cultural tradition.

Individually, none of them proves anything.

Together, however, they raise an important scientific question:

**Why do the same geometric patterns repeatedly appear throughout human history?**

Could these symbols represent different cultural descriptions of the same natural system?

Rather than treating these symbols as evidence, I treated them as hypotheses capable of generating measurable predictions.

That decision became the starting point of the **Measurable Science** project.

The models presented in this directory are the result of that approach.

They are working hypotheses developed through field observations, measurements and analysis.

Their purpose is not to ask the reader for belief.

Their purpose is to generate testable predictions that can be confirmed, refined or rejected through observation and independent experimentation.

As new measurements become available, every model remains open to refinement, modification or replacement.

Scientific models should evolve together with the evidence.

# Contents

## Carrier

Introduces the concept of the **Carrier**, the fundamental structural element of the proposed planetary model.

According to the model, four primary carriers form the basic resonant framework of the system.

The remaining models are built upon this foundation.

---

## Main Lines (Magistrale)

Describes the proposed **Main Lines (Magistrale)** located between neighbouring carriers.

The initial hypothesis for their locations originated from historical maps and was later investigated through field measurements performed across multiple geographical regions.

Within the proposed model, the Main Lines represent the primary pathways through which information propagates throughout the system.

---

## Emitters

Introduces the concept of **Emitters**.

Within the proposed model, emitters generate localized plasma phenomena within the firmament.

These phenomena are interpreted as what we observe as celestial bodies.

Emitters are proposed to perform specific functions within the system while operating upon the existing carrier structure rather than creating it.

---

## Directors

Describes the proposed **Directors**, structures responsible for organizing and maintaining the operation of the system.

This model remains under active development and continues to evolve as additional measurements become available.

---

## Dreams

Explores the proposed relationship between the measurable model and human consciousness.

This remains one of the most experimental parts of the project and will continue to develop as new observations become available.

---

## Ocean Model

Investigates whether large-scale ocean circulation follows the geometry predicted by the proposed model.

Rather than relying solely on theoretical descriptions, publicly available measurements from oceanographic monitoring stations are analyzed and compared with the predictions of the model.

This section is currently under active development.

---

The purpose of these models is not to replace existing scientific theories.

Their purpose is to provide a framework capable of generating measurable predictions that can be tested through observation, field measurements and independent verification.

Ultimately, the value of every model depends only on one thing:

**How well it agrees with reality.**
