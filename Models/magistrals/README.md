# Main Lines (Magistrale)

The **Main Lines (Magistrale)** are one of the fundamental components of the **Measurable Science** model.

According to the proposed model, the Main Lines are formed naturally between the four primary Carriers.

Without the Carriers, the Main Lines cannot exist.

---

# Origin

The model proposes that the planetary system is organized around four central regions.

Because these regions are not identical in size or geometry, the four Carriers they generate are also not perfectly symmetrical.

As a consequence, the Main Lines formed between neighbouring Carriers are also expected to differ rather than forming four identical pathways.

---

# Why They Matter

Within the proposed model, the Main Lines are much more than geographical boundaries.

They are proposed to represent the primary information pathways of the planetary system.

Using a computer analogy, the Carriers provide the structural framework of the system, while the Main Lines function similarly to a system bus connecting its principal components.

According to the model, most information required for the operation of the planetary system propagates through these Main Lines.

For this reason, locating and measuring them became one of the primary objectives of the **Measurable Science** project.

---

# Initial Hypothesis

The first working hypothesis for the locations of the Main Lines originated from the study of historical maps, particularly the seventeenth-century **Aerdsch Plane** map.

Several large-scale alignments suggested by these maps were later investigated through field expeditions and measurements.

The purpose of these expeditions was not to validate the historical maps themselves, but to determine whether the proposed routes correspond to repeatable measurable phenomena.

As additional measurements become available, the proposed routes continue to be refined.

---

# Field Measurements

The Main Lines are investigated using field measurements collected across different countries and geographical environments.

If the proposed model is correct, measurements performed along the same Main Line should exhibit repeatable characteristics regardless of where they are recorded.

One of the principal objectives of this project is to determine whether these characteristics remain consistent over large geographical distances.

---

# Current Status

The Main Line model remains a working hypothesis.

Its value depends entirely upon whether the proposed routes continue to agree with repeatable field measurements collected in different parts of the world.

Ultimately, the Main Lines will be judged by only one question:

**Can they be identified repeatedly through independent measurements?**
