# Emitters

The **Emitter** is one of the fundamental components of the **Measurable Science** model.

According to the proposed model, Emitters were introduced during a later stage of the system's development as an improvement of the original planetary structure.

Unlike the Carriers and the Main Lines, Emitters do not create the fundamental framework of the system.

Instead, they interact with it to produce localized electromagnetic phenomena.

---

# Function

According to the proposed model, an Emitter transforms mechanical (acoustic) energy into electromagnetic energy.

Before leaving the geological structure, the generated signal couples with the local Carrier.

The resulting emission therefore depends both on the Emitter and on the Carrier present at that location.

The detailed physical mechanism is described in the accompanying book.

---

# Different Emitters

The model proposes that Emitters perform different functions within the planetary system.

For this reason, different Emitters are expected to produce different measurable emissions.

The plasma phenomena observed in the firmament are proposed to depend on the role of the individual Emitter rather than following a single universal pattern.

---

# Field Measurements

Field investigations compare measurements collected at different proposed Emitters in order to identify repeatable emission patterns.

The objective is to determine whether Emitters can be classified according to their measurable characteristics.

---

# Current Status

The Emitter model remains a working hypothesis.

Its value depends entirely upon whether different Emitters produce repeatable and distinguishable measurable emissions under real-world conditions.
