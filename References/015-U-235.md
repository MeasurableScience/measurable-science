# [15] Uranium-235 — Fission, Chain Reaction and Criticality

## Uranium-235

Uranium has atomic number 92.

Uranium-235 contains:

- 92 protons
- 143 neutrons

U-235 is a fissile isotope.

A U-235 nucleus can absorb a neutron and undergo nuclear fission.

During fission, the nucleus splits into smaller nuclei and releases energy
and additional neutrons.

Those neutrons can subsequently cause fission in other U-235 nuclei,
creating a fission chain reaction.

## Chain reaction

A fission chain reaction occurs when neutrons released by one fission
produce additional fissions.

The reaction becomes self-sustaining when enough neutrons from one
generation survive leakage and non-fission absorption to produce the
next generation of fissions.

## Criticality

Criticality is the condition in which a fission chain reaction becomes
self-sustaining.

It therefore does not depend on mass alone.

Important factors include:

- amount of fissile material
- concentration of U-235
- geometry and distribution of the material
- neutron leakage
- neutron absorption by surrounding materials
- neutron energy
- presence and properties of moderators or reflectors

When the effective neutron multiplication factor (k_eff) is:

- below 1: subcritical
- equal to 1: critical
- above 1: supercritical

## Energy and heat

Most of the energy released in U-235 fission initially appears as kinetic
energy of the fission fragments.

These fragments collide with surrounding atoms and transfer their energy
to the surrounding material.

This produces heat.

## Relevance to the book

The established nuclear physics used in the text is:

U-235 + neutron → fission → energy + additional neutrons

Under suitable physical conditions, the additional neutrons can produce
further fissions, allowing the process to become self-sustaining.

The application of this mechanism to the author's proposed primordial
material plane is the author's hypothesis and is not an established
conclusion of nuclear physics.
