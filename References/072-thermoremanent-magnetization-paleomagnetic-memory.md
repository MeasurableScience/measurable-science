# Reference 072 — Thermoremanent Magnetization and Long-Term Paleomagnetic Record

## Purpose

This reference supports the statement in *Merljiva nauka* that cooling volcanic rocks can acquire a remanent magnetic record of the ambient magnetic field and that such magnetic information can remain preserved and measurable over geological timescales.

It provides the scientific basis for the author's functional comparison:

**cooled magnetized volcanic rock → candidate for long-term / non-volatile storage**

The comparison with computer storage is the author's model and is not a claim made by the cited scientific literature.

## Scientific basis

### Thermoremanent magnetization

Ferromagnetic and ferrimagnetic minerals in igneous rocks can acquire thermoremanent magnetization (TRM) while cooling in an external magnetic field.

As magnetic grains cool through their Curie and blocking-temperature ranges, their magnetic moments become progressively stabilized.

Consequently, volcanic rocks can retain information about the geomagnetic field that existed when they cooled.

### Paleomagnetic recording

Measurements of thermoremanent and natural remanent magnetization in volcanic rocks are routinely used in paleomagnetism to reconstruct properties of the ancient geomagnetic field.

Depending on the material and experimental conditions, these records can provide information about:

- magnetic-field direction;
- magnetic polarity;
- paleointensity.

### Long-term stability

Magnetic remanence is not equally stable in every mineral or rock.

Stability depends on magnetic mineralogy, grain size and domain state, thermal history, chemical alteration and later magnetic overprints.

Nevertheless, suitable magnetic particles can preserve primary magnetic information for geological timescales.

Micromagnetic modelling and experimental work show that stable vortex states in magnetite can retain magnetic signals over billion-year timescales.

### Examples from ancient volcanic rocks

Paleomagnetic measurements from Sheep Creek Range lava flows in Nevada identified primary thermoremanent magnetization in volcanic rocks approximately 15.58 million years old.

Paleomagnetic studies of Icelandic lava sequences have independently reconstructed geomagnetic polarity reversals extending to approximately 13 million years before present.

Large compilations of dated lava flows likewise use volcanic rocks to reconstruct geomagnetic-field behaviour across multi-million-year intervals.

## Important limitation

The cited literature does NOT demonstrate that volcanic rock functions as computer memory, a hard disk, or a deliberately addressable digital storage device.

It demonstrates the narrower physical facts required by the model:

1. volcanic material can acquire magnetic information during cooling;
2. that information can remain physically stored after cooling;
3. the stored magnetic state can survive for geological periods under suitable conditions;
4. the previous magnetic state can later be measured and reconstructed.

The interpretation of this property as a functional analogue of non-volatile storage in the natural-computer model is the author's hypothesis.

## Sources

### Clark, H. C. (1974)

**Remanent Magnetism of Volcanic Rocks.**

In *Developments in Solid Earth Geophysics*, Volume 6, pp. 195–214.

DOI: 10.1016/B978-0-444-41141-9.50014-0

Describes thermoremanent magnetization as the principal stable remanent-magnetization mechanism associated with formation and cooling of volcanic rocks.

### Shaar, R. & Tauxe, L. (2015)

**Instability of thermoremanence and the problem of estimating the ancient geomagnetic field strength from non-single-domain recorders.**

*Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences*, 112(36).

PMID: 26305946  
PMCID: PMC4568663

Important both for the use of TRM in reconstructing ancient magnetic fields and for defining limitations caused by magnetic-domain state and long-term alteration.

### Nagy, L. et al. (2017)

**Stability of equidimensional pseudo-single-domain magnetite over billion-year timescales.**

*Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences.*

PMCID: PMC5625920

Demonstrates through micromagnetic modelling that vortex states in magnetite can retain magnetic signals over billion-year timescales.

### Bogue, S. W. (2010)

**Very rapid geomagnetic field change recorded by the partial remagnetization of a lava flow.**

*Geophysical Research Letters.*

DOI: 10.1029/2010GL044286

Reports primary thermoremanent magnetization in Sheep Creek Range volcanic rocks associated with a radiometric age of approximately 15.58 Ma.

### Harrison, C. G. A., McDougall, I. & Watkins, N. D. (1979)

**A geomagnetic field reversal time scale back to 13.0 million years before present.**

*Earth and Planetary Science Letters*, 42(2), 143–152.

DOI: 10.1016/0012-821X(79)90020-7

Uses Icelandic lava sequences and their magnetic polarity to provide an independent geomagnetic reversal chronology extending to approximately 13 Ma.

### Johnson, C. L. et al. (2008)

**Recent investigations of the 0–5 Ma geomagnetic field recorded by lava flows.**

*Geochemistry, Geophysics, Geosystems.*

DOI: 10.1029/2007GC001696

Synthesizes paleomagnetic directional data from more than 2,000 volcanic sites and demonstrates the large-scale use of lava flows as records of geomagnetic-field behaviour over millions of years.
