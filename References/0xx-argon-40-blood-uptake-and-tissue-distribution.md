# Reference XX — Argon-40: Atmospheric Abundance, Blood Uptake and Tissue Distribution

## Purpose

This reference documents the experimentally established facts relevant to the proposed role of inhaled argon in the model:

1. atmospheric argon is overwhelmingly composed of argon-40 (^40Ar);
2. naturally inhaled atmospheric argon is measurable in human blood;
3. inhaled argon can pass through the lungs into the circulation;
4. argon partitions between blood and tissues;
5. no direct measurement specifically demonstrating accumulation or selective retention of natural ^40Ar in the human thyroid gland was identified.

The proposed role of ^40Ar as an inert registration carrier or reference in the model is a hypothesis and is not established by these measurements.

---

## 1. Argon-40 is the dominant isotope of atmospheric argon

The Commission on Isotopic Abundances and Atomic Weights (CIAAW) reports ^40Ar as overwhelmingly dominant in atmospheric argon.

Historically reported atmospheric isotope composition is approximately:

- ^40Ar: 99.6%
- ^36Ar: 0.34%
- ^38Ar: 0.06%

Thus, when atmospheric argon is inhaled, nearly all of the inhaled argon atoms are ^40Ar.

## 2. Natural atmospheric argon has been directly measured in human blood

Mazor et al. developed an analytical method capable of simultaneously determining naturally occurring atmospheric noble gases in human blood.

The investigators directly measured helium, neon, argon, krypton and xenon in human blood.

They separately analyzed blood plasma and red blood cells.

The measured noble-gas concentrations in plasma were broadly consistent with equilibrium solubility expected from atmospheric exposure.

Importantly, red blood cells showed a distinct supersaturation pattern. The authors reported increasing noble-gas excess with atomic mass and interpreted this as evidence of adsorption onto red blood cells.

This provides direct experimental evidence that naturally occurring atmospheric argon is present in human blood.

## 3. Inhaled argon enters the bloodstream

Experimental pharmacokinetic measurements have directly demonstrated the appearance of inhaled argon in blood.

In a controlled study in juvenile pigs, animals inhaled a mixture containing 75% argon and 25% oxygen.

Blood samples were subsequently analyzed for argon concentration using quadrupole mass spectrometry.

Measured maximum blood concentrations ranged approximately from 190 to 872 μM under the experimental exposure conditions.

After argon administration stopped, blood argon declined rapidly, with an estimated half-life of approximately 75 seconds.

These measurements directly demonstrate pulmonary uptake of inhaled argon into circulating blood and subsequent washout.

## 4. Argon is soluble in blood

A quadrupole mass-spectrometry method has been developed specifically to quantify argon solubility in liquids and blood.

Experimental measurements included rabbit blood and demonstrated measurable argon solubility.

This provides a physical mechanism by which inhaled argon can enter and be transported by the circulation without undergoing chemical metabolism.

## 5. Argon partitions into tissues

Inert gases distribute between blood and tissues according to tissue:blood partition coefficients.

Published argon pharmacokinetic models include separate compartments for blood, fat, liver, brain, muscle, richly perfused tissues and poorly perfused tissues.

Reported argon tissue:blood partition coefficients are not zero.

Examples include approximately:

- fat:blood ≈ 4.16
- richly perfused tissue:blood ≈ 1.03–1.07
- poorly perfused tissue:blood ≈ 1.00
- liver:blood ≈ 0.75
- muscle:blood ≈ 0.72
- brain:blood ≈ 0.67

These values describe reversible physical partitioning of argon between circulating blood and body tissues.

They should not be interpreted as evidence of permanent chemical binding.

## 6. Important limitation — thyroid gland

No direct published measurement was identified that specifically quantified naturally inhaled ^40Ar in the human thyroid gland.

Likewise, no evidence was identified demonstrating selective accumulation or long-term retention of ^40Ar by thyroid tissue.

This distinction is important.

The established experimental chain is:

atmospheric argon
→ inhalation
→ dissolution in blood
→ circulation
→ reversible distribution into tissues

A specific step:

^40Ar
→ selective thyroid accumulation or retention

has not been experimentally established in the literature identified for this reference.

Because the thyroid is a highly perfused tissue, some dissolved argon reaching thyroid tissue would be physically expected from ordinary circulation. That expectation is not equivalent to a direct measurement of thyroid argon concentration or evidence of selective retention.

---

## Relevance to the model

The measurements establish that the first inhaled air introduces predominantly ^40Ar, that natural atmospheric argon is measurable in human blood, and that argon can physically partition between blood and tissues.

They do not establish that ^40Ar functions as an identifier, produces a resonant signature, accumulates selectively in the thyroid, or performs registration in a biological or external information system.

Those proposed functions belong to the model and remain experimentally testable hypotheses.

A direct test would require simultaneous measurement of:

1. ^40Ar concentration in blood;
2. ^40Ar concentration in thyroid tissue;
3. retention and washout kinetics;
4. comparison with other highly perfused tissues;
5. any reproducible electromagnetic or resonant change associated with argon uptake.
