# Te Whānau Mārama — The Family of Light

## Purpose

This reference documents the Māori tradition of Te Whānau Mārama — the Family of Light — and the six celestial children of Tangotango and Wainui.

## Te Whānau Mārama

Māori astronomer Dr Rangi Mātāmua describes Tangotango and Wainui as the parents of Te Whānau Mārama, the Family of Light.

Tangotango is associated with the darkness between the stars, while Wainui is associated with water.

Their six children are:

1. **Te Rā** — the Sun
2. **Te Marama** — the Moon
3. **Ngā Whetū** — the stars
4. **Hinātore** — phosphorous / phosphorescent light
5. **Parikoikoi** — gloomy light
6. **Hinerauāmoa** — a small or petite star

Mātāmua gives their order explicitly: the Sun is the oldest, the Moon the second, the stars the third, followed by Hinātore, Parikoikoi and Hinerauāmoa.

Collectively these beings are called **Te Whānau Mārama — the Family of Light**.

## Placement in the sky

In the tradition related by Mātāmua, after Tāne separated Ranginui and Papatūānuku the world remained dark.

Tāne approached Tangotango and Wainui and asked for their children so that the world could be illuminated.

The celestial lights had originally been placed in Hui-te-Rangiora.

Tāne later carried them in a basket and placed them in the sky.

The basket is identified as **Mangaroa**, associated with the Milky Way.

## Important distinction

The Māori source identifies six members of Te Whānau Mārama and preserves their order.

It does **not** identify these six beings with the six noble gases.

The proposed correspondence between:

Te Rā — helium  
Te Marama — neon  
Ngā Whetū — argon  
Hinātore — krypton  
Parikoikoi — xenon  
Hinerauāmoa — radon

is an interpretation proposed in *Measurable Science*.

### 3. University of Waikato

A University of Waikato thesis reproduces the Te Whānau Mārama genealogy as a six-branch diagram:

Tangotango + Wainui  
→ Te Rā  
→ Te Marama  
→ Ngā Whetū  
→ Hinātore  
→ Parikoikoi  
→ Hinerauāmoa

The figure is attributed to a 2016 public lecture by Dr Rangi Mātāmua.
