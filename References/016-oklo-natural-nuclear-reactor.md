# [16] Oklo — Natural Nuclear Fission Reactors

## The Oklo phenomenon

At Oklo in present-day Gabon, naturally occurring uranium deposits
underwent self-sustaining nuclear fission chain reactions approximately
1.8–2.0 billion years ago.

The phenomenon provides direct evidence that a self-sustaining nuclear
fission system can arise naturally without an artificial reactor.

## Conditions for natural criticality

Investigations of Oklo identified several important conditions:

- a high local concentration of uranium,
- a substantially higher natural abundance of U-235 at that time than today,
- sufficiently low concentrations of strong neutron-absorbing materials,
- and the presence of water acting as a neutron moderator.

Because U-235 has a shorter half-life than U-238, its proportion in
natural uranium approximately two billion years ago was much higher than
the approximately 0.72% found in natural uranium today.

IAEA sources describe the U-235 abundance at the time of the Oklo
reactions as approximately 3–4%.

## Role of water

Water played a crucial role in the natural reactor.

Hydrogen in water slows fast neutrons produced in fission.

Slower neutrons increase the probability that U-235 will undergo further
fission, allowing a chain reaction to become self-sustaining under
suitable conditions.

The Oklo system therefore demonstrates a natural sequence:

uranium concentration
→ water moderation
→ self-sustaining fission
→ energy and heat

## Natural regulation

Evidence and reactor modelling indicate that water also provided a
natural feedback mechanism.

As fission generated heat, water in the reaction zone could heat and
vaporize.

Loss of water reduced neutron moderation and therefore reduced or stopped
the chain reaction.

After the system cooled and water returned, moderation could increase
again and the reaction could restart.

The natural reactor could therefore operate cyclically.

## Importance

Oklo demonstrates experimentally, through preserved isotopic evidence,
that naturally occurring geological material can reach conditions for a
self-sustaining nuclear fission chain reaction.

It does not demonstrate that such a reactor existed in the primordial
material plane proposed in this book.

The application of the Oklo mechanism to that proposed environment is
the author's hypothesis.

Oklo is used here only as evidence that the underlying physical process
— natural concentration of uranium, neutron moderation by water and
self-sustaining fission — can occur without human construction.
