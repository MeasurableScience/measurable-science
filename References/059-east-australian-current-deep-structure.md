# Reference 059 — Deep Structure of the East Australian Current and Tasman Front

## Purpose

This reference supports the statements in the book concerning the measured depth of the East Australian Current and Tasman Front and their interaction with deep submarine topography.

Researchers obtained surface-to-bottom Conductivity-Temperature-Depth (CTD) casts along multiple sections through the East Australian Current and Tasman Front during:

eight different research cruises.

The observations demonstrated that the vertical extent of these major oceanographic features was highly variable.

Their measured depth ranged approximately between:

2,500 m

and

4,500 m.

The observations also showed that flow below approximately:

1,500 m

within the Tasman Front could not simply continue eastward.

It encountered major submarine structures, first the Dampier Ridge and then the Lord Howe Rise.

The deep flow was consequently redirected either northward or southward depending on the orientation of the overlying circulation.

## Additional observation

At abyssal depths the investigators also identified near-bottom water bodies approximately:

0.05°C colder

than their surroundings.

These structures were approximately tens of kilometres wide and hundreds of metres deep.

The temperature observation is included here for completeness and should not be interpreted as geothermal heating.

## Relevance to the model

This is direct observational evidence that the East Australian Current/Tasman Front system extends thousands of metres beneath the surface.

It also demonstrates that deep circulation is physically redirected by major submarine geological structures.

The measurements therefore establish a connection between surface-to-deep ocean circulation and seafloor topography.

They do not demonstrate that the geological structures supply the heat carried by the East Australian Current.

## Primary source

Mulhearn, P. J., Hamilton, L. J., & Scott, B. D. (1989).

Deep Structure of the East Australian Current and Tasman Front.

Oceanographic study based on surface-to-bottom CTD observations from eight research cruises.
