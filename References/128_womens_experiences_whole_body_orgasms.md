
# [128] Women's Experiences of Different Types of Orgasms

## Original publication

Weitkamp, K., & Wehrli, F. S. V. (2023).

Women's Experiences of Different Types of Orgasms—A Call for Pleasure Literacy?

International Journal of Sexual Health, 35(2), 193–208.

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1080/19317611.2023.2182861

Full text: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10903554/

PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38595859/

PMID: 38595859
PMCID: PMC10903554

## Research objective

To investigate women's subjective experiences of different
types of orgasms, including experiences not adequately
captured by conventional anatomical classifications.

## Methodology

Mixed-methods online survey combining open-ended
descriptions with quantitative ratings.

Participants:
- Total sample: 513 women.
- Mean age: 25.89 years.
- Age range: 18–56 years.
- Predominantly young, educated, heterosexual women.

A subgroup of 257 women who reported experiencing
both vaginal and clitoral orgasms rated these
experiences separately.

The study used a semantic differential consisting
of 28 bipolar adjective scales.

Qualitative responses were systematically coded.

## Measured results

### Clitoral and vaginal orgasms

Among the 257 women who experienced both types,
statistically significant differences were identified
on 17 of the 28 adjective scales.

Clitoral orgasms were more often rated as sharper,
easier and more controllable.

Vaginal orgasms were more often rated as deeper,
longer, wilder, more pulsating and more extensive.

These results concern subjective ratings, not
direct physiological measurements.

### Combined orgasms

Forty-six women described mixed clitoral and
vaginal orgasms.

Some reported greater intensity, duration and
a stronger sense of fullness compared with
clitoral orgasms alone.

### Whole-body orgasms

Thirty-seven women described whole-body orgasms.

37 / 513 = approximately 7.2% of the study sample.

Descriptions included:
- Deep relaxation and surrender.
- Sensations spreading throughout the body.
- Intense pleasure and increased energy.
- Altered perception of the body and surroundings.
- Experiences described as being outside the body.

Some participants associated these experiences
with prolonged sexual activity.

Others emphasized emotional closeness and
connection with their partners.

Several women expressed uncertainty about
whether their experiences qualified as
whole-body orgasms.

## Interpretation

The study documents considerable diversity in
women's subjective orgasmic experiences.

It also identifies recurring descriptions of
emotional connection, prolonged sexual activity
and surrender in some accounts of whole-body
orgasms.

These observations provide a basis for further
research into partnered sexual interaction.

## Limitations

The study used a nonrepresentative convenience sample.

The 7.2% figure describes this particular sample
and is not a population prevalence estimate.

No brain activity, hormone concentrations,
nerve impulses or muscle contractions were
recorded during the reported orgasms.

The study cannot establish that the different
subjective experiences correspond to distinct
physiological mechanisms.

It cannot determine whether oxytocin, prolactin,
vagus nerve activity or endogenous DMT caused
the reported experiences.

## Relevance to Measurable Science

The participants' independent accounts can be
compared with the subjective experiences
described in the nine-gates hypothesis.

Of particular interest are reports involving
prolonged sexual interaction, emotional
connection with a partner and surrender.

These similarities motivate further investigation;
they do not constitute experimental confirmation
of the nine-gates hypothesis.

## Evidence classification

MEASUREMENT:
Self-reported experiences and quantitative ratings
from 513 participants.

MATHEMATICAL ANALYSIS:
Statistical comparison of subjective ratings
and descriptive counts of reported experiences.

HYPOTHESIS:
Specific patterns of prolonged partnered sexual
interaction may be associated with coordinated
neural, hormonal and subjective changes.

Direct physiological measurements are required
to test this hypothesis.
