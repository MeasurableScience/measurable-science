
# [130] Jinn in the Quran: Smokeless Fire, Heavenly Guards and the Boundaries of Heaven and Earth

## Primary source

The Quran, Surahs Al-Hijr (15), Ar-Rahman (55) and Al-Jinn (72).

## 1. The creation of jinn

**Quran 15:27**

"And the jinn We created before from scorching fire."

**Quran 55:15**

"And He created the jinn from a smokeless flame of fire."

These verses describe jinn as beings created before humanity, from fire.

Sources:
- https://quran.com/15/27
- https://quran.com/55/15

## 2. Jinn possess moral agency

**Quran 72:11–14**

Jinn describe themselves as belonging to different groups. Some are righteous, while others are not. Some submit to God, while others deviate.

The passage presents jinn as morally responsible beings capable of making choices.

Source:
- https://quran.com/72/11-14

## 3. Attempts to reach heaven

**Quran 72:8–9**

Jinn describe their attempts to reach heaven and listen to what occurs there. They encounter powerful guards and flaming projectiles. They report that anyone attempting to listen now encounters a flaming projectile lying in wait.

Source:
- https://quran.com/72/8-9

## 4. The boundaries of heaven and earth

**Quran 55:33**

"O company of jinn and mankind, if you are able to pass beyond the regions of the heavens and the earth, then pass. You will not pass except by authority [from Allah]."

Translation: Sahih International.

The verse addresses jinn and humanity together. It describes the limits of their power and introduces *sultan*, translated as authority or power.

Sources:
- https://quran.com/55/33
- https://corpus.quran.com/translation.jsp?chapter=55&verse=33

## 5. Traditional interpretation

In classical Islamic exegesis, including Ibn Kathir, Quran 55:33 is commonly interpreted as a challenge demonstrating that humans and jinn cannot escape God's authority and judgment.

The verse does not explicitly describe a method of attaining immortality or suggest that jinn originate through sexual activity.

## 6. Author's interpretation

In *Measurable Science*, the author proposes a personal interpretation connecting three Quranic motifs with the experience described in the chapter on the nine gates:

1. **Smokeless fire:** sexual lovemaking.
2. **The attempt to reach heaven and the encounter with guards:** the experience of the "little death", altered bodily awareness and subsequent return.
3. **Crossing the boundaries of heaven and earth:** a possible analogy for enlightenment and the question of immortality, with the ultimate authority belonging to God.

These connections are the author's philosophical hypotheses, not claims made by the Quran or established findings in religious scholarship or physiology.
