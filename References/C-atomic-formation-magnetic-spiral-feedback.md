# Reference C — Formation of the Atom: Phase Relations, Spiral Electric Current, Electromagnetic Feedback and Photon Emission

## 1. Purpose and Scope

Reference [A] develops the mathematical model in which fundamental units carry unmanifested potential and energy becomes manifested through their mutual relations.

The central normalized relational-energy function is:

```text
E_ij = 1 - cos(phi_i - phi_j)
```

Reference [A] also demonstrates that three interacting units can produce nonzero internal rotational contributions while their total rotational action remains balanced.

Reference [B] examines the next proposed level of organization: the neutron, its mass, magnetic moment, spin and precession.

Reference [C] investigates the formation of the atom.

In my model, the neutron is considered a generator of matter, while the atom represents the first complete material form.

The central principle remains:

**POTENTIAL → RELATION → MANIFESTED ENERGY → ATTRACTION / REPULSION / ROTATION → MOTION → ORGANIZATION → TRANSFORMATION.**

At the atomic level, the interacting objects are no longer the initial fundamental units alone.

The model now considers a proton and an electron, particles with measurable electric charges, magnetic moments and electromagnetic interactions.

I propose that changes in their phase relations can influence their mutual organization. In particular, I investigate whether rotational changes of the proton's internal organization could contribute to alternating relational attraction and repulsion, producing radial and angular motion during the formation of an atom.

The proposed sequence is:

**ELECTRIC AND MAGNETIC RELATIONS → PHASE-DEPENDENT ACTION → RADIAL AND ANGULAR MOTION → SPIRAL ORGANIZATION → ELECTRIC CURRENT → ELECTROMAGNETIC FEEDBACK → ORGANIZED ATOMIC FORM → TRANSFORMATION AND POSSIBLE PHOTON EMISSION.**

This reference examines that possibility using established electromagnetic equations, geometrical current models, quantum current density, measured hydrogen properties and energy conservation.

The electromagnetic shell proposed in my model is treated as an organized spatial structure, not as an experimentally established physical membrane surrounding the atom.

The objective is to distinguish what follows mathematically from the proposed geometry, what is already measured, and what remains a physical hypothesis.

### 2.2 Center of mass

Let the proton and electron positions be:

```text
r_p

r_e
```

The center of mass is:

```text
R = (m_p*r_p + m_e*r_e)/(m_p + m_e)
```

The relative position is:

```text
r = r_e - r_p
```

The reduced mass is:

```text
mu_red = (m_p*m_e)/(m_p + m_e)
```

The individual coordinates relative to the center of mass are:

```text
r_e - R = (m_p/(m_p + m_e))*r

r_p - R = -(m_e/(m_p + m_e))*r
```

These equations establish an important geometrical point.

Proton and electron motion can be described through a common center of mass and a relative coordinate.

Because their masses differ greatly, their movements relative to the center of mass need not have identical spatial amplitudes.

In my model, spiral organization refers primarily to changing relative motion and the organization of electromagnetic relations.

It does not require two identical spirals.

## 3. Relational Energy and Phase-Dependent Action

### 3.1 The geometrical function from Reference A

The initial model describes a relation through its relative phase:

```text
phi_ij = phi_i - phi_j
```

The angular projection is:

```text
R_ij = cos(phi_i - phi_j)
```

The normalized relational-energy function is:

```text
E_ij = 1 - cos(phi_i - phi_j)
```

This function assigns different normalized relational-energy values to different phase configurations.

For example:

```text
E_ij(0 degrees) = 0

E_ij(90 degrees) = 1

E_ij(180 degrees) = 2
```

The potential of the fundamental units is not changed by these phase differences.

**THE RELATION CHANGES. THE MANIFESTATION OF ENERGY CHANGES WITH IT.**

For a physically calibrated interaction, an energy scale can be introduced:

```text
U_ij = K_ij * [1 - cos(phi_i - phi_j)]
```

Here, `K_ij` represents an interaction-energy scale.

The corresponding generalized rotational action is:

```text
T_i = -partial(U_ij)/partial(phi_i)
```

For a single relative phase:

```text
T_phi = -K_ij * sin(phi_ij)
```

This mathematical structure connects the angular configuration to a rotational response.

The function was introduced for the proposed fundamental relational model. Its application to the proton–electron system is a further hypothesis, not an experimentally established atomic interaction law.

### 3.2 Proton rotation and changing phase relations

In my model, I propose that the proton's internal rotational organization changes its phase relation with the electron.

Let:

```text
phi_p(t) = proton phase

phi_e(t) = electron phase
```

Their relative phase is:

```text
delta_phi(t) = phi_p(t) - phi_e(t)
```

The corresponding proposed normalized relational energy is:

```text
E_relation(t) = 1 - cos(delta_phi(t))
```

The phase changes with time according to:

```text
d(delta_phi)/dt =
d(phi_p)/dt - d(phi_e)/dt
```

Therefore, if the relative angular motion changes, the energy associated with the proposed phase relation can also change.

The normalized angular action is:

```text
T_relation = -sin(delta_phi)
```

This allows a changing rotational response as the relative phase evolves.

In my interpretation, different phase configurations may also correspond to different characters of relational action.

The proposed responses are:

**ATTRACTION — REPULSION — ROTATION.**

I investigate the possibility that the changing phase of the proton contributes to alternating radial attraction and repulsion of the electron.

In the proposed sequence:

**ATTRACTION → APPROACH.**

**REPULSION → SEPARATION.**

The simultaneous angular motion provides the possibility of a spiral.

An important physical distinction remains necessary.

In established electromagnetism, the electrostatic force between a proton and an electron remains attractive. Proton spin or rotation does not reverse the sign of their electric charges.

The alternating radial response proposed here would therefore have to arise from an additional phase-dependent interaction within the model.

The angular energy function by itself determines a generalized rotational response. It does not yet determine a radial force.

To calculate the proposed approach and separation quantitatively, the model must also specify how relational energy depends on spatial separation.

This is a defined physical task, not an additional assumption that its result is already known.

## 4. Magnetic Interaction Between Proton and Electron

### 4.1 Magnetic dipole interaction

The proton and electron possess measured magnetic moments.

In the classical point-dipole approximation, the magnetic interaction energy is:

```text
U_mag = (mu_0/(4*pi*r^3)) *
        [dot(mu_p,mu_e)
         - 3*dot(mu_p,r_hat)*dot(mu_e,r_hat)]
```

Where:

```text
mu_p   = proton magnetic-moment vector

mu_e   = electron magnetic-moment vector

r      = separation

r_hat  = unit vector along separation

mu_0   = vacuum permeability
```

This interaction depends on both separation and orientation.

For fixed orientations:

```text
F_r = -d(U_mag)/dr
```

Since the point-dipole energy has a factor proportional to:

```text
1/r^3
```

the radial component satisfies:

```text
F_r = 3*U_mag/r
```

within this fixed-orientation approximation.

The sign depends on the orientation of the two magnetic moments.

Thus, the magnetic contribution can be attractive or repulsive depending on geometry.

This is a known example of an orientation-dependent interaction.

### 4.2 Connection with the proposed phase function

Reference [A] uses:

```text
U_ij = K_ij * [1 - cos(phi_ij)]
```

For a fixed positive `K_ij`, this differs from:

```text
U_old = -K_ij*cos(phi_ij)
```

only by the constant `K_ij`.

Both give the same angular derivative:

```text
T_phi = -K_ij*sin(phi_ij)
```

The new expression places the reference energy at zero for aligned phases.

The established magnetic interaction also depends on relative orientation, although its complete geometrical structure is more complex than a single cosine.

Therefore, magnetic interactions provide a physical example of angularly dependent energy.

They do not establish that the proposed relational phase is identical to an ordinary magnetic-dipole angle.

### 4.3 Relative strength of magnetic and electric forces

At the characteristic hydrogen distance:

```text
r = a_0
```

the classical magnetic dipole energy scale is:

```text
U_mag_scale =
(mu_0/(4*pi))*abs(mu_p*mu_e)/a_0^3
```

Approximately:

```text
U_mag_scale = 8.84*10^(-26) J

U_mag_scale = 5.52*10^(-7) eV
```

The corresponding force scale is:

```text
F_mag_scale = 3*U_mag_scale/a_0
```

Approximately:

```text
F_mag_scale = 5.01*10^(-15) N
```

The electrostatic force magnitude is:

```text
F_C = e^2/(4*pi*epsilon_0*a_0^2)
```

Approximately:

```text
F_C = 8.24*10^(-8) N
```

Therefore:

```text
F_mag_scale/F_C = 6.08*10^(-8)
```

At the characteristic hydrogen distance, the simple magnetic dipole force scale is approximately sixteen million times smaller than the electrostatic force.

This does not mean magnetic properties are absent or irrelevant.

They influence measurable atomic energy levels.

However, the comparison shows that a decisive role for magnetic phase organization in atomic formation cannot be assumed merely from the existence of magnetic moments.

It must follow from the proposed dynamics.

A complete quantum calculation must also include the relevant spin interactions, including the contact contribution to hydrogen hyperfine structure.

## 6. Electromagnetic Induction and Electric Current

### 6.1 Faraday's law

A changing magnetic field can induce an electric field.

Maxwell–Faraday induction is described by:

```text
curl(E) = -dB/dt
```

Its integral form is:

```text
closed_integral(E.dl) = -d(Phi_B)/dt
```

Here:

```text
Phi_B = magnetic flux through a surface
```

The magnetic flux depends on the magnetic field and the surface through which it is evaluated.

For a spatially uniform magnetic field passing through a planar surface:

```text
Phi_B = B*S*cos(theta)
```

Where:

```text
S     = surface area

theta = angle between field and surface normal
```

The surface used in the flux calculation must be specified by the physical configuration or by the chosen contour.

In the proposed atomic model, the organized form has a spatial extent. Any flux calculation intended to describe that form must therefore relate the field and integration surface to the actual proposed spatial organization.

It is not sufficient to choose an arbitrary area and identify its result with a measured atomic property.

### 6.2 Current and moving charge

The proton and electron already carry electrical charges:

```text
q_p = +e

q_e = -e
```

Their movement contributes to electric current.

The current does not require electromagnetic induction in order to exist.

Induction can modify the electric field and consequently influence the motion of existing charges.

The electromagnetic sequence is therefore more accurately represented as:

**MOVING CHARGE → ELECTRIC CURRENT → MAGNETIC FIELD.**

And:

**CHANGING MAGNETIC FIELD → INDUCED ELECTRIC FIELD → CHANGE IN CHARGE MOTION.**

These processes can operate together in a coupled electromagnetic system.

### 6.3 Lorentz force

The classical electromagnetic force on a charged particle is:

```text
F = q*(E + cross(v,B))
```

Where:

```text
q = electric charge

E = electric field

v = particle velocity

B = magnetic field
```

The electric field influences acceleration.

The magnetic contribution depends on particle motion relative to the field.

In my model, I investigate whether the interaction between moving charges and their electromagnetic fields can participate in the formation of an organized spiral pattern.

## 7. Mathematical Construction of a Spiral Electric Current

### 7.1 Current density

In the classical description, electric current density is:

```text
J = rho*v
```

Where:

```text
rho = charge density

v   = velocity field
```

In cylindrical coordinates, current density can be decomposed into:

```text
J = J_r*r_hat + J_phi*phi_hat + J_z*z_hat
```

The components describe:

```text
J_r   radial current

J_phi azimuthal current

J_z   axial current
```

A current field can possess both radial and angular components.

This makes spiral organization mathematically possible.

The radial component describes current toward or away from the center.

The azimuthal component describes circulation around the center.

Both are necessary for a noncircular planar spiral.

### 7.2 Spiral geometry

For a planar current field with nonzero azimuthal component, the streamline equation is:

```text
dr/dphi = r*(J_r/J_phi)
```

Suppose that, within the region considered:

```text
J_r/J_phi = k
```

where `k` is constant.

Then:

```text
dr/dphi = k*r
```

Separating variables:

```text
dr/r = k*dphi
```

Integrating:

```text
ln(r/r_0) = k*(phi - phi_0)
```

Therefore:

```text
r(phi) = r_0*exp[k*(phi - phi_0)]
```

This is the equation of a logarithmic spiral.

The parameter `k` determines its character:

```text
k > 0   outward spiral

k < 0   inward spiral

k = 0   circular streamline
```

This is a mathematical result.

It establishes that a current field with the appropriate radial-to-azimuthal relationship can have spiral streamlines.

It does not establish that every electron follows a sharply defined classical spiral trajectory.

Nor does it prove that the required current distribution exists during hydrogen formation.

### 7.3 Alternating radial motion

My model introduces the additional possibility that the radial interaction changes with the relative phase of proton and electron.

During the proposed transformation, an attractive relational response would correspond to a tendency toward approach.

A repulsive relational response would correspond to a tendency toward separation.

If these changes occur while angular movement continues, the motion need not have a constant radius.

Its radial component may change sign during the evolution.

In mathematical terms, the radial motion may satisfy:

```text
dr/dt < 0   approach

dr/dt > 0   separation

dr/dt = 0   instantaneous radial turning point
```

At the same time:

```text
dphi/dt != 0
```

The combination allows angular motion with alternating radial behavior.

However, such a trajectory is not necessarily a logarithmic spiral.

The logarithmic form derived above applies when the specified ratio of radial to azimuthal current is constant.

A changing ratio can produce more complicated spiral-like or oscillatory paths.

The model must determine that ratio dynamically rather than assigning a spiral shape in advance.

### 7.4 Conservation of electric charge

Any acceptable current description must satisfy the continuity equation:

```text
d(rho)/dt + div(J) = 0
```

This expresses local conservation of electric charge.

A transient process may contain radial current while the charge distribution changes.

A stationary bound state cannot sustain an uncompensated net outward or inward charge flux through every enclosing surface.

The current associated with the formation of a state therefore need not be identical to the current structure of the completed state.

**THE MOTION THROUGH WHICH A FORM EMERGES NEED NOT BE IDENTICAL TO THE ORGANIZATION THAT REMAINS AFTER TRANSFORMATION.**

This distinction is central to the proposed model.

## 8. Quantum Currents During Atomic Transformation

### 8.1 Stationary hydrogen ground state

In the nonrelativistic approximation, the spatial wavefunction of the hydrogen ground state is:

```text
psi_1s(r) =
exp(-r/a)/(sqrt(pi)*a^(3/2))
```

Here, `a` includes the appropriate reduced-mass correction.

The orbital probability-current density is:

```text
j = (hbar/mu_red)*Im[conj(psi)*grad(psi)]
```

For the real stationary ground-state spatial wavefunction:

```text
j_orbital = 0
```

This means that the ordinary orbital probability current vanishes in this representation.

It does not imply the absence of the electron's intrinsic magnetic moment.

Spin-related magnetization currents and magnetic interactions require separate treatment.

The stationary hydrogen ground state is not described by an electron following a fixed classical circular orbit.

The quantum state instead has a spatial probability distribution.

That distribution has radial dependence, but radial probability distribution is not itself evidence that the electron repeatedly travels inward and outward along a definite trajectory.

The proposed phase-driven radial motion must therefore be investigated as a distinct physical hypothesis.

### 8.2 Transient currents from quantum superposition

Time-dependent quantum states can contain currents that are absent from their individual stationary components.

Consider a coherent superposition:

```text
Psi(r,t) =
  a*psi_1s(r)*exp(-i*E_1*t/hbar)
+ b*psi_2p(r)*exp(-i*E_2*t/hbar)
```

Where:

```text
a, b = complex amplitudes
```

For a simplified example, take real spatial orbitals and omit spin and vector-potential contributions.

The probability current is:

```text
j = (hbar/mu_red)*Im[conj(Psi)*grad(Psi)]
```

The interference contribution can be written as:

```text
j = (hbar/mu_red)
  * Im[conj(a)*b*exp(-i*(E_2-E_1)*t/hbar)]
  * [psi_1s*grad(psi_2p)
     - psi_2p*grad(psi_1s)]
```

Depending on the relative phase and the spatial structure of the states, this current can be nonzero.

Since the two orbitals have different radial and angular dependence, their interference can create local current components absent from the separate stationary states.

**MATHEMATICAL RESULT: TIME-DEPENDENT QUANTUM STATES CAN PRODUCE TRANSIENT CURRENT THROUGH THE INTERFERENCE OF THEIR COMPONENTS.**

This demonstrates the possibility of time-dependent current organization.

It does not by itself establish the spiral geometry proposed in my model or reproduce the complete physical process of radiative proton–electron binding.

### 8.3 Relation to the phase model

The central geometrical relation introduced in Reference [A] is:

```text
E_ij = 1 - cos(phi_i - phi_j)
```

Quantum interference also depends on physically relevant relative phases.

The existence of phase dependence in both descriptions provides a useful mathematical comparison.

However, the relational phase of my proposed fundamental model is not automatically identical to the phase of a quantum wavefunction.

A quantitative connection would have to specify how one maps into the other.

In my model, I investigate the following sequence:

**CHANGE OF INTERNAL PHASE RELATION → CHANGE OF INTERACTION → CHANGE OF CURRENT DISTRIBUTION → ORGANIZATION OF FORM.**

The mathematical objective is to determine whether the required radial and azimuthal current components follow from the proposed relational dynamics.

## 10. Electromagnetic Feedback and Organized Form

### 10.1 Maxwell–Ampère equation

Electric current and changing electric fields contribute to magnetic-field circulation.

Maxwell–Ampère's equation is:

```text
curl(B) =
mu_0*J + mu_0*epsilon_0*dE/dt
```

Faraday's law is:

```text
curl(E) = -dB/dt
```

Together, these equations describe coupled electromagnetic fields.

A changing current modifies the magnetic field.

A changing magnetic field can induce an electric field.

The resulting fields influence the movement of charged matter.

**MOTION CHANGES THE FIELD. THE FIELD ACTS ON MOTION.**

This provides the established electromagnetic basis for investigating feedback during atomic organization.

### 10.2 Electromagnetic field energy

The classical electromagnetic energy density is:

```text
u_EM = 0.5*epsilon_0*E^2
     + B^2/(2*mu_0)
```

The electromagnetic energy flux is represented by the Poynting vector:

```text
S = (1/mu_0)*cross(E,B)
```

Poynting's theorem states:

```text
d(u_EM)/dt + div(S) = -dot(J,E)
```

This equation relates changes in electromagnetic-field energy, energy transported by the field, and energy exchanged with charged matter.

It provides a quantitative framework for examining how electromagnetic organization changes during the formation of a bound state.

## 11. Photon Emission and the Formation of Hydrogen

### 11.1 Radiative recombination

A proton and electron can form a hydrogen atom through radiative recombination:

```text
p + e^- -> H + gamma
```

Here:

```text
H     = hydrogen atom

gamma = emitted photon
```

The photon carries away energy and momentum.

This permits a transition from an unbound proton–electron system into a bound atomic state while preserving the relevant conservation laws.

The process is experimentally established.

### 11.2 Energy conservation

Let:

```text
K_i = initial relative kinetic energy

E_bind = binding energy of final state

E_recoil = recoil kinetic energy of atom
```

In the initial center-of-mass frame, neglecting small corrections, the photon energy satisfies:

```text
E_gamma = K_i + E_bind - E_recoil
```

For the ground state of hydrogen:

```text
E_bind = approximately 13.5984 eV
```

When the initial relative kinetic energy is small, the emitted photon energy is close to the binding energy, with a recoil correction.

Its exact energy depends on the initial and final states.

### 11.3 Photon emission as a manifestation of transformation

In my model, photon emission is considered one possible measurable manifestation of the transformation into a new organized atomic state.

The proposed sequence is:

**CHANGING RELATIONS → ENERGY REDISTRIBUTION → ORGANIZED ATOMIC STATE + EMITTED PHOTON.**

In the language of my model:

**THE FLASH MARKS THE TRANSFORMATION. THE SHELL REPRESENTS THE FORM THAT EMERGES.**

This does not imply that every transformation must produce a visible flash or emit a photon.

Nor does the observation of photon emission establish the proposed shell.

The established physical result is the radiative transition and its energy balance.

The proposed interpretation concerns the additional organization through which the atomic form arises.

## 12. Transformation and the Recurrence of Fundamental Constituents

The organizational sequence proposed in my model is:

**NEUTRINO → ORGANIZED RELATIONS → NEUTRON → TRANSFORMATION → ATOM.**

The same general principle is proposed to apply when an existing form changes or disintegrates.

**WHAT PARTICIPATES IN THE FORMATION OF A STRUCTURE MAY REAPPEAR THROUGH ITS TRANSFORMATION OR DISINTEGRATION.**

Known nuclear reactions provide examples through which this proposal can be discussed.

### 12.1 Electron capture

In energetically allowed nuclear electron capture, a proton in a nucleus captures an electron and transforms into a neutron while an electron neutrino is emitted.

The local weak-interaction process is:

```text
p + e^- -> n + nu_e
```

In an actual nucleus, this corresponds to a transformation of the parent nucleus into a daughter nucleus.

The process is only possible under the appropriate nuclear and energetic conditions.

It is not a spontaneous reaction available to an isolated proton and electron in ordinary hydrogen.

Source: [11 — Electron Capture](https://github.com/MeasurableScience/measurable-science/blob/main/References/011-electron-capture.md)

### 12.2 Free-neutron beta decay

A free neutron can undergo beta decay:

```text
n -> p + e^- + anti_nu_e
```

The products are:

```text
Proton

Electron

Electron antineutrino
```

This is an experimentally established weak-interaction process.

The appearance of neutrinos and antineutrinos in such transformations is one of the observations that motivates my investigation of neutrino relations and matter organization.

Within my model, I examine whether the transformation of an organized form can be understood as a change in the relationships through which its fundamental potential is manifested.

The established weak-interaction description does not demonstrate that the neutron was originally assembled from neutrinos.

Those are distinct hypotheses.

**FORMATION AND DISINTEGRATION ARE DIFFERENT POSSIBLE OUTCOMES OF CHANGING ORGANIZATION.**

The broader principle is proposed to apply at different levels of nature, even though the specific physical mechanisms may differ.
