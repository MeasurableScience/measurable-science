# Reference 010 — Ancient Atomism: India and Greece

## Purpose

Historical reference for ancient atomistic ideas discussed in
*Merljiva Nauka*.

## Classical Indian atomism

Atomistic theories appeared in classical Indian philosophy several
centuries before the Common Era.

The Nyāya and Vaiśeṣika traditions developed atomistic descriptions
of the material world.

The terms **aṇu** and **paramāṇu** were used for minimal units or
smallest parts.

Nyāya–Vaiśeṣika philosophy held that perceptible composite objects
are formed from smaller imperceptible units.

A specific order of combination was proposed:

- two atoms combine into a dyad (*dyaṇuka*);
- three dyads combine into a triad (*tryaṇuka*);
- further combinations produce larger perceptible structures.

Other Indian traditions, including Buddhist and Jaina schools,
developed different forms of atomism.

## Ancient Greek atomism

Leucippus and Democritus developed an atomistic description of nature
based on atoms and void.

Atoms were regarded as indivisible units existing in infinite number
and moving through the void.

Visible objects were explained as temporary organizations or clusters
of these atoms.

Particularly relevant to the discussion in *Merljiva Nauka* is the
cosmological account associated with early Greek atomism:

large groups of moving atoms could become organized through a
**whirling or vortex-like motion**, producing clusters and eventually
ordered worlds (*kosmoi*).

These worlds were not permanent; their structures could later
disintegrate.

## Comparison

Ancient Indian and Greek atomism should not be identified with the
modern scientific atom.

Their concepts of *aṇu*, *paramāṇu* and *atomos* belonged to different
philosophical systems and carried meanings different from the modern
atomic model.

The historical comparison in *Merljiva Nauka* concerns the broader
pattern:

**basic units → combination and organization → higher composite form**

and, particularly in early Greek atomism:

**many units → motion → vortex → organized structure**
