

Petar II Petrović Njegoš (1813–1851)

*Luča mikrokozma*  
(*The Ray of the Microcosm*)

First published in Belgrade in 1845.

Njegoš was a Montenegrin ruler, bishop, poet and philosopher.
*Luča mikrokozma* is his philosophical and religious cosmological poem,
dealing with creation, the structure of the cosmos, God, light, darkness,
the origin and destiny of humanity, and the relationship between the
material and spiritual worlds.

The poem consists of a dedication and six cantos.

## The central mountain and the throne

Of particular relevance to the discussion in the book is Njegoš's
description of the divine throne and the geometry surrounding it.

Njegoš places a great mountain in the middle of an immense plain:

"На средини непрегледне равни
крута се је гора узвисила,
основ јој је од чиста рубина,
а сва гора маса брилијантна;"

The mountain is therefore described as rising in the centre of an
immense plain, with its foundation made of pure ruby and its mass
described as brilliant or diamond-like.

Njegoš then places the throne and palace of the eternal ruler at the
summit of this mountain:

"Наврх горе трон се горди виси
и палата превјечнога цара;"

This is important because the central mountain is not merely part of
the landscape in the poem. It is explicitly associated with the throne
of God.


## Four surrounding mountains

Njegoš then describes a second geometrical feature surrounding the
central throne-bearing mountain:

"Око горе престолодржеће
четири су горе од алмаза,
превисоке, у правилном реду;"

Thus, four extremely high mountains of diamond are positioned around
the central mountain in a regular arrangement.


## Four fountains

From these four surrounding mountains emerge four fountains:

"из њих бију четири фонтана
са жалошћу пламтећијех лучах;"

Njegoš describes their columns rising upward through the heavenly
sphere.

Their vertical extent is expressed through an extraordinary comparison:

"Кад стубове у висину попну
колико је с земље до мјесеца"

— "when the columns rise to a height as great as from the Earth
to the Moon."


## The arcs

After reaching this height, the columns bend into regular arcs:

"онда ти се у правилне дуге
свију сфером неба блаженога,
на четири небесне границе
у воздушни океан падају."

The four streams therefore rise as columns, curve into regular arcs,
and extend toward four boundaries of the heavenly sphere before
falling into the "aerial ocean."


## Structure described by Njegoš

Without imposing a modern physical interpretation on the poem,
the spatial structure explicitly described by Njegoš can be summarized
as follows:

- an immense plain;
- a great central mountain;
- a foundation described as pure ruby;
- a brilliant central mountain mass;
- the throne of God located at its summit;
- four extremely high mountains surrounding it;
- the four mountains arranged in a regular order;
- four fountains emerging from those mountains;
- enormous vertical columns associated with the fountains;
- a height compared with the distance from Earth to the Moon;
- the columns bending into regular arcs;
- four directions or boundaries of the heavenly sphere;
- an "aerial ocean" into which the streams descend.

These are descriptions present in the literary source itself.

Any comparison between this geometry and a physical model developed
elsewhere in the book is the author's interpretation and should not
be attributed to Njegoš.


## Relevance to the book

This reference is used to document the original literary description,
not to establish that Njegoš was describing a physical geological or
geophysical structure.

The comparison made in the book concerns the recurring geometry:

central structure → four surrounding structures → four streams →
large vertical structures/arcs.

The significance of that similarity is examined as a hypothesis by
the author.

It is not presented here as an established interpretation of
*Luča mikrokozma*.
