# Reference 052 — Vitória–Trindade Ridge: Volcanic Origin and Deep Ocean Circulation

## Purpose

This reference supports the statements in the book concerning the geological nature of the Vitória–Trindade Ridge and the deep-water circulation surrounding it.

The Vitória–Trindade Ridge (VTR) is a major submarine ridge extending approximately 1,200 km eastward from the Brazilian continental margin into the South Atlantic.

It consists of a series of large seamounts and volcanic structures, some rising several kilometres above the surrounding ocean floor.

Geological studies associate the formation of the ridge with submarine volcanic activity.

Oceanographic observations across the ridge also show that several major water masses interact with this topography at different depths.

These include:

- Antarctic Intermediate Water (AAIW),
- North Atlantic Deep Water (NADW),
- Antarctic Bottom Water (AABW).

The ridge therefore represents not merely an elevated feature of the ocean floor, but a major topographic structure interacting with circulation through a large portion of the water column.

## Relevance to the model

The book uses the Vitória–Trindade Ridge as a geological and oceanographic comparison point while following the Brazil Current.

The relevant observation is that a major warm western boundary current encounters a large submarine volcanic/seamount system, while deeper water masses also interact with the same structure.

This does not by itself demonstrate a geothermal origin for the Brazil Current.

It establishes the geological setting and the presence of deep circulation around the same submarine ridge.
