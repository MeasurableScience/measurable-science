# Reference 061 — Qaidam Basin Salt Lakes and Deep Fluids

Li, X. et al. (2022).

Boron and lithium isotopic constraints on their origin, evolution, and enrichment processes in a river–groundwater–salt lake system in the Qaidam Basin, northeastern Tibetan Plateau.

Ore Geology Reviews,
149,
105110.

DOI:
10.1016/j.oregeorev.2022.105110

RESULT:

The Qaidam Basin contains a major river–groundwater–salt lake mineral system enriched in boron and lithium.

Isotopic measurements indicate that deep fluids provide an important source of boron and lithium.

The deep fluids rise through tectonic pathways and discharge into river and groundwater systems through geothermal springs developed along:

- deep faults
- volcanic craters

The river and groundwater systems then transport these materials into terminal salt lakes.

Evaporation subsequently concentrates the dissolved material and produces brines and major salt-lake deposits.

USED IN THE BOOK:

Large saline continental basins occur farther north in the investigated Asian region.

Their chemistry can include measurable contributions from deep fluids transported upward through faults and geothermal systems.
