# Reference 006 — Neutrino Oscillations and Mass

## Source

The Nobel Prize in Physics 2015 — Takaaki Kajita and Arthur B. McDonald

## Relevance

Neutrinos are electrically neutral elementary particles belonging to the lepton family.

For a long time, neutrinos were considered to be massless.

Experiments at Super-Kamiokande and the Sudbury Neutrino Observatory demonstrated that neutrinos can change identity, or "flavour", while travelling.

This phenomenon is known as **neutrino oscillation**.

The discovery of neutrino oscillations showed that neutrinos cannot all be massless. The 2015 Nobel Prize in Physics was awarded for this discovery.

The oscillation experiments establish differences between neutrino mass states, but do not by themselves provide a direct measurement of the absolute neutrino mass.

There are three known neutrino flavours:

- electron neutrino
- muon neutrino
- tau neutrino

The fact that neutrinos were historically regarded as massless, are electrically neutral, interact only very weakly with matter, and can oscillate between flavours is the scientific basis for their discussion in this section of *Merljiva nauka*.

The further interpretation of the neutrino as a possible "particle of Nothing" belongs to the model proposed in the book and is not a claim of established particle physics.
