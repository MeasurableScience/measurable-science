# Reference 092 — Endogenous DMT in the Brain From Birth

## Source

Beaton, J. M., & Morris, P. E. (1984).

**Ontogeny of N,N-dimethyltryptamine and related indolealkylamine levels in neonatal rats.**

*Mechanisms of Ageing and Development*, 25(3), 343–347.

DOI: 10.1016/0047-6374(84)90007-1

PMID: 6588281

## Method

Brain concentrations of N,N-dimethyltryptamine (DMT) and related indolealkylamines were measured in rats at different ages.

The compounds were identified using gas chromatography-mass spectrometry with isotope dilution.

## Key Findings

DMT was directly detected in neonatal rat brain from birth.

DMT concentrations remained relatively low during the earliest neonatal period but increased significantly on approximately days 12 and 17 before returning to lower levels at later ages.

Related endogenous indolealkylamines were also detected and showed age-dependent changes.

## Relevance

The experiment demonstrates that endogenous DMT is already present in the mammalian brain at birth and that its concentration changes during postnatal development.

The study does not establish that the first breath causes DMT release and did not measure DMT immediately before and after the first breath.

It therefore establishes the presence of the DMT system at birth, while leaving the immediate relationship between birth, first respiration and DMT concentration experimentally unresolved.
