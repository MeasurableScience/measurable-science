Human Origins, Shared Life, and Religious Traditions --- Source Reference

Purpose

This reference supports the book section "Transformacija --- Šta smo
mi?". It separates the modern scientific account of human biological
origins from religious and traditional accounts used for comparison. The
sources support the statements attributed to science or to each
tradition; they do not establish the book's further hypothesis that
humans are mobile subsystems of a natural information-processing system.

1. Modern science --- human evolutionary origin

Modern biology classifies humans as primates and places Homo sapiens
within the great-ape evolutionary tree. Humans did not evolve from any
primate species living today. Fossil and genetic evidence indicates that
humans and chimpanzees/bonobos descend from a common ancestor;
Smithsonian places the divergence at approximately 8--6 million years
ago. Evolutionary biology also places humans within the wider history of
life and common ancestry.

Sources

Smithsonian Institution, Human Origins Program --- "Introduction
to Human Evolution."
https://humanorigins.si.edu/education/introduction-human-evolution

Smithsonian Institution, Human Origins Program --- "Genetics."
https://humanorigins.si.edu/evidence/genetics

Smithsonian Institution, Human Origins Program --- "Human
Evolution Evidence."
https://humanorigins.si.edu/evidence

Supported manuscript claim

Modern biology does not say that humans descended from today's monkeys
or apes. It places Homo sapiens within the primate evolutionary tree
and concludes that humans and other living primates descend from
common ancestors.

The sources establish biological common ancestry. They do not establish
a Creator, a soul, or the special systems role proposed in the book.

2. Christianity / Biblical tradition --- humanity in the image of God

Genesis explicitly presents humans as created in God's "image" and
"likeness."

Primary source

Genesis 1:26--27 (NRSVUE).
https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Gen+1%3A26-27&version=NRSVUE

Supported manuscript claim

Christianity says that the human being is created in the image and
likeness of God.

The passage does not define "image" as biological similarity, computer
architecture, information processing, or physical structure. Such
comparisons belong to the author's model.

3. Islam / Qur'an --- earthly material and spirit

The Qur'an describes the human being as fashioned from clay/altered mud
and then speaks of God breathing into the fashioned human "of My spirit"
(min rūḥī).

Primary sources

Qur'an 15:28--29, Sūrat al-Ḥijr.
https://quran.com/al-hijr/28-29

Sahih International rendering, Qur'an 15:28--29.
https://legacy.quran.com/15/28-29

Supported manuscript claim

Islam uses different language: the human being is fashioned from
earthly material, after which God breathes into him of His spirit.

This should not be expanded into a claim that Islam teaches that a human
is literally a "part of God." The safe textual sequence is:

earthly material → fashioned human → spirit breathed by God

4. Hindu / Upanishadic tradition --- Self, beings, and underlying unity

The Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad, especially the "Madhu" or "Honey" section
(2.5), relates the elements, living beings, the human species, the
individual Self, and Brahman. It repeatedly presents reciprocal
relations between beings and the world and identifies an underlying
unity with Brahman.

Primary/classical source

Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad 2.5.1--14 --- Madhu Brāhmaṇa.
https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/the-brihadaranyaka-upanishad/d/doc117952.html

Especially relevant are 2.5.1, 2.5.13 and 2.5.14.

Supported manuscript claim

The Upanishadic tradition describes a deep underlying unity connecting
the Self, living beings, the elements, and Brahman.

The formula "one → many, but behind the many remains one" is an
interpretive summary, not a direct quotation.

5. Māori tradition --- whakapapa and an interconnected world

In Māori thought, whakapapa is more than an ordinary human family
tree. Te Ara describes it as a framework linking animate and inanimate,
known and unknown phenomena in terrestrial and spiritual worlds. Māori
genealogies can include humans, atua, plants and animals. Te Ara also
records traditions in which Tāne shapes Hineahuone from earth/soil and
humans descend from their union.

Sources

Rāwiri Taonui --- "Whakapapa -- genealogy: What is whakapapa?" Te
Ara.
https://teara.govt.nz/en/whakapapa-genealogy/page-1

John Andrews --- "Māori genealogy." Te Ara.
https://teara.govt.nz/en/whakapapa/10934/maori-genealogy

Basil Keane --- "Traditional Māori religion -- ngā karakia a te
Māori: Ngā atua -- the gods." Te Ara.
https://teara.govt.nz/en/traditional-maori-religion-nga-karakia-a-te-maori/page-1

Te Ahukaramū Charles Royal --- "Papatūānuku -- the earth mother."
Te Ara.
https://teara.govt.nz/en/papatuanuku-the-land/page-2

Supported manuscript claims

Māori whakapapa links humans, atua, plants, animals and the wider
natural world.

In the Hineahuone tradition, Tāne forms the first woman from earth,
and humans descend from Tāne and Hineahuone.

Māori creation traditions vary by iwi and source, so this should not be
presented as the single universal Māori creation account.

6. Haudenosaunee --- responsibility within the living world

The Haudenosaunee Thanksgiving Address explicitly says that people have
a duty to live "in balance and harmony" with one another and all living
things. It then gives thanks to Earth, waters, fish, plants and other
parts of the natural world.

Source

Haudenosaunee Thanksgiving Address: Greetings to the Natural
World. National Museum of the American Indian, Smithsonian
Institution.
https://americanindian.si.edu/environment/pdf/01_02_Thanksgiving_Address.pdf

Supported manuscript claim

Haudenosaunee tradition places human beings inside a wider living
order and assigns them a responsibility to live in balance and harmony
with one another and all living things.

This supports a statement about relationship and responsibility; it does
not establish that Haudenosaunee cosmology is identical to the other
traditions or to the author's model.

Comparative result used in the book

The cited traditions are not doctrinally identical. The sources justify
the narrower comparison below:

evolutionary tree of life and share

organisms

and likeness of God

Qur'an / Islam                      Humanity is fashioned from earthly
material and receives spirit

Upanishadic Hindu tradition         Self, beings, elements and Brahman
are presented through an underlying
unity and reciprocal relations

the natural world are connected

The author can then make an explicitly identified comparative
interpretation:

Different traditions use different conceptual languages, but several
independently describe the human being through relations to a greater
source, to the natural world, or to a wider living whole.

The stronger proposition---

natural intelligence → natural information system → human as a mobile
node of the same network

---is the hypothesis developed in Measurable Science. It is not a
claim established by the cited scientific or religious sources.
