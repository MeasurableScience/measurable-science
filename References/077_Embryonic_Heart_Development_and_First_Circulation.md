# Reference 077 — Embryonic Heart Development and the First Circulation

## Purpose

This reference documents the early development of the human heart and
cardiovascular system used in the chapter “Creation of Man”.

The established embryological observations relevant to the model are:

- the cardiogenic region develops during the third week after fertilization;
- primitive cardiac structures appear during approximately days 18–21;
- paired endocardial structures fuse into a primitive heart tube;
- rhythmic cardiac activity begins very early in development;
- blood circulation begins while the heart is still undergoing morphogenesis;
- the primitive heart tube elongates, bends and undergoes cardiac looping;
- blood flow produces mechanical forces that participate in cardiovascular
  development.

---

## 1. Formation of the Primitive Heart

During the third week of human embryonic development, mesodermal cells form
the cardiogenic region.

Blood islands and primitive cardiac structures become identifiable during this
period.

By approximately day 21, embryonic folding brings the developing endocardial
tubes together, producing the primitive heart tube.

## 2. Beginning of Cardiac Activity and Circulation

The embryonic heart becomes functionally active extremely early in human
development.

Traditional embryological descriptions commonly place the beginning of
rhythmic cardiac activity at approximately days 21–23 after fertilization.

A modern review of the available evidence notes that the exact instant of the
first human heartbeat cannot be assigned as precisely as traditional textbook
statements sometimes suggest.

Direct modern observations nevertheless confirm cardiac pumping activity
during the fourth post-fertilization week.

## 3. Heart-Tube Looping

The early heart does not begin as a four-chambered organ.

It develops initially as a primitive tube.

As this tube elongates, it bends and undergoes a major three-dimensional
reorganization known as cardiac looping.

Heart-tube formation and looping occur approximately during days 20–28 of
human embryonic development.

This process transforms the early tubular structure and establishes the
spatial organization required for subsequent chamber formation.

## 4. Flow During Construction

A particularly important feature of cardiac development is that the heart
begins functioning while its anatomical structure is still being constructed.

Blood flow therefore exists during cardiac morphogenesis.

The developing cardiovascular tissues are exposed to physical forces
including:

- wall shear stress;
- pressure;
- stretching;
- mechanical deformation.

These forces interact with biological development while the cardiovascular
system continues to change its form.

## Observable Sequence

The established developmental sequence can therefore be summarized as:

**CARDIOGENIC REGION  
→ PRIMITIVE CARDIAC STRUCTURES  
→ HEART TUBE  
→ RHYTHMIC CONTRACTION  
→ BLOOD FLOW  
→ HEART-TUBE LOOPING  
→ CHAMBER DEVELOPMENT**

The cardiovascular system therefore begins transporting material before the
mature anatomical structure of the heart has been completed.
