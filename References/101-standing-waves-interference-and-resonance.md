# Standing Waves, Interference and Resonance

## Purpose of this reference

This reference documents the standard physical basis for standing-wave formation.

It is used in *Measurable Science* to support the established physical statement that waves propagating in opposite directions can interfere and, under appropriate conditions, form a stationary wave pattern with nodes and antinodes.

The application of this principle to underground water flows and the proposed "acoustic column" in the book is the author's model and is not established by the sources below.

---

## 1. Standing waves

A standing wave is a wave pattern that varies in amplitude but does not propagate through space in the manner of an ordinary travelling wave.

OpenStax explains that standing waves can be produced by the superposition of travelling waves.

For two identical waves moving in opposite directions, their disturbances add as the waves pass through one another. With the appropriate matching of amplitude and wavelength, constructive and destructive interference produces a stationary pattern.

The resulting standing wave contains:

- **nodes** — positions of zero displacement;
- **antinodes** — positions at which the oscillation reaches maximum amplitude.

For two identical counter-propagating sinusoidal waves,

\[
y_1(x,t)=A\sin(kx-\omega t)
\]

and

\[
y_2(x,t)=A\sin(kx+\omega t),
\]

their superposition gives

\[
y(x,t)=2A\sin(kx)\cos(\omega t).
\]

The spatial term and temporal term are separated. The nodes therefore remain at fixed spatial positions while the regions between them oscillate.

---

## 2. Resonance and normal modes

Standing-wave patterns depend on the physical properties and boundary conditions of the system.

Only particular frequencies and wavelengths may produce stable normal modes for a given geometry.

OpenStax describes the lowest such frequency as the fundamental frequency, with higher permitted frequencies forming higher normal modes or overtones.

This means that a physical structure can select particular oscillatory states according to its geometry, dimensions, material properties and boundary conditions.

---

## Relevance to the model

The established physics used in the book is limited to the following chain:

**counter-propagating waves → interference → standing-wave pattern → nodes and antinodes**

The book then proposes a further hypothesis:

**opposing underground water-flow systems → mechanical excitation of the surrounding material → standing mechanical/acoustic structure**

The proposed vertical structure called the **acoustic column** is therefore a model inference to be investigated experimentally, not a conclusion of the cited physics literature.
