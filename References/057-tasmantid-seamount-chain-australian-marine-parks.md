# Reference 057 — Tasmantid Seamount Chain: Australian Marine Geological Setting

## Purpose

This reference provides institutional geological and bathymetric context for the Tasmantid Seamount Chain and the surrounding Temperate East marine region.

The region east of Australia contains major seamount, ridge and deep-ocean structures interacting with the circulation of the Tasman Sea.

The Tasmantid Seamount Chain consists of large submarine volcanic mountains rising several kilometres above the surrounding deep ocean floor.

Some of these structures approach comparatively shallow depths despite originating from the deep Tasman and Coral Sea floor.

The same regional oceanographic system contains the East Australian Current, its eddies and the Tasman Front.

## Relevance to the model

The reference provides an independent Australian institutional source for the physical scale and geological character of the submarine topography discussed in the book.

It supports the geographical comparison between:

- the East Australian Current system,
- the Tasman Sea,
- the Tasmantid Seamount Chain,
- and surrounding deep-ocean topography.

It does not establish that the volcanic structures provide the heat transported by the East Australian Current.
