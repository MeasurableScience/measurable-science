## Relevance

In physics, potential and potential energy are related but distinct concepts.

For example, electric potential is defined as electric potential energy per unit charge:

**V = U / q**

This means that potential is not itself the energy possessed by a particular charge. The energy associated with a charge depends on both the potential and the charge:

**U = qV**

A similar distinction exists in gravitational physics, where gravitational potential can be defined independently of the particular mass placed in the field.

This distinction is useful when describing a physical field: the potential characterizes the field or position, while the potential energy depends on the object interacting with that field.
