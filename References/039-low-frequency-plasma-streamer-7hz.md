# Reference 039 — Low-frequency plasma streamer propagation at 7 Hz

## Purpose

This reference documents an experimental result relevant to the discussion
in the book concerning the formation and propagation of plasma at very low
pulse repetition frequencies.

The purpose is not to reproduce the hypothetical large-scale system proposed
in the book.

The purpose is to establish experimentally whether a plasma streamer can
form and propagate through a rarefied gas when the high-voltage excitation
is repeated at a frequency as low as approximately 7 Hz.

---

## Experimental study

Sosnin and co-authors investigated a form of plasma discharge known as an:

**apokampic discharge**

An apokamp is a plasma structure that develops from a region associated
with a high-voltage discharge and propagates through the surrounding gas.

The authors investigated its formation and propagation under different
electrical and gas conditions.

Source:

Sosnin, E. A., et al. (2019).

"Apokampic Discharge: Formation Conditions and Mechanisms."

Russian Physics Journal, 62, 1289–1297.

DOI:

https://doi.org/10.1007/s11182-019-01846-1

---

## Experiment at 7 Hz

Of particular relevance to the model developed in the book is an experiment
performed at a pulse repetition frequency of:

# f = 7 Hz

with an applied pulse voltage of approximately:

# U = 33 kV

and gas pressure:

# p = 110 Torr

The experimental conditions reported for the relevant high-speed imaging
sequence were:

p = 110 Torr

f = 7 Hz

C = 30 pF

Up = 33 kV

The evolution of the plasma structure was recorded with high-speed imaging
using nanosecond-scale temporal resolution.

The experiment therefore directly demonstrates that lowering the pulse
repetition frequency to only several pulses per second does not prevent
the formation and propagation of a plasma streamer in rarefied gas.

---

## Streamer propagation

The high-speed observations showed that the streamer moved away from its
formation region while its spatial dimensions increased.

The reported initial propagation velocity was approximately:

445 km/s

and later decreased to approximately:

335 km/s.

Under another experimental configuration the initial velocity reached
approximately:

560 km/s.

These measurements demonstrate that the plasma structure was not merely
a stationary glow around the electrode.

It propagated through the surrounding rarefied gas.

---

## Effect of frequency

The authors investigated the influence of pulse repetition frequency on
the behavior of the plasma structure.

Their experiments showed that the characteristics of the apokamp depend
on the operating frequency together with the applied voltage and other
discharge parameters.

In the relevant experimental sequence the repetition frequency was reduced
from the kilohertz range down to:

7 Hz

while the voltage was increased sufficiently to maintain the discharge.

The resulting streamer remained capable of propagating through the gas.

---

## Important distinction

The value:

7 Hz

is the **pulse repetition frequency**.

It means that a short high-voltage excitation was applied approximately
seven times per second.

It does not mean that the microscopic development of an individual
streamer occurred over one seventh of a second.

Individual streamer development occurs on much shorter time scales.

Therefore this experiment establishes:

**low repetition frequency does not prevent streamer formation and
propagation**

but it does not establish that the plasma itself oscillates internally
at 7 Hz.

---

## Relevance to the working model

The hypothetical system developed in the book independently reaches a
stage in which:

- a conductive central structure exists;
- a very large electric current is available;
- a megavolt-scale potential difference is estimated;
- the summit reaches rarefied atmospheric conditions;
- ionization and plasma formation become physically possible.

The question then becomes whether a low-frequency electrical system could
still generate a propagating plasma structure.

The Sosnin et al. experiment provides an experimental answer to the limited
question:

# Yes.

A propagating plasma streamer was directly observed in rarefied gas with
high-voltage excitation repeated at:

# 7 Hz.

This experiment does not determine the dimensions or final geometry of
the hypothetical structure proposed in the book.

It establishes only that very low pulse repetition frequency, by itself,
does not prevent the formation and spatial propagation of a plasma streamer.

---

## Conclusion

The experimentally demonstrated combination:

**rarefied gas + high voltage + 7 Hz pulse repetition**

produced a propagating plasma streamer.

This provides an experimental basis for continuing the investigation of
the hypothetical low-frequency plasma structure discussed in the book,
without assuming that low-frequency excitation would automatically prevent
plasma propagation.
