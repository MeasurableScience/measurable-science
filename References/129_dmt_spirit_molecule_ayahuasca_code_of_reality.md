
# [129] DMT: The Spirit Molecule, Ayahuasca and the Code of Reality

## Purpose

This reference brings together three lines of research
relevant to the final stage of the nine-gates hypothesis:

1. Rick Strassman's clinical research into the effects
   of N,N-dimethyltryptamine (DMT).
2. The pharmacological mechanism of ayahuasca.
3. Danny Goler's reported "Code of Reality" phenomenon.

The central question is whether the altered states
described in these sources share any physiological
mechanism with the author's experiences, those of
his partners, and the accounts of whole-body orgasms
documented in reference [128].

### Experimental method

Strassman conducted clinical DMT research
at the University of New Mexico.

His research programme involved approximately
60 volunteers.

One controlled dose-response experiment involved
11 experienced hallucinogen users.

Participants received intravenous DMT or saline
placebo in a randomized, double-blind design.

### Measurements

The researchers recorded dose-dependent
neuroendocrine, autonomic, cardiovascular
and subjective responses.

DMT produced intense, short-lived alterations
of perception and consciousness.

The broader research programme documented
unusual subjective experiences, including
reports interpreted by participants as
mystical or otherworldly encounters.

### The pineal gland hypothesis

In his 2001 book, Strassman explored the
possibility that endogenous DMT and the pineal
gland might contribute to extraordinary
states of consciousness.

This was a speculative interpretation,
not a finding demonstrated by his clinical trials.

The trials administered DMT intravenously.
They did not measure DMT release during
sexual activity or orgasm.

Full text:

### Pharmacological mechanism

Traditional ayahuasca preparations commonly
combine two plants:

Psychotria viridis:
A source of N,N-dimethyltryptamine.

Banisteriopsis caapi:
A vine containing harmala alkaloids,
including harmine and harmaline.

DMT taken orally is ordinarily broken down
rapidly by monoamine oxidase A (MAO-A),
particularly during its passage through
the intestinal wall and liver.

Harmala alkaloids reversibly inhibit MAO-A.

This reduces the rapid degradation of
externally administered DMT, allowing
a greater amount to reach the bloodstream
and brain.

DMT's psychedelic effects are primarily
associated with serotonin 5-HT2A
receptor activation.

### Relevance

Ayahuasca demonstrates a pharmacological
method of producing a DMT-induced altered
state of consciousness using externally
supplied compounds.

The nine-gates hypothesis proposes a
different possibility: an altered state
produced through the body's own processes
during prolonged partnered sexual activity.

The rapid degradation of oral DMT does not,
by itself, establish that endogenous DMT
causes the latter experience.

That possibility requires direct measurement.

## 3. Danny Goler — The Code of Reality

Goler, D. (2025).

Detailing a Pilot Study: The "Code of Reality"
Protocol, A Phenomenon of N,N-DMT
Induced States of Consciousness.

IPI Letters, 3(1), N1–N5.

DOI:
https://doi.org/10.59973/ipil.158

Publication:
https://www.ipipublishing.org/index.php/ipil/article/view/158

### Research question

Do different individuals under the influence
of DMT report similar structured visual
phenomena when observing a particular
diffracted laser-light pattern?

### Experimental approach

Goler developed a protocol using a
650 nm red laser and a diffraction grating.

He reported seeing organized geometric
structures and symbols resembling
digital code.

The pilot publication describes similar
reports from other observers.

Goler states that observations were collected
from more than 1,000 participants.

### Reported results

Participants reportedly described recurring
geometric structures and code-like symbols.

The publication presents the protocol
and the reported consistency of
these subjective observations.

### Evidence limitations

The article was published in the
News and Views section of IPI Letters.

It was not a conventionally peer-reviewed,
blinded clinical experiment.

The publication does not provide a public
participant-level dataset or a statistical
analysis demonstrating exact agreement
between independently recorded symbols.

Consequently, the reported similarity
between participants is an observation
requiring independent replication.

It does not establish that the symbols
are objectively present in the laser light
or that they reveal a digital structure
underlying physical reality.

### Relevance

Goler's experiment attempts to investigate
whether unusual subjective visual experiences
can be compared across different observers.

This is relevant to the author's interest
in the similarities between independently
reported extraordinary experiences.

## 4. Connection with the nine-gates hypothesis

Four groups of descriptions motivate
the author's investigation:

1. The final stages of the traditional
   nine-gates instructions.
2. The author's personal experiences.
3. The experiences reported by his partners.
4. Women's independent accounts of
   whole-body orgasms in reference [128].

These descriptions include altered
body perception, unusual visual experiences,
loss of ordinary spatial orientation
and intense subjective states.

Some features resemble experiences
reported following externally
administered DMT.

The author proposes that the final
nine-gates stage may activate an
endogenous physiological mechanism,
potentially involving DMT.

This is a hypothesis based on
phenomenological similarities.

The available studies do not establish
that endogenous DMT is released during
orgasm, that the pineal gland initiates
such a release, or that DMT is responsible
for the reported whole-body experiences.

## Evidence classification

### MEASUREMENT

Strassman's controlled experiments:
Physiological and subjective responses
to intravenously administered DMT.

Ayahuasca pharmacology:
The interaction between externally
supplied DMT and MAO-A inhibitors.

Goler's pilot:
Reported descriptions of visual
phenomena from multiple observers.

### MATHEMATICAL ANALYSIS

Strassman's dose-response analysis
and controlled comparisons.

Pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic
analysis of ayahuasca constituents.

Goler's published pilot does not
provide a statistical demonstration
of exact symbol correspondence
across observers.

### HYPOTHESIS

The final stage of the nine-gates
process may produce an endogenous
altered state resembling some
subjective effects of DMT.

Endogenous DMT is one candidate
mechanism proposed for investigation.

Testing this hypothesis requires
direct biochemical measurements
synchronized with physiological
recordings and subjective reports.

## Conclusion

Controlled clinical studies demonstrate
the physiological and subjective
effects of externally administered DMT.

Ayahuasca research explains how
MAO-A inhibition makes oral DMT active.

Goler's pilot documents reports of
similar code-like visual phenomena
among multiple observers, without
establishing objective symbol identity.

These sources provide the scientific
and observational background for
investigating the author's hypothesis
about the final stage of the nine gates.
