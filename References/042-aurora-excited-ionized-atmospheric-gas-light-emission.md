# Reference 042 — Aurora and light emission from excited atmospheric gas

## Purpose

This reference documents the physical mechanism by which gases in the
upper atmosphere can emit visible light when energy is transferred to
their atoms and molecules.

This provides a modern measured example demonstrating that luminous
structures can exist high in the atmosphere through excitation and
ionization processes.

---

## Aurora

Aurora borealis and aurora australis are luminous phenomena occurring
in Earth's upper atmosphere.

NOAA describes aurora as the result of energetic electrons interacting
with atoms and molecules in the upper atmosphere.

The accelerated electrons travel along Earth's magnetic field and collide
primarily with atmospheric oxygen and nitrogen.

During these collisions energy is transferred to the atoms and molecules,
placing them into excited states.

When they subsequently return toward lower-energy states, that energy is
released in the form of photons:

# light.

NOAA compares the basic emission mechanism to that occurring in neon
lighting.

---

## Altitude

According to NOAA, auroral emissions typically occur at altitudes of
approximately:

# 80–500 km

above Earth's surface.

NASA likewise explains that energetic particles interacting with gases
in the atmosphere transfer energy to atoms and molecules, which subsequently
release that energy as visible light.

Different atmospheric species and excitation conditions produce different
auroral colors.

---

## Physical significance

The relevant experimentally established sequence is therefore:

energetic charged particles
→ interaction with atmospheric gas
→ excitation / ionization processes
→ photon emission
→ visible atmospheric light.

This demonstrates an important physical principle:

# gas high in an atmosphere can become a luminous structure when supplied
# with sufficient energy.

The existence of visible atmospheric light therefore does not require
the luminous gas itself to be a conventional thermal light source.
