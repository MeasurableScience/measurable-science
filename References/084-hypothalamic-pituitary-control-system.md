## Evidence

The hypothalamus is a major integration center for endocrine, autonomic and
behavioral regulation and is a key regulator of pituitary function and
homeostasis.

The hypothalamus communicates with the pituitary through specialized neuronal
and vascular pathways.

Hypothalamic releasing and inhibitory hormones regulate secretion from the
anterior pituitary.

The posterior pituitary represents a direct anatomical extension of the central
nervous system and contains axons and nerve terminals originating from
hypothalamic neurons.

The anterior pituitary contains specialized endocrine cell populations that
secrete hormones including:

- growth hormone (GH)
- prolactin
- adrenocorticotropic hormone (ACTH)
- thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH)
- luteinizing hormone (LH)
- follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH)

Through these pathways, signals originating in the brain can regulate endocrine
functions throughout the body.

## Relevance to the text

This provides the biological foundation for the simplified architecture used
in the book:

hypothalamus -> controller
pituitary -> endocrine output

The hypothalamus integrates information concerning the internal state of the
organism and regulates pituitary activity.

The pituitary then converts this regulation into hormonal signals capable of
changing physiological activity throughout the body.

The computer terminology "controller" and "output" is the author's engineering
analogy.
