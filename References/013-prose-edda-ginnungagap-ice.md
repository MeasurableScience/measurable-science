# [13] Snorri Sturluson — The Prose Edda: Ginnungagap, Niflheim and the Primordial Ice

## Primary source

Snorri Sturluson. *The Prose Edda*, Gylfaginning.

English translation by Arthur Gilchrist Brodeur, 1916.

## Relevant passage

In the Norse creation account preserved in the *Prose Edda*, the world as we know it does not yet exist.

Ginnungagap is described as the primordial yawning void. Toward the north lies Niflheim, associated with cold, ice and rime.

The streams flowing from this region eventually freeze. The text describes the accumulation of frozen material as:

> “frost over frost, each over the other”

until the northern part of Ginnungagap becomes filled with heavy masses of ice and rime.

The southern part is associated with Muspelheim, from which sparks and glowing material later enter Ginnungagap.

## Relevance to the book

The source is used because the Norse cosmological sequence places extensive ice and frost before the formation of the ordered material world.

The comparison made in the book is not presented as proof that the Norse account describes a physical cosmological model. It is used as an ancient parallel to the sequence developed in the author's model:

potential → transformation → matter → organization → plane → ice

The later appearance of heat and fire from Muspelheim is deliberately treated separately, because the author's model has not yet derived a permanent source of heat at this stage.
