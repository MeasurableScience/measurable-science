## Relevance to the text

Human thymus development begins approximately during the sixth week of gestation.

The thymic primordia arise principally from the third pharyngeal pouches and subsequently migrate toward the mediastinum.

The thymus becomes a primary lymphoid organ responsible for the development and selection of T lymphocytes.

An especially important function of thymic development is the establishment of self-tolerance: developing T cells are selected so that the immune system can distinguish appropriate self structures from potentially harmful foreign targets.

The thymus therefore plays a central role in establishing the body's biological distinction between self and non-self.
