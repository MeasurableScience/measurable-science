# Reference 054 — Brazil Current and Intermediate Western Boundary Current Flow Through the Vitória–Trindade Ridge

## Purpose

This reference supports the quantitative statements in the book concerning the passage of the Brazil Current and the Intermediate Western Boundary Current through the Vitória–Trindade Ridge.

Direct oceanographic observations show that the complex topography of the ridge divides and redirects the currents through passages between its seamounts.

For the Brazil Current, measured velocity cores in the ridge region reached approximately:

0.34 m/s.

Farther south, after the current reorganized, measured velocities reached approximately:

0.74 m/s.

The associated transport was approximately:

3.7 ± 0.5 Sv.

Beneath the southward-flowing Brazil Current, the Intermediate Western Boundary Current (IWBC) flows predominantly northward.

In a passage between Besnard Bank and Vitória Seamount, measurements indicated transport of approximately:

7.3 ± 1.8 Sv,

with velocities reaching approximately:

0.41 m/s.

## Relevance to the model

These observations demonstrate that the Vitória–Trindade Ridge is crossed by substantial flows at different depths.

They also show an important vertical circulation structure:

- the warm Brazil Current flows generally southward,
- an intermediate western boundary current flows northward beneath it,
- both interact with passages in the same submarine ridge system.

The measurements therefore provide direct evidence that substantial oceanic transport occurs through and around the volcanic/seamount topography rather than being restricted to the surface.

These measurements do not establish that the Vitória–Trindade Ridge heats the Brazil Current.
