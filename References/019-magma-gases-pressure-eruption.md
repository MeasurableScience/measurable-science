# Reference 019 — Magmatic gases, pressure and explosive eruption

## Source

U.S. Geological Survey, Volcano Hazards Program.
*Volcanic gases can be harmful to health, vegetation and infrastructure.*

U.S. Geological Survey, Hawaiian Volcano Observatory.
*Volcano Watch — Now you're monitoring with gas!*

## Relevant findings

Magma contains dissolved gases.

At high pressure, gases can remain dissolved in magma. As pressure
decreases, gases exsolve and form bubbles.

The expansion of these gases can drive magma upward and plays a
fundamental role in volcanic eruptions.

USGS descriptions of explosive volcanism also explain that eruption
can occur when the surrounding volcanic structure can no longer
contain internal magmatic pressure, allowing gases to expand
explosively.

## Relevance to the book

These observations support the physical sequence:

magma + dissolved gases → changing pressure conditions →
gas exsolution and expansion → increasing eruptive driving force.

They do not establish the size, geometry or origin of the author's
proposed primordial subsurface system.
