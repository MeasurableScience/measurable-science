# [114] Moon, Liquids and Circalunar Biology

## Purpose

This reference documents two groups of evidence used in the chapter discussing the possible function of the Moon:

1. historical associations of the Moon with water, moisture, bodily fluids, reproduction and growth;
2. experimentally observed circalunar and circasemilunar biological rhythms, particularly those associated with reproduction.

These two categories must not be confused.

Historical texts document what earlier authors and traditions associated with the Moon.

Modern biological experiments establish that lunar-period environmental signals can synchronize biological processes in some organisms.

None of these observations by itself establishes the physical mechanism proposed in my model.

The purpose of this reference is therefore limited to documenting the repeated historical association between the Moon, liquids and reproduction, and the experimentally measured existence of lunar-period biological rhythms.


## 1. Historical association: Moon and moisture

### Plutarch

Source:

Plutarch, *De facie quae in orbe lunae apparet*  
(*On the Face Which Appears in the Orb of the Moon*), Section 25.

Public text:

https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus%3Atext%3A2008.01.0357%3Asection%3D25

Plutarch explicitly describes the Moon as having a moistening or humidifying character.

Among the phenomena associated with it he lists:

- growth of plants;
- decay of meat;
- changes in wine;
- softening of timber;
- childbirth;
- dew and atmospheric moisture.

He also discusses the idea that air is liquefied by the Moon and becomes dew.

Of particular importance for this reference is that the text does not restrict the lunar association to one particular form of water.

The same conceptual connection extends to atmospheric moisture, plants, biological material and reproduction.

This is a HISTORICAL SOURCE.

It records an ancient physical interpretation and must not be treated as a modern experimental demonstration of lunar causation.


## 2. Historical association: water and bodily fluids

### Heinrich Cornelius Agrippa

Source:

Heinrich Cornelius Agrippa, *Three Books of Occult Philosophy*, Book I, Chapter XXIV:

"What things are Lunary, or under the power of the Moon."

Public text:

https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Three_Books_of_Occult_Philosophy/Book_1/Chapter_24

Agrippa explicitly places among "Lunary" things:

- water;
- moist things;
- moisture of trees;
- moisture of animals;
- fat;
- sweat;
- phlegm;
- bodily fluids and secretions.

This is another HISTORICAL SOURCE.

It is useful here because it shows that the historical lunar association was not limited to astronomical timekeeping.

The Moon was explicitly connected with water, moisture and fluids in living organisms.


## 3. Maori and Polynesian traditions: Moon and reproduction

### Elsdon Best

Source:

Elsdon Best, *The Maori Division of Time, Including References to Polynesian Systems*, Dominion Museum Monograph No. 4, Wellington, 1922.

Public text:

https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Maori_Division_of_Time

Best records an explicit traditional association between the Moon and reproduction in Polynesia and New Zealand.

He writes:

> "In Asiatic beliefs of old the moon is closely connected with reproduction, as in Polynesia and New Zealand."

The monograph also documents Maori use of particular lunar nights in activities including planting and fishing.

For example, Best records specific lunar phases considered favourable for planting the sweet potato, while other phases were considered unfavourable.

This evidence should be classified as CULTURAL / HISTORICAL EVIDENCE.

It establishes that lunar phase, reproduction and biological production were connected within these traditional systems.

It does not establish the physical mechanism responsible for such associations.


## 4. Circalunar biological rhythms

Modern chronobiology documents biological rhythms associated with lunar and semilunar periods in numerous marine organisms.

Examples include rhythms in:

- reproduction;
- spawning;
- maturation;
- locomotor behaviour;
- larval release;
- population-level synchronization.

Review:

Kronfeld-Schor et al. (2013),  
*Chronobiology by moonlight*

https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3712431/

The review discusses lunar-period biological timing in several groups of organisms.

These include corals and marine invertebrates in which reproductive or behavioural events occur in relation to lunar or semilunar cycles.

The important point is that these are not merely historical observations.

Controlled experiments have demonstrated biological timing systems that can be synchronized by lunar-associated environmental signals.


## 5. Experimental example: Platynereis dumerilii

One particularly useful experimental system is the marine annelid:

    Platynereis dumerilii

Zantke et al. studied both its circadian and circalunar timing systems.

Source:

Zantke et al. (2013)

*Circadian and Circalunar Clock Interactions in a Marine Annelid*

https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3913041/

The experiment is important because the lunar signal was manipulated under laboratory conditions.

The animals were maintained under a daily light-dark cycle.

For eight consecutive nights during the artificial lunar month they were exposed to dim nocturnal illumination simulating the full-Moon period.

The timing of reproductive maturation synchronized with this artificial monthly light regime.

The experiment therefore separated the laboratory lunar-light schedule from the phase of the natural Moon.

The researchers then performed a free-running experiment.

After more than two months of entrainment, the monthly simulated full-Moon light stimulus was removed while the ordinary daily light-dark cycle was retained.

The animals continued to show reproductive periodicity with a period of approximately:

    30 days

The authors concluded that the reproductive rhythm is controlled by an endogenous circalunar clock.

This is EXPERIMENTAL EVIDENCE.

It demonstrates that a lunar-period environmental signal can entrain a biological timing mechanism and that the resulting rhythm can subsequently persist without the monthly light stimulus.


## 6. Moonlight and precise spawning time

Later experiments on the same organism identified mechanisms capable of distinguishing lunar illumination from sunlight.

Source:

Zurl et al. (2022)

*Two light sensors decode moonlight versus sunlight to adjust a plastic circadian/circalunidian clock to moon phase*

https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9295771/

The experiments show that lunar light information participates in controlling the precise timing of mass spawning.

The system involves two light-sensitive mechanisms that allow the animal to distinguish information associated with moonlight from ordinary daylight.

This provides a measurable biological pathway through which lunar illumination can participate in reproductive synchronization.


## 7. Endogenous lunar rhythms beyond a single species

Lunar biological rhythms are not restricted to Platynereis.

A review by E. Naylor describes experimentally demonstrated:

- circatidal rhythms;
- circadian rhythms;
- circasemilunar rhythms;
- circalunar rhythms.

Reference:

E. Naylor (1985)

*Tidally rhythmic behaviour of marine animals*

PubMed PMID 3914727

https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3914727/

The review reports that free-running endogenous rhythms with these periodicities had been demonstrated in a number of marine species under controlled laboratory conditions.

This distinction is important.

A biological event occurring during a particular Moon phase does not by itself demonstrate an internal lunar clock.

Persistence of the rhythm after removal of the periodic environmental signal provides substantially stronger evidence for an endogenous oscillator.


## 8. What the evidence establishes

### Historical evidence

Multiple historical authors and traditions associated the Moon with:

    water
    moisture
    dew
    plant fluids
    bodily fluids
    reproduction
    birth
    growth

These observations are historical evidence, not modern proof of mechanism.


### Biological measurement

Circalunar and circasemilunar biological rhythms have been documented in multiple organisms.

Controlled experiments demonstrate that lunar-period environmental signals can synchronize reproductive timing.

In Platynereis dumerilii, an approximately monthly reproductive rhythm continued after removal of the artificial monthly moonlight stimulus following prior entrainment.


## 9. What the evidence does NOT establish

The evidence in this reference does not demonstrate that:

- all organisms possess a lunar clock;
- all biological processes respond to lunar periodicity;
- human physiology is controlled by lunar phase;
- historical lunar beliefs establish a physical mechanism;
- the Moon acts directly upon bodily water;
- a physical spiral travels from the Moon to Earth;
- neon produces these biological effects.

Those questions belong to separate parts of the investigation.


## 10. Relevance to my model

Reference [114] establishes a deliberately narrow connection:

    historical descriptions -> liquids / reproduction

    modern experiments -> lunar-period biological synchronization

The physical mechanism is not determined by this reference.

In my model, these observations are compared with the independently developed hypothesis that the lunar transformation produces an organized structure whose principal functional medium on Earth is liquid.

The neon identification is treated separately in reference [113].
