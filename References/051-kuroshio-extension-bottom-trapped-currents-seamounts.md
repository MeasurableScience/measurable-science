# Reference 051 — Bottom-Trapped Currents beneath the Kuroshio Extension

## Source

Bishop, S. P., Watts, D. R., Park, J.-H., & Hogg, N. G. (2012).

**Evidence of Bottom-Trapped Currents in the Kuroshio Extension Region.**

*Journal of Physical Oceanography*, 42(2), 321–328.

DOI: 10.1175/JPO-D-11-0144.1

Original publication:
https://doi.org/10.1175/JPO-D-11-0144.1

---

## Experiment

The measurements formed part of the Kuroshio Extension System Study (KESS).

Five current-meter moorings were used to investigate abyssal currents beneath the Kuroshio Extension.

---

## Deep currents

The observations demonstrate that abyssal currents throughout the region are weakly bottom intensified.

The analysis used a reference depth of approximately:

# **5,300 m.**

Therefore measurable current variability associated with the Kuroshio Extension system exists several kilometres below the ocean surface.

---

## Seamount interaction

One of the moorings was located near an isolated seamount.

At this location the bottom trapping was:

# **more pronounced and more energetic**

than in regions without steep topography.

The fitted vertical trapping depth near the isolated seamount was approximately:

**5 km**

compared with approximately:

**8–15 km**

away from steep topography.

The authors conclude that currents were amplified and bottom trapping intensified in the vicinity of isolated seamounts.

---

## Significance

This establishes that the Kuroshio Extension has a measurable abyssal dynamical component.

It also establishes that interaction with isolated seamounts can strengthen the bottom-intensified current signal.

---

## Relevance to Measurable Science

Together with the Tokara Strait observations, this provides evidence that interaction between the Kuroshio system and submarine topography occurs at very different depths.

In Tokara Strait, the Kuroshio interacts strongly with shallow seamounts.

Farther east beneath the Kuroshio Extension, measurable currents extend to abyssal depths and become more strongly bottom trapped near isolated seamounts.

The study does not establish a geothermal origin for these currents.
