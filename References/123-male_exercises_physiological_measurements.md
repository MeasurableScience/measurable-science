
# Male Exercises — Physiological Measurements

## Purpose

This reference documents studies relevant to the four male exercises discussed in Measurable Science: testicular massage, Kegel exercises, the Deer Exercise, and the Knife Sharpening technique.

It distinguishes measured physiological responses from my specific exercise protocols.

## 1. Mechanical testicular compression and testosterone

Boustead et al. (1993).
Testicular compression during exercise: serum testosterone levels.

Archives of Andrology, 30(3), 209–211.

https://doi.org/10.3109/01485019308987758

Fourteen male volunteers underwent controlled mechanical testicular compression using a pneumatic cuff.

Blood testosterone was measured before, during, and after compression.

Mean testosterone increased by 16.7–18.2%, with the maximum increase occurring approximately 1.5 minutes after compression was released.

The researchers concluded that compression might contribute to exercise-related testosterone changes but could not explain the entire response.

This experiment did not investigate gentle circular testicular massage, 60 repetitions three times daily, or long-term testosterone changes.

The result is not a safety assessment of testicular massage.

## 2. Pelvic-floor training and erectile function

Dorey et al. (2004).
Randomised controlled trial of pelvic floor muscle exercises and manometric biofeedback for erectile dysfunction.

British Journal of General Practice.

https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1324914/

The randomized trial recruited 55 men with erectile dysfunction.

The intervention combined pelvic-floor muscle training, biofeedback, and lifestyle changes. The initial control group received lifestyle advice.

After three months, the intervention group showed significantly greater improvements in erectile function and anal pressure.

At the end of the study, 22 men (40%) had regained normal erectile function, 19 (34.5%) had improved, and 14 (25.5%) had not improved.

The findings support pelvic-floor rehabilitation for erectile dysfunction in the studied population.

They do not validate my specific protocol of 60 contractions three times daily.

## 3. Pelvic-floor rehabilitation and ejaculatory control

Pelvic floor muscle rehabilitation for patients with lifelong premature ejaculation: a novel therapeutic approach (2014).

https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4003840/

Forty men with lifelong premature ejaculation underwent 12 weeks of pelvic-floor rehabilitation.

At the end of treatment, 33 men (82.5%) were classified as having gained control over their ejaculatory reflex.

Their mean intravaginal ejaculatory latency time reached approximately 146 seconds.

The study supports investigating pelvic-floor rehabilitation as a treatment for premature ejaculation.

However, it did not include a randomized control group and did not test the precise two-stage contraction and upward-pulling sequence described in my Deer Exercise.

## 4. Stop-start training and the Knife Sharpening technique

Behavioral Therapies for Management of Premature Ejaculation: A Systematic Review (2015).

https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26468381/

The review included 10 randomized controlled trials involving 521 participants.

The interventions included stop-start, squeeze techniques, pelvic-floor rehabilitation, and other behavioral approaches.

The stop-start principle involves approaching ejaculation, interrupting stimulation, allowing arousal to decrease, and resuming stimulation.

Some trials reported improvements in ejaculatory latency and control, while others did not demonstrate significant differences.

The authors considered the evidence limited because of methodological weaknesses and variation between interventions.

These findings establish that the central stop-start principle has been investigated clinically.

They do not validate my exact Knife Sharpening technique or its prescribed number of movements.

## Conclusion

The scientific literature documents short-term hormonal responses to mechanical compression, improvements in erectile function after pelvic-floor rehabilitation, and clinical investigation of ejaculatory-control techniques.

These findings provide separate physiological reference points for my investigation.

They do not demonstrate that my four exercises, performed together in the proposed sequence, produce the claimed final transformation.
