# Reference 056 — Tasmantid Seamount Chain: Volcanic Origin and Morphology

## Purpose

This reference supports the statements in the book concerning the volcanic origin, length and age progression of the Tasmantid Seamount Chain east of Australia.

The Tasmantid Seamount Chain is a major submarine volcanic chain extending for more than:

2,400 km.

Detailed geological and geophysical investigation identifies the Tasmantid Seamounts as an age-progressive chain of intraplate volcanoes.

Their ages range approximately from:

56 Ma in the north

to

7 Ma in the south.

The chain was emplaced across the extinct Tasman Sea spreading centre and associated fracture-zone structures.

Morphological and geophysical observations demonstrate that pre-existing tectonic structures strongly influenced the position, orientation and morphology of the later submarine volcanoes.

## Relevance to the model

The book follows the East Australian Current through a region containing a major submarine volcanic system.

This reference establishes independently that the Tasmantid structures are volcanic and that they form a continuous geological system extending for more than 2,400 km.

It does not demonstrate that the volcanoes heat or generate the East Australian Current.
