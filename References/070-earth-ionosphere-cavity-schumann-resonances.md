# Reference 070 — Earth–Ionosphere Electromagnetic Cavity and Schumann Resonances

## Purpose

This reference supports the statement that the region bounded by the
Earth's surface and the ionosphere behaves as a natural electromagnetic
resonant cavity.

Its experimentally observed resonant modes are known as
**Schumann resonances**.

## Earth–ionosphere electromagnetic cavity

The Earth's surface and the ionosphere form boundaries of a global
electromagnetic cavity.

Extremely low frequency (ELF) electromagnetic waves can propagate
through this cavity and establish global resonant modes.

The possibility of electromagnetic standing waves in the
Earth–ionosphere cavity was theoretically developed by
**Winfried Otto Schumann in 1952**.

Experimental spectra of the cavity resonances were subsequently
measured by **Martin Balser and Charles A. Wagner in 1960**.

## Schumann resonances

The lowest average resonant frequencies of the Earth–ionosphere
cavity are approximately:

- 7.8 Hz
- 14.3 Hz
- 20.8 Hz
- 27.3 Hz
- 33.8 Hz

These frequencies lie in the Extremely Low Frequency (ELF) range.

The fundamental mode is therefore approximately:

**f₁ ≈ 7.8 Hz**

The precise resonant frequencies are not perfectly fixed values.
They depend on the physical properties and changing conditions of
the Earth–ionosphere cavity.

## Relation between resonance and cavity size

For an idealized thin and lossless spherical cavity, the resonant
eigenfrequencies can be approximated by:

ωₙ = (c / R) √[n(n + 1)]

or, expressed as ordinary frequency:

fₙ = (c / 2πR) √[n(n + 1)]

where:

- **c** = speed of light,
- **R** = radius of the resonant cavity,
- **n** = resonance mode number.

Therefore, the characteristic dimensions of the cavity are directly
involved in determining its resonant frequencies.

The real Earth–ionosphere cavity is not an ideal lossless spherical
resonator. Atmospheric conductivity, ionospheric structure, losses
and variations of the upper boundary modify the frequencies from
the values predicted by the simplest ideal model.

## Experimental confirmation

Balser and Wagner experimentally observed Earth–ionosphere cavity
resonances in 1960.

Their work provided direct observational evidence for the
electromagnetic cavity resonances predicted theoretically by Schumann.

Later ground and satellite observations have repeatedly measured
these ELF resonant modes.

## Important distinction

The experimentally measured Schumann resonance near **7.8 Hz**
should not be treated as an exact universal constant.

The observed resonance frequency varies because the real
Earth–ionosphere cavity is dynamic and lossy.

The important established result for the present discussion is:

**Earth's surface + ionosphere form a natural electromagnetic
resonant cavity whose fundamental observed mode is approximately
7.8 Hz.**
