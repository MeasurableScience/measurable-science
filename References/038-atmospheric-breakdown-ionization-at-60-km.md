# Reference 038 — Atmospheric electrical breakdown and plasma formation near 60 km altitude

## Purpose

This reference documents the known atmospheric physics used in the book
to examine what could happen above the summit of the hypothetical
Rupes Nigra structure.

Reference 037 produced a modeled summit potential difference in the
megavolt range.

The present reference asks a separate question:

What happens to air near an altitude of approximately 60 km when a
sufficiently strong electric field is applied?

---

## Breakdown field decreases with altitude

The conventional electrical breakdown field of air is strongly dependent
on atmospheric density.

At sea-level pressure, the conventional breakdown field is of the order of:

3.2 × 10^6 V/m

As atmospheric density decreases with altitude, the breakdown threshold
also decreases.

Pasko (2010) describes this relationship and notes that the conventional
breakdown field decreases approximately in proportion to neutral gas
density.

Source:

Pasko, V. P. (2010).

"Recent advances in theory of transient luminous events."

Journal of Geophysical Research: Space Physics, 115, A00E35.

DOI:

https://doi.org/10.1029/2009JA014860

---

## Conditions near 60 km

Rycroft et al. (2010) modeled electrical breakdown at several mesospheric
altitudes, including approximately:

60 km
70 km
80 km

Their calculated threshold fields vary strongly with altitude because of
the decreasing neutral gas density.

At approximately 60 km, the relevant breakdown and streamer thresholds
are in the range of hundreds of volts per metre.

The model demonstrates that sufficiently strong electrical disturbances
can exceed the local breakdown threshold at approximately 60 km and
produce conditions associated with sprite development.

Source:

Rycroft, M. J., Odzimek, A., Arnold, N. F., Füllekrug, M.,
Kulak, A., & Neubert, T. (2010).

"Effects of lightning and sprites on the ionospheric potential,
and threshold effects on sprite initiation, obtained using an
analog model of the global atmospheric electric circuit."

Journal of Geophysical Research: Space Physics, 115, A00E37.

DOI:

https://doi.org/10.1029/2009JA014758

## Conventional breakdown criterion

Hu et al. describe the altitude-dependent conventional breakdown field as:

Ek = 3.2 × 10^6 (N/N0) V/m

where:

N = local atmospheric number density

N0 = atmospheric number density at ground level

This relationship shows directly why electrical breakdown becomes
possible at much lower electric-field strengths at mesospheric altitude
than near the Earth's surface.

Source:

Hu, W., Cummer, S. A., Lyons, W. A., & Nelson, T. E. (2007).

"Testing sprite initiation theory using lightning measurements
and modeled electromagnetic fields."

Journal of Geophysical Research: Atmospheres, 112, D13115.

DOI:

https://doi.org/10.1029/2006JD007939

## What happens after breakdown?

When the local electric field becomes sufficiently strong, electrons
gain enough energy between collisions to ionize additional neutral
molecules.

This produces electron multiplication.

Localized ionization can then develop into self-propagating streamer
discharges.

The gas therefore changes from a predominantly neutral insulating medium
into a partially ionized electrically conductive medium:

plasma.

The process can also produce visible optical emission.

---

## Direct natural example: sprites

Sprites are transient luminous electrical discharges observed in the
upper atmosphere.

Published observations and models place sprite structures broadly within
approximately:

40–90 km altitude.

Their fine structures include streamer channels and branching luminous
formations.

Gamerota et al. (2011) state that most sprites occur between approximately
40 and 90 km and relate their formation to conventional electrical
breakdown in the upper atmosphere.

Source:

Gamerota, W. R., Cummer, S. A., Li, J., Stenbaek-Nielsen, H. C.,
Haaland, R. K., & McHarg, M. G. (2011).

"Comparison of sprite initiation altitudes between observations
and models."

Journal of Geophysical Research: Space Physics, 116, A02317.

DOI:

https://doi.org/10.1029/2010JA016095

---

## Observed electric fields in sprite plasma

Spectroscopic observations provide additional evidence that strong
electric fields exist inside sprite streamer structures.

Adachi et al. (2006) estimated reduced electric fields in sprite regions
and found a transition near approximately 75 km.

Below this altitude, measured values were approximately:

100–260 Td

where the conventional breakdown reduced field is approximately:

128 Td.

Source:

Adachi, T. et al. (2006).

"Electric field transition between the diffuse and streamer regions
of sprites estimated from ISUAL/array photometer measurements."

Geophysical Research Letters, 33, L17803.

DOI:

https://doi.org/10.1029/2006GL026495

## Application to the Rupes Nigra working model

The scientific references above establish independently that:

1. the electrical breakdown threshold of air decreases strongly with altitude;

2. at approximately 60 km, breakdown and streamer propagation occur at
electric fields many orders of magnitude smaller than the sea-level
breakdown field;

3. electrical breakdown at these altitudes can produce ionization;

4. ionization can develop into streamer channels;

5. these channels consist of electrically conductive, luminous plasma;

6. natural luminous plasma structures are directly observed in the
approximately 40–90 km altitude region.

These observations do not establish the existence of Rupes Nigra or of
the electrical system proposed in the book.

They provide the measured physical behavior of rarefied atmospheric gas
against which the hypothetical model can be tested.

---

## Connection with Reference 037

Reference 037 independently estimated that the working Rupes Nigra model,
with an idealized current of approximately:

33 MA

could produce a summit potential difference of the order of:

4–10 MV

depending on the effective electrical resistivity of the volcanic column.

Reference 038 does not derive that voltage.

It establishes what known atmospheric physics predicts if a sufficiently
strong electric field is produced near approximately 60 km altitude:

electric breakdown
→ electron multiplication
→ ionization
→ streamer formation
→ luminous plasma.

The next stage of the model is therefore to calculate the electric field
around the summit and determine whether the modeled megavolt potential is
sufficient to cross the local breakdown threshold.
