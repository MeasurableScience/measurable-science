# Reference 047 — Atlantis II Seamounts: Deep Current and Temperature Measurements

## Source

Godin, O. A., Tan, T. W., Joseph, J. E., et al. (2024).

**Observation of exceptionally strong near-bottom flows over the Atlantis II Seamounts in the northwest Atlantic.**

*Scientific Reports*, 14, Article 10308.

Published: 5 May 2024.

Original publication:
https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-60528-2

---

## Location

The measurements were performed at the Atlantis II Seamounts in the northwestern Atlantic.

The Atlantis II Seamounts belong to the:

**New England Seamount Chain**

which is volcanic in origin.

The authors explicitly state that, depending on the position of the Gulf Stream axis, the experiment site was located in or close to the area where:

**the Gulf Stream crosses the New England Seamount Chain.**

---

## Direct measurements

Moored instruments were deployed on the flanks and within the deep terrain of the Atlantis II Seamounts during April–June 2023.

Measurement depths included:

- 2,573 m
- 2,994 m
- 4,443 m

Sensors were positioned only several metres above the seafloor.

The instruments directly measured:

- horizontal current velocity
- current direction
- water temperature
- acoustic pressure fluctuations

This is therefore direct in-situ measurement of water moving immediately above the deep seafloor around the volcanic seamount structures.

---

## Exceptionally strong deep currents

At the 2,573 m site, measured current speeds reached:

**1.13 m/s**

and exceeded 0.20 m/s approximately 5% of the measurement period.

At the 4,443 m site, speeds reached:

**0.44 m/s**

The authors report that the peak speeds exceeding 1.1 m/s were greater than previously reported directly measured current speeds at comparable or greater depths.

This demonstrates that exceptionally strong water movement occurs immediately above the deep seafloor on the flanks of the Atlantis II Seamounts.

---

## Temperature measurement

The most important observation for the present investigation occurred during the strongest current event at the 2,573 m site.

The beginning of the extreme-current event was accompanied by a rapid increase in water temperature of approximately:

**0.2 °C**

and the end of the event was accompanied by a corresponding temperature decrease.

The strong-current episode lasted approximately 21 hours.

The authors report that similar features were reproduced in other strong-current events at the same measurement site.

At the deeper 4,443 m station, some strong-flow events also showed elevated temperature, although the behaviour was less consistent.

---

## Important limitation

The experiment demonstrates a correlation between strong near-bottom flow events and increased water temperature.

It does **not** establish that the additional heat originates inside the Atlantis II Seamounts.

The paper investigates deep-current dynamics and acoustic signatures rather than geothermal heat flow.

Therefore the measured temperature increase must not, by itself, be described as proof of geothermal heating.

What is directly established is:

**a volcanic seamount system exists beneath the Gulf Stream crossing region;**

**exceptionally strong deep currents occur immediately above its seafloor;**

and

**the strongest measured flow events can be accompanied by measurable increases in water temperature.**

---

## Additional observation — low-frequency pressure fluctuations

The experiment also recorded pressure fluctuations associated with the near-bottom currents.

The authors analysed the infrasonic range from approximately:

**0.5–20 Hz**

and found a strong relationship between current speed and low-frequency flow-noise intensity.

At the 2,573 m station, the squared current speed and acoustic intensity were strongly correlated, with a reported Pearson correlation coefficient of:

**0.873**

The peak frequency of the flow-associated pressure spectrum increased with increasing current speed.

This observation is independent of the temperature result but may be relevant to later investigation of low-frequency mechanical signals generated by large water flows.

---

## Relevance to the model

The deep-water system proposed in *Measurable Science* predicts that large subsurface pathways should leave measurable signatures where they interact with the ocean.

At Atlantis II, several independently measurable features occur together:

1. a volcanic seamount structure,
2. the Gulf Stream crossing region,
3. exceptionally strong near-bottom water movement at depths of several kilometres,
4. measurable temperature increases during the strongest flow events,
5. measurable low-frequency pressure fluctuations associated with the flow.

These measurements do not establish the origin of the deep water or heat.

They provide observational constraints against which the proposed deep-flow model can be tested.
