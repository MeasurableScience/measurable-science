# Reference 044 — Water radiolysis under ionizing radiation

## Purpose

This reference documents a directly measured and experimentally established
physical process relevant to the model developed in the book:

# ionizing radiation can decompose water and produce new reactive and
# molecular species, including molecular hydrogen.

This reference does NOT demonstrate the existence of the geological reactor
proposed in the book.

It establishes only the underlying physical process:

**water exposed to sufficiently energetic ionizing radiation undergoes
radiolysis.**

---

# 1. What is water radiolysis?

Water radiolysis is the decomposition and chemical transformation of water
caused by ionizing radiation.

Ionizing radiation deposits energy into H₂O molecules, producing ionization
and excitation.

The initial processes generate reactive species including:

- hydrated electrons: e⁻aq
- hydrogen atoms/radicals: H•
- hydroxyl radicals: •OH
- hydronium ions: H₃O⁺

Subsequent reactions produce stable molecular products including:

- molecular hydrogen: H₂
- hydrogen peroxide: H₂O₂

A simplified representation is therefore:

H₂O + ionizing radiation
→ e⁻aq + H• + •OH + H₃O⁺ + H₂ + H₂O₂ + secondary products

The exact yields depend on:

- radiation type;
- radiation energy;
- linear energy transfer (LET);
- temperature;
- pressure;
- dissolved gases;
- impurities;
- mineral and oxide surfaces;
- and surrounding chemical conditions.

---

# 2. This is experimentally established

Le Caër (2011) summarizes the experimentally established chemistry of water
radiolysis.

The review states that ionizing radiation acting on water produces:

- electrons;
- H• atoms;
- •OH radicals;
- H₃O⁺ ions;
- molecular hydrogen H₂;
- hydrogen peroxide H₂O₂.

Water radiolysis has been studied experimentally for decades and is relevant
to several fields including nuclear reactors and nuclear-waste storage.

The phenomenon therefore does not depend on the geological model proposed
in this book.

It is independently reproducible radiation chemistry.

---

# 3. Molecular hydrogen can be produced directly from water

An important product for the present investigation is:

# H₂

Ionizing radiation deposits energy into water.

After the initial ionization and excitation processes, hydrogen atoms,
hydrated electrons and other reactive intermediates undergo further
reactions.

Some of these reaction pathways produce molecular hydrogen.

Representative reactions include:

H• + H• → H₂

and reactions involving hydrated electrons, protons and other radiolytic
intermediates.

Therefore:

# ionizing radiation provides a demonstrated physical mechanism by which
# water can become a source of molecular hydrogen.

This does not imply that all irradiated water is converted into hydrogen.

Most water remains water, and many radiolytic products subsequently react
with one another.

The final chemical composition depends strongly on the physical and chemical
environment.

---

# 4. Hydrogen peroxide and oxidizing species are also produced

Radiolysis does not produce only hydrogen.

Important products include:

H₂O₂

and reactive oxidizing species such as:

•OH

These products are important because they strongly affect the redox chemistry
and corrosion behavior of irradiated aqueous systems.

Thus radiolysis creates simultaneously:

**reducing species**

and

**oxidizing species.**

The resulting chemistry is a dynamic reaction network rather than a simple
one-step decomposition of:

H₂O → H₂ + O₂.

---

# 5. Radiolysis occurs in real nuclear reactor systems

Water is widely used as coolant in nuclear reactors.

Inside water-cooled nuclear reactors, the coolant is exposed to intense
ionizing radiation.

Consequently, radiolysis of reactor water is a real engineering phenomenon
that must be taken into account when controlling reactor chemistry and
corrosion.

Reviews of water-cooled fission reactors identify radiolytic species such as:

- H₂
- O₂
- H₂O₂
- •OH

as important components of reactor coolant chemistry.

In pressurized-water reactors, hydrogen is deliberately added to the primary
coolant partly to suppress the accumulation of oxidizing radiolysis products.

The fact that commercial reactor chemistry must actively control these
reactions demonstrates that water radiolysis is not merely a theoretical
possibility.

It is an observed engineering process.

---

# 6. Mineral and oxide surfaces matter

The geological model in the book contains another potentially relevant
feature:

water is not isolated.

It is in contact with rock and mineral surfaces.

Experiments show that solid/liquid interfaces can significantly modify
radiolytic chemistry.

Le Caër specifically reviews experiments showing that oxide surfaces can
alter H₂ production during irradiation.

Therefore the radiolytic behavior of water flowing through rock cannot be
assumed to be identical to the radiolysis of chemically pure water in an
isolated laboratory container.

The surrounding material participates in the chemistry.

This point is important for the model but does NOT by itself establish the
magnitude or composition of radiolysis products in the proposed geological
system.

Those quantities would require additional assumptions or measurements.

---

# 7. What is established and what remains part of the model?

## Experimentally established

Ionizing radiation can:

1. ionize and excite water molecules;
2. generate hydrated electrons;
3. generate H• radicals;
4. generate •OH radicals;
5. generate H₂;
6. generate H₂O₂;
7. produce additional secondary chemical species;
8. alter water chemistry in nuclear reactor environments;
9. interact with mineral/oxide surfaces in ways that change radiolytic
   product yields.

These statements are experimentally established.

---

## Not established by this reference

This reference does NOT establish:

- the existence of the geological nuclear reactor proposed in the book;
- the radiation intensity inside that proposed reactor;
- the fraction of the 9 Sv water flux that would undergo radiolysis;
- the total amount of H₂ that the proposed system would produce;
- the direction in which the resulting fluids would travel;
- the existence of a connection between such fluids and particular ocean
  currents.

Those are separate questions.

The purpose of this reference is narrower:

# if water is exposed to sufficiently energetic ionizing radiation,
# radiolysis provides a known physical pathway for producing hydrogen
# and other reactive chemical species.

# Sources

## Primary review

Le Caër, S. (2011).

**Water Radiolysis: Influence of Oxide Surfaces on H₂ Production under
Ionizing Radiation.**

*Water*, 3(1), 235–253.

DOI:
https://doi.org/10.3390/w3010235

The paper reviews the fundamental mechanism of water radiolysis, the
formation of e⁻aq, H•, •OH, H₃O⁺, H₂ and H₂O₂, and experimental evidence
for modification of H₂ production at oxide/water interfaces.

Fusion Reactors: Part I — Assessment of Radiolysis Models.**

*Corrosion and Materials Degradation*.

https://doi.org/10.3390/cmd3030028

This review examines water radiolysis specifically in water-cooled nuclear

corrosion.

# Relevance to the book

The physical chain established by experiment is:

ionizing radiation
↓
energy deposited into water
↓
ionization / excitation
↓
e⁻aq + H• + •OH
↓
secondary chemical reactions
↓
H₂ + H₂O₂ + other products

Therefore, when the book later investigates what could happen to water
entering the proposed radioactive geological system, the possibility of
radiolysis does not need to be invented as a new physical mechanism.

# Water radiolysis is an experimentally established process.

What remains to be determined by the model is its scale under the proposed
conditions.
