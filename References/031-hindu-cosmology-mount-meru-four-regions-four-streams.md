# Reference 031 — Hindu Cosmology: Mount Meru, Four Regions and Four Streams

## Source tradition

Traditional Hindu cosmology as described in the **Viṣṇu Purāṇa** and related Purāṇic literature.

The relevant passage appears in **Book II, Chapter II** of the *Viṣṇu Purāṇa*.

## Mount Meru at the centre

The *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* describes **Mount Meru** as the great central mountain of the world-system.

Meru is placed in the central region of Jambūdvīpa and functions as the principal geographical and cosmological axis of the surrounding structure.

Source:

*Viṣṇu Purāṇa*, Book II, Chapter II.

https://www.sacred-texts.com/hin/vp/vp059.htm

## Divine city at the summit

At the summit of Mount Meru is described the great city or celestial abode of **Brahmā**.

This makes Meru not merely a geographical mountain within the cosmological description, but a central structure directly associated with a divine realm.

Source:

*Viṣṇu Purāṇa*, Book II, Chapter II.

https://www.sacred-texts.com/hin/vp/vp059.htm

## Four surrounding regions

The same chapter describes four principal regions associated with the four sides of Mount Meru:

- **Bhadrāśva** — East
- **Bhārata** — South
- **Ketumāla** — West
- **Uttarakuru** — North

These regions are described as lying around Meru in the four principal directions.

Some translations compare their arrangement around the central mountain to petals surrounding the centre of a lotus.

Source:

*Viṣṇu Purāṇa*, Book II, Chapter II.

https://www.sacred-texts.com/hin/vp/vp059.htm

## Four streams of the Ganga

The same cosmological description states that the celestial **Ganga** reaches the region of Meru and divides into **four great streams**.

These streams flow toward the four cardinal directions:

- East
- South
- West
- North

Thus, within the same traditional description, Mount Meru is associated with both:

- four surrounding regions;
- four major water streams extending in the four principal directions.

Source:

*Viṣṇu Purāṇa*, Book II, Chapter II.

https://www.sacred-texts.com/hin/vp/vp059.htm

## Parallel Purāṇic tradition

A similar cosmological structure also appears in the **Śiva Purāṇa**, where Mount Meru occupies a central position and the Ganga is described as dividing into multiple streams associated with the cardinal directions.

Source:

*Śiva Purāṇa*, English translation.

https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/shiva-purana-english/d/doc226562.html

## Structure documented by the Hindu tradition

Without imposing a modern physical interpretation on the source, the relevant geometry can be summarized as:

- a **central mountain — Mount Meru**;
- a divine city or celestial abode associated with its summit;
- **four principal regions** positioned around the central mountain;
- the four regions associated with the **four cardinal directions**;
- the **Ganga dividing into four major streams**;
- the four streams extending toward the four principal directions.

These elements belong to the Purāṇic cosmological description itself.

## Relevance to the book

This reference documents the Hindu cosmological structure.

It does **not** establish that Mount Meru is identical with Mercator's *Rupes Nigra*, Njegoš's central mountain, the Buddhist Mount Meru, the Vatican obelisk, or any physical structure proposed in *Measurable Science*.

The comparison made in the book concerns the recurring geometry:

**central structure → divine association → four surrounding regions → four streams**

Any interpretation connecting these descriptions with the physical model developed in *Measurable Science* is the author's hypothesis.
