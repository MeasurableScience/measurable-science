# Reference 093 — Endogenous Brain DMT and Cardiac Arrest

## Source

Dean, J. G., Liu, T., Huff, S., Sheler, B., Barker, S. A., Strassman, R. J., Wang, M. M., & Borjigin, J. (2019).

**Biosynthesis and Extracellular Concentrations of N,N-dimethyltryptamine (DMT) in Mammalian Brain.**

*Scientific Reports*, 9, 9333.

DOI: 10.1038/s41598-019-45812-w

## Key Findings

The researchers demonstrated the presence of the enzymatic machinery required for endogenous DMT synthesis in mammalian brain tissue.

Using in vivo microdialysis, endogenous extracellular DMT was measured in the rat brain.

Following experimentally induced cardiac arrest, extracellular DMT concentrations increased significantly.

The increase was also observed in animals whose pineal glands had been removed, demonstrating that the pineal gland is not required for the observed increase.

## Relevance

The study provides direct experimental evidence that endogenous DMT exists extracellularly in the mammalian brain and that its concentration can increase during the physiological transition associated with cardiac arrest.

The experiment was performed in rats and does not establish that the same increase occurs during human death.

It also does not establish that DMT causes near-death experiences or that DMT carries consciousness.

For the model developed in this book, the result is relevant because it establishes a measurable change in endogenous DMT during a major transition at the end of biological function.
