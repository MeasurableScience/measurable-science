# Reference 025 — Google Earth imagery around the North Pole

## Source

Whitehead, T. (2016).
"Why Santa's base remains a secret."

## Satellite imagery limits

The article examines the unusual appearance and limitations of imagery
around the geographic poles in Google Earth.

It notes that the North Pole lies in the Arctic Ocean and that Google
Earth normally displays seafloor mapping rather than ordinary satellite
imagery over the open ocean.

The article also gives specific limits relevant to polar satellite
coverage.

The northern limit of Landsat imagery is approximately:

82.7° N

The northern circular boundary visible in Google Earth's global
historical-imagery mosaics is reported at approximately:

83.6° N

The author identifies this as the limit of those mosaics as generated
in Google Earth Engine.

## Mapping and projection

The article explains several technical factors affecting polar imagery:

- sun-synchronous imaging satellites do not necessarily cover the
  geographic poles;
- Landsat imagery terminates before reaching 90° N;
- Web Mercator mapping cannot represent the pole itself;
- low Sun angles and months of polar darkness complicate optical
  imagery;
- Google's imagery and rendering systems produce visible seams and
  boundaries in polar regions.

## Relevance to the book

This reference supports the limited claim that publicly displayed
Google Earth imagery around the geographic North Pole is not a normal,
continuous high-resolution satellite mosaic comparable with imagery
available over many populated land areas.

The visible boundaries themselves do not demonstrate intentional
concealment.

The author's decision to investigate older geographical accounts of
the region is separate from the technical explanation offered for the
imagery limitations.
