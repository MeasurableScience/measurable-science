# Reference 008 — Neutron

## Purpose

Reference for the standard physics description of the neutron used in
*Merljiva Nauka*, including its quark structure, beta decay, measured mass,
and the modern QCD description of hadron mass.

## Standard description

According to CERN:

- the neutron is a composite baryon;
- its valence-quark structure is **udd**:
  - one up quark with electric charge **+2/3**,
  - two down quarks with electric charge **-1/3** each;
- the total electric charge of the neutron is therefore zero;
- the strong interaction binds quarks together to form protons and neutrons;
- in beta decay, a neutron can transform into a proton, with one down
  quark changing into an up quark through the weak interaction.

The neutron structure therefore changes from:

**udd → uud**

corresponding to the transition from neutron to proton.

Free-neutron beta decay is conventionally written:

**n → p + e⁻ + ν̄ₑ**

where the products are a proton, electron and electron antineutrino.

## Neutron mass

The neutron has a precisely measured mass.

The CODATA value is approximately:

**mₙ = 1.67492750056 × 10⁻²⁷ kg**

with the corresponding rest-energy equivalent:

**mₙc² = 939.56542194 MeV**

The neutron therefore provides a directly measured example of a composite
hadronic structure possessing a definite mass and corresponding rest energy.

## Mass and internal QCD dynamics

In quantum chromodynamics, the mass of a hadron is not described as the
simple sum of the masses of its valence quarks.

The mass of a hadronic system can be analysed through contributions associated
with quark and gluon energy, quark masses, and the QCD trace anomaly.

Lattice-QCD calculations have demonstrated this type of mass decomposition
for hadronic systems.

In 2018, Yang et al. reported a lattice-QCD decomposition of the proton mass
using the QCD energy-momentum tensor. Their calculation separated
contributions associated with quark energy, gluon-field energy, quark scalar
condensates and the trace anomaly.

In 2026, Bollweg et al. reported the first lattice-QCD validation of multiple
sum rules associated with the quark-gluon decomposition of hadron mass,
calculating the relevant components of the QCD energy-momentum tensor from
first principles.

The 2026 calculation was demonstrated for the ηc and charmonium states,
not for the neutron. The method, however, is general and the authors state
that it can be applied to mass decompositions of other hadrons and nuclei.

These results support the standard QCD description in which hadron mass is
connected not only with the masses of constituent quarks, but also with the
energy and dynamics of the interacting quark-gluon system.
