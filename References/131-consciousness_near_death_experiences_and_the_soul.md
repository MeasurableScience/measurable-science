# Consciousness, Near-Death Experiences, and the Question of the Soul

## Scientific Background

Modern science has not established the existence of a soul as an independent entity that controls the human body or survives biological death.

Neuroscience studies consciousness, memory, and personal identity primarily through their relationship with brain activity. However, the nature of subjective experience remains an active area of research.

Several experimental studies are relevant to this question.

## 1. AWARE II — Consciousness During Cardiac Arrest (2023)

**Researchers:** Sam Parnia et al.  
**Journal:** *Resuscitation*

This multicenter study investigated awareness during cardiopulmonary resuscitation using EEG monitoring, cerebral oxygen measurements, interviews, and audiovisual tests.

Some survivors reported memories or experiences associated with consciousness during cardiac arrest. The researchers also observed organized EEG activity during some resuscitation periods.

The study did not establish that consciousness exists independently of the brain.

**Original research:**
- [PubMed — PMID 37423492](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37423492/)
- [DOI — Full publication](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resuscitation.2023.109903)

## 2. Near-Death Experiences — Netherlands Study (2001)

**Researchers:** Pim van Lommel et al.  
**Journal:** *The Lancet*

Researchers studied 344 successfully resuscitated cardiac patients across ten Dutch hospitals.

Of these patients, 62 (18%) reported near-death experiences.

The study examined possible relationships between these experiences and medical, psychological, and physiological factors.

The findings raised questions about consciousness during cardiac arrest but did not demonstrate survival of consciousness after irreversible death.

**Original research:**
- [PubMed — PMID 11755611](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11755611/)
- [DOI — The Lancet](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(01)07100-8)

## 3. Brain Connectivity During General Anesthesia (2019)

**Researchers:** Phillip E. Vlisides et al.  
**Journal:** *Anesthesiology*

Researchers recorded EEG activity in 53 surgical patients to investigate changes in cortical connectivity during general anesthesia.

The study examined how patterns of communication between brain regions change under anesthesia.

These measurements support a relationship between brain-network activity and states of consciousness, but they do not resolve the philosophical question of whether consciousness is entirely produced by the brain.

**Original research:**
- [Full study — PubMed Central](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6520139/)
- [DOI — Original publication](https://doi.org/10.1097/ALN.0000000000002677)

## Conclusion

**Measurement:** Brain activity and connectivity change with states of consciousness. Some resuscitated patients report experiences associated with periods of cardiac arrest.

**Scientific interpretation:** These findings provide evidence about the relationship between consciousness and brain function, but they do not establish the existence of a separate soul.

**Open hypothesis:** Whether personal consciousness or identity can exist independently of the biological brain remains unconfirmed.

No reproducible experiment cited here demonstrates that a soul leaves the body or survives irreversible biological death.

The purpose of this reference is to document the available measurements and provide direct access to the original research, not to claim proof for or against the existence of the soul.
