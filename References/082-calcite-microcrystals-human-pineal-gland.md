# Reference 082 — Calcite Microcrystals in the Human Pineal Gland

## Source

Baconnier S, Lang SB, Polomska M, Hilczer B, Berkovic G, Meshulam G.

**Calcite Microcrystals in the Pineal Gland of the Human Brain:
First Physical and Chemical Studies.**

Bioelectromagnetics. 2002;23(7):488–495.

DOI: 10.1002/bem.10053

PubMed:
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12224052/

## Experiment

Researchers isolated microscopic crystalline structures from human pineal
gland tissue.

Scanning electron microscopy revealed cubic, hexagonal and cylindrical
crystal morphologies.

Energy-dispersive spectroscopy showed that the crystals contained calcium,
carbon and oxygen.

Selected-area electron diffraction and near-infrared Raman spectroscopy
identified the material as calcite (calcium carbonate).

The crystals were less than approximately 20 micrometers in length and were
distinct from the larger hydroxyapatite concretions commonly known as pineal
"brain sand."

The authors also discussed the possibility that the complex crystal structure
could permit symmetry breaking and possible piezoelectric properties.

## Relevance to the text

The study provides direct physical and chemical evidence that genuine
microcrystalline structures occur naturally inside the human pineal gland.

Therefore, the statement in the book that the pineal gland contains crystalline
material is experimentally established.

The possible electromagnetic or information-processing role assigned to these
crystals in the model remains a hypothesis and is not demonstrated by this
experiment.
