# Reference 060 — Tasmantid Seamount Chain: Regional Submarine Volcanic System

## Purpose

This reference supports the broader geological interpretation of the Tasmantid Seamount Chain as a large regional submarine volcanic system east of Australia.

Modern high-resolution bathymetric mapping reveals numerous large volcanic edifices along the Tasmantid system.

The chain forms one of several major subparallel volcanic trails in eastern Australia and the southwest Pacific region.

Together with the East Australian Volcanic Chain and Lord Howe Seamount Chain, it records extensive intraplate volcanic activity extending across a very large geographical area.

The Tasmantid volcanoes also intersect older tectonic structures associated with the extinct Tasman Sea spreading centre and fracture zones.

## Relevance to the model

The book uses the Tasmantid system as the geological structure encountered while examining the fourth major warm current.

The relevant observation is not simply the existence of isolated seamounts.

It is the existence of an extensive submarine volcanic chain occupying the same broad southwest Pacific region through which the East Australian Current system develops and continues into the Tasman Sea.

This reference establishes the geological character of that system.

It does not establish that volcanic heat powers the East Australian Current.
