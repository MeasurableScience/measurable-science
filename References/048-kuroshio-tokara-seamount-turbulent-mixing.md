# Reference 048 — Kuroshio–Seamount Interaction and Water-Mass Modification in Tokara Strait

## Source

Tsutsumi, E., Matsuno, T., Lien, R.-C., Nakamura, H., Senjyu, T., & Guo, X. (2017).

**Turbulent mixing within the Kuroshio in the Tokara Strait.**

*Journal of Geophysical Research: Oceans*, 122(9), 7082–7094.

DOI: 10.1002/2017JC013049

Original publication:
https://doi.org/10.1002/2017JC013049

---

## Location

The observations were conducted in the Tokara Strait, where the Kuroshio exits the East China Sea and enters the northwestern Pacific.

The authors explicitly identify numerous seamounts and small islands within the route of the Kuroshio.

Several abrupt topographic structures in the strait rise to shallower than approximately 300 m, while the typical base of the Kuroshio flow in the East China Sea is approximately 400–500 m.

The current therefore directly interacts with the seafloor topography.

---

## Direct measurements

The experiment used:

- microstructure profilers,
- acoustic Doppler current profilers (ADCP),
- CTD measurements,
- a moored 75-kHz ADCP.

The measurements included:

- current velocity,
- turbulence,
- temperature,
- salinity.

---

## Strong interaction with seamounts

Downstream of shallow seamounts, the vertical structure and water properties of the Kuroshio were strongly modified.

Behind one seamount, vertical eddy diffusivity reached approximately:

**10^-3 to 10^-2 m²/s**

which was nearly:

# **100 times greater**

than at the upstream site.

The authors conclude that even stronger mixing, potentially of order:

**10^-2 to 10^-1 m²/s**

may occur immediately around the seamount.

---

## Strong subsurface current

Approximately 15 km downstream of Seamount II, a strong subsurface counter-current was directly observed beneath the Kuroshio.

Its velocity reached approximately:

# **0.5 m/s**

The interaction between the Kuroshio and the seamount therefore produces substantial changes not only in turbulence but also in the velocity structure of the water column.

---

## Temperature and salinity modification

The measurements show that the temperature-salinity structure of the Kuroshio changes downstream of the seamounts.

The vertical turbulent heat flux was strongly elevated near and downstream of the seamounts.

The measured heat-flux divergence was large enough to produce an estimated temperature evolution of approximately:

# **0.1–0.2 °C in one day.**

The authors conclude that the observed turbulent mixing produces significant water-mass modification along the Kuroshio.

---

## Relevance to Measurable Science

This study provides direct observational evidence that the Kuroshio does not simply pass above seafloor topography without interaction.

Seamounts located directly in its route measurably alter:

- current structure,
- turbulence,
- temperature,
- salinity,
- and water-mass properties.

The study therefore establishes a measurable connection between a major warm ocean current and major submarine topographic structures beneath its pathway.

It does not establish a geothermal origin for the observed temperature changes.
