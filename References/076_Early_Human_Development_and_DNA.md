## 1. From Zygote to Blastocyst

Human embryonic development begins with the formation of the zygote.

The zygote undergoes successive cleavage divisions. During early development,
the embryo passes through the morula stage and subsequently forms the
blastocyst.

During these stages, initially similar cells begin to acquire different
developmental identities through gene regulation, signalling pathways and
cell-to-cell interactions.

## 2. DNA — Information in a Double Helix

DNA (deoxyribonucleic acid) is the hereditary material of humans and almost all
other organisms.

Its physical structure consists of two complementary strands wound around one
another to form the characteristic **double helix**.

The sequence of DNA bases carries biological information, while complementary
base pairing provides the physical basis for copying that information.

## 3. Maternal–Fetal Material Transfer

The developing organism does not create the chemical elements required for its
construction.

The placenta forms the principal exchange interface between maternal and fetal
systems.

Through placental transport, the developing fetus receives substances required
for growth and metabolism, including:

- oxygen;
- water;
- glucose;
- amino acids;
- fatty acids;
- calcium;
- magnesium;
- potassium;
- phosphate and other mineral ions.

Maternal and fetal blood do not simply mix. Exchange occurs across specialized
placental structures through multiple transport mechanisms.

## 4. Observable Developmental Sequence

The experimentally observable biological sequence can therefore be summarized
as:

**FERTILIZATION  
→ ZYGOTE  
→ CLEAVAGE  
→ MORULA  
→ BLASTOCYST  
→ CELL DIFFERENTIATION  
→ TISSUES  
→ ORGANS  
→ ORGAN SYSTEMS**

The physical material required for this construction is supplied from the
maternal environment, while inherited DNA and developmental signalling regulate
how that material is organized into the developing organism.
