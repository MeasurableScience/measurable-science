
# Victor Truviano, Maria Skobtsova, and Tibetan Tukdam --- Source Reference

## Purpose

This reference supports the section "Transformacija" in Measurable Science, in which I examine personal testimony, Christian teachings about love, and Tibetan Buddhist practices associated with the process of dying.

I distinguish personal testimony, religious teachings, and experimental measurements.

## 1. Victor Truviano --- meditation and out-of-body testimony

Victor Truviano is an Argentine musician and former professional violinist.

In a public talk in Bali in 2015, he described his earlier life in Buenos Aires, his work as a concert violinist and teacher, and his subsequent withdrawal from that lifestyle.

He also described experiences in which he believed he had left his physical body for periods lasting two or three days.

Truviano publicly claims to have lived for prolonged periods without food or water and to have experienced unusual physical changes, including the regrowth of teeth.

### Sources

Victor Truviano --- "Talks on Breatharianism and the BabaJi State."

Transcript of a public talk in Bali, August 2015.

https://www.sacredmountainretreat.org/victor-truviano-talks-on-breatharianism-and-the-babaji-state

Victor Truviano --- interviews and conference recordings.

https://www.breatharianworld.com/en/video-pwf-2021-victor-truviano/

### Supported manuscript claims

Truviano describes his professional musical career, subsequent change in lifestyle, prolonged abstinence from food and water, and experiences that he interprets as leaving his physical body.

His statements constitute personal testimony.

They do not independently establish that he survived without water for the claimed periods, regenerated teeth, experienced the cessation of circulation, or separated his consciousness from his body.

I met Victor personally and exchanged a few words with him. This is my own experience, not a claim established by the cited sources.

The specific interview with Miroslav Mišek still requires its original recording or transcript as a separate source.

## 2. Maria Skobtsova --- the path to God through love

Maria Skobtsova, also known as Mother Maria of Paris, was an Orthodox nun who devoted much of her life to helping people in need.

Her writings emphasize active love for other human beings rather than religious observance understood as an end in itself.

In a passage concerning the Last Judgment, she explains that the decisive questions concern whether she fed the hungry, clothed the naked, and visited the sick and imprisoned.

### Church source

American Carpatho-Russian Orthodox Diocese of North America --- "St. Maria Skobtsova of Paris."

https://www.acrod.org/orthodox-christianity/articles/saints/stmaria

This Orthodox diocesan article presents her biography and reproduces her teaching about love, asceticism, and the Last Judgment.

### Supported manuscript claim

Maria Skobtsova places love expressed through concrete actions toward other people at the center of her understanding of Christian life.

Her teaching provides a different perspective from the search for extraordinary meditative experiences: the relationship established with other human beings is itself spiritually significant.

The comparison between her teaching and the systems model developed in Measurable Science is my interpretation.

## 3. Tibetan Buddhism --- tukdam and the clear light of death

Tukdam is a state recognized in Tibetan Buddhist traditions in which an advanced practitioner is believed to remain in meditation after clinical death.

According to these traditions, the process of dying presents an opportunity to recognize the fundamental nature of mind, often discussed in relation to clear light.

Experienced practitioners prepare for this through sustained meditation during life.

Traditional accounts associate tukdam with an apparent delay in bodily decomposition.

### Scientific source

Lott, D. T., et al. (2021).

"No Detectable Electroencephalographic Activity After Clinical Declaration of Death Among Tibetan Buddhist Meditators in Apparent Tukdam, a Putative Postmortem Meditation State."

Frontiers in Psychology, 11, 599190.

https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2020.599190

Full article:

https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2020.599190/full

### Scientific measurements

Researchers associated with the University of Wisconsin–Madison and Tibetan medical and religious institutions investigated reported cases of tukdam in India.

Between 2013 and 2019, EEG recordings were collected from 13 deceased individuals identified as potential tukdam cases.

Six recordings were excluded from the reported analysis because of excessive electrical interference or incomplete protocols.

The earliest postmortem recording began approximately 26 hours after clinical death. In many cases, recording began three to five days after death.

The researchers investigated spontaneous EEG activity and electrical responses to auditory stimulation.

They found no recognizable EEG waveforms in the analyzed postmortem cases.

### Supported manuscript claims

Tibetan Buddhist traditions describe tukdam as an advanced meditative state associated with the process of dying.

The practice has become the subject of scientific investigation.

The published study did not detect recognizable EEG activity in the analyzed postmortem cases.

Because recording did not begin immediately after death, these results do not establish what occurred during the earlier stages of dying.

The study does not demonstrate the survival of consciousness after death.

## 4. Comparative result used in Measurable Science

I examine three different perspectives.

Victor Truviano offers personal testimony about extraordinary experiences associated with meditation and withdrawal from ordinary life.

Maria Skobtsova emphasizes active love toward other people as a central element of spiritual transformation.

Tibetan Buddhism describes advanced meditation directed toward recognizing the nature of mind during the process of dying.

These are different kinds of sources and should not be treated as equivalent evidence.

The question I develop in the book is whether transformation requires withdrawal from ordinary life, whether it can be pursued through relationships with other people, and whether any relevant physiological processes can be measured while a person is still alive.

That question belongs to my investigation. None of the cited sources establishes the larger systems hypothesis proposed in Measurable Science.
