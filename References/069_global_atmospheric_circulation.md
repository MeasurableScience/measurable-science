## Relevance

NOAA describes the atmosphere as a global circulation system rather than a collection of isolated winds.

In each hemisphere, the large-scale circulation is commonly represented by three major circulation cells:

- Hadley cell
- Ferrel cell
- Polar cell

These cells contain large-scale meridional movement of air between lower and higher latitudes.

For example, in the Hadley circulation, air rises in the tropics, moves poleward in the upper atmosphere, descends in the subtropics and returns toward the equatorial region near the surface.

The Ferrel cell contains poleward and eastward flow near the surface and equatorward and westward flow higher in the atmosphere.

The Polar cell similarly contains circulation between polar and lower latitudes.

Earth's rotation deflects these meridional motions and produces major zonal wind systems, including:

- trade winds
- prevailing westerlies
- polar easterlies

NOAA therefore describes a global atmospheric circulation containing both meridional (north–south / south–north) and zonal (east–west / west–east) components.

NOAA's ocean-current material also explicitly notes that atmospheric circulation produces global wind patterns including the trade winds and westerlies, which are connected with the forcing of surface ocean circulation.

## Use in *Merljiva nauka*

This reference supports the statement that above the global circulation of ocean water there exists another organized global circulation system — the atmosphere — containing both meridional and zonal transport.

It does not by itself demonstrate that atmospheric circulation follows the four oceanic "magistrales" proposed in the author's model. That possible spatial relationship would require a separate comparison of measured atmospheric and oceanic circulation.
