# Reference 036 — Motional electromagnetic induction and estimated current of Rupes Nigra

## Purpose

This reference documents the physical method and calculation used in the book
to estimate the electric current produced in the working Rupes Nigra model.

The calculation applies known electromagnetic induction in moving conductive
seawater to the dimensions and parameters previously established in the model.

The result is a theoretical model result, not a direct measurement of
Rupes Nigra.

---

## Physical principle

Seawater is electrically conductive because it contains dissolved ions.

When conductive seawater moves through a magnetic field, the charged particles
in the water experience the Lorentz force and an electric field and electric
current can be generated.

This phenomenon is known as:

**motional electromagnetic induction**

and is a standard process studied in physical oceanography.

The generalized Ohm's law for a moving conducting fluid can be written:

J = σ(E + v × B)

where:

- J = electric current density (A/m²)
- σ = electrical conductivity (S/m)
- E = electric field (V/m)
- v = velocity of the conducting fluid (m/s)
- B = magnetic flux density (T)

For the first idealized calculation used in the book, the internally generated
opposing electric field is neglected and the water velocity is assumed to have
the orientation that maximizes the motional term.

The working expression therefore becomes:

J ≈ σvB

This represents an idealized upper-bound / short-circuit calculation.

## Electrical conductivity of seawater

For standard seawater with:

- salinity = 35
- temperature = 15 °C
- pressure = 0 dbar

the reference electrical conductivity is:

σ = 4.29140 S/m

Source:

"Electrical conductivity of the global ocean"

Earth, Planets and Space (2017).

https://doi.org/10.1186/s40623-017-0739-7

For this calculation:

σ = 4.2914 S/m

The calculation uses the following values established in the preceding

### Magnetic field

From Reference 033:

B = 19 μT

or:

B = 19 × 10^-6 T

### Water velocity

From Reference 035, the working value used in the book is:

v = 11.1 m/s

### Diameter of Rupes Nigra

D = 47 km

D = 47,000 m

### Submerged height of the structure

The working model assumes approximately:

H = 250 km

H = 250,000 m

---

## Surface exposed to the moving seawater

The circumference of a cylindrical structure with diameter 47 km is:

C = πD

C = π × 47,000

C ≈ 147,655 m

The submerged lateral surface is therefore:

A = C × H

A = 147,655 × 250,000

A ≈ 3.691 × 10^10 m²

or approximately:

36,914 km²

---

## Motional current density

Using:

J ≈ σvB

with:

σ = 4.2914 S/m

v = 11.1 m/s

B = 19 × 10^-6 T

gives:

J ≈ 4.2914 × 11.1 × 19 × 10^-6

J ≈ 9.05 × 10^-4 A/m²

Therefore:

J ≈ 0.000905 A/m²

or approximately:

0.905 mA/m²

---

## Idealized total current

Using the submerged lateral area:

I = JA

I ≈ 0.000905 × 3.691 × 10^10

I ≈ 3.34 × 10^7 A

Therefore:

# I ≈ 33,400,000 A

or approximately:

# 33 MA

---

## Result used in the book

Under the stated assumptions, the first idealized calculation gives an
electric-current scale of approximately:

# 33 million amperes

The book therefore reports the result as:

**≈33 MA**

---

## Important limitation

The calculated value is not a measured current and should not be interpreted
as proof that 33 MA would necessarily flow through the structure.

The full moving-conductor relation is:

J = σ(E + v × B)

In a real system, charge separation creates an electric field E that can oppose
the motional term.

The actual current would also depend on:

- orientation between velocity and magnetic field;
- electrical resistance of the volcanic structure;
- conductivity variations in seawater;
- conductivity of surrounding rock;
- geometry of the current paths;
- availability and resistance of a return path;
- electromagnetic feedback;
- spatial variation of the magnetic field.

The calculation therefore represents an idealized upper-bound or
short-circuit current scale for the working model.

---

## Interpretation

The purpose of this calculation is not to assume the existence of an enormous
electric current.

It tests what follows mathematically if the previously established working
parameters are combined with known motional electromagnetic induction.

For:

- a magnetic field of approximately 19 μT,
- conductive seawater,
- water velocity of 11.1 m/s,
- a 47 km diameter structure,
- and approximately 250 km of submerged contact,

the idealized model produces a current scale of approximately:

**33 MA.**

This result is used as the starting point for the next test in the book:
what electromagnetic and atmospheric effects such a current could produce
if transported through the central structure toward its summit.
