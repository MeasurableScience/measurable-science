# Reference 055 — East Australian Current: Warm Western Boundary Current

## Purpose

This reference supports the basic description of the East Australian Current (EAC) used in the book.

The East Australian Current is the major western boundary current of the South Pacific subtropical circulation.

It carries warm tropical and subtropical water poleward along the eastern coast of Australia and forms an important pathway for the southward transport of oceanic heat.

The EAC originates from the western boundary redistribution of waters arriving from the South Pacific, including branches associated with the South Equatorial Current.

Farther south, the current separates from the Australian coast and contributes to the Tasman Front and the highly energetic eddy field of the Tasman Sea.

## Relevance to the model

The book identifies the East Australian Current as the fourth major warm current examined after the Gulf Stream, Kuroshio and Brazil Current.

The purpose of this reference is only to establish the basic observed oceanographic character of the EAC:

- it is a major western boundary current,
- it transports warm water poleward,
- and it forms part of the large-scale South Pacific circulation.

It does not establish a geological or geothermal origin for the current.
