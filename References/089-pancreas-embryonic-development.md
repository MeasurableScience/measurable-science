## Relevance to the text

The pancreas begins developing during approximately the fifth embryonic week from two endodermal structures: the dorsal and ventral pancreatic buds.

As development proceeds, movement and rotation of the developing duodenum reposition the ventral pancreatic bud.

The ventral and dorsal pancreatic buds subsequently unite, with their ductal systems joining during approximately the seventh week.

The resulting pancreas contains both exocrine tissue and endocrine tissue organized into the islets of Langerhans.

The endocrine pancreas secretes hormones directly into the circulation, including insulin and glucagon, which are fundamental regulators of glucose availability and metabolic energy balance.

The pancreas therefore provides a biological regulatory system controlling the storage, release and utilization of one of the body's principal metabolic fuels.
