# Reference 024 — Gilles Elkaim, Arktika-2 and Svalbard authorities

## Source

Staalesen, A. (2017).
"French explorer challenges Svalbard governance."
The Barents Observer, 22 February 2017.

Source:
https://www.thebarentsobserver.com/arctic/french-explorer-challenges-svalbard-governance/114399

## Expedition

French explorer Gilles Elkaim planned an Arctic expedition aboard the
47-foot vessel Arktika-2.

The plan was to sail east toward the New Siberian Islands and then allow
the vessel to drift with the Arctic ice while the expedition continued
toward the North Pole using dog sleds.

The expedition was intended to operate in complete autonomy for
approximately two years.

## Intervention by Norwegian authorities

After weather conditions forced the expedition toward Svalbard,
Norwegian authorities intervened.

According to The Barents Observer:

- Arktika-2 was towed from Duve Fjord to Longyearbyen;
- passports and computers were confiscated;
- the expedition's dogs were denied entry;
- Elkaim was fined 25,000 NOK for entering the strictly protected
  Duve Fjord area.

Elkaim challenged the actions in court.

He argued that Norwegian authorities had improperly restricted his
freedom of navigation under the Svalbard Treaty.

Norwegian authorities maintained that Duve Fjord was subject to strict
environmental protection regulations and that the expedition had
violated those rules.

## Relevance to the book

This case demonstrates that a privately organised and highly autonomous
Arctic expedition attempting an unusual route toward the North Pole
encountered direct intervention by Norwegian authorities, including the
towing of the vessel and confiscation of passports and computers.

The incident does not by itself establish why access restrictions exist
or demonstrate that geographical information is being concealed.

It is included because it illustrates the practical difficulty of
treating the high Arctic as an area that can always be independently
investigated without governmental restrictions.
