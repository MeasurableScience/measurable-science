# Reference 007 — Chaos and the Primordial State

## Purpose

Sources supporting the discussion of primordial disorder, darkness,
nothingness and chaos in mythology, philosophy and science.

---

## Ancient Greek tradition

Hesiod, *Theogony*.

In Hesiod's cosmogony, Chaos appears at the beginning of the account,
before the later ordered structure of the cosmos.

This is used only as a historical example of chaos preceding cosmic order,
not as evidence for the physical model proposed in the book.

---

## Biblical tradition

*Genesis 1:1–2.*

The opening description presents the earth as formless and empty,
with darkness over the deep before the subsequent ordering of creation.

## Māori tradition

Te Ara — The Encyclopedia of New Zealand,
"Māori creation traditions."

Māori creation traditions commonly describe transitions involving
Te Kore, Te Pō and Te Ao: from nothingness or potential, through darkness,
toward light and the formed world.

Te Ara also records interpretations of Te Kore as a realm of
potential being.

Sources:

## Scientific chaos

In modern science, chaos theory concerns deterministic nonlinear systems
whose evolution can be extremely sensitive to small differences in
initial conditions.

The scientific meaning of "chaos" should not be confused with
mythological primordial chaos.
