# Reference 090 — Acute Thyrotropin Release After Birth

## Source

Fisher, D. A., & Odell, W. D. (1969).

**Acute release of thyrotropin in the newborn.**

*Journal of Clinical Investigation*, 48(9), 1670–1677.

DOI: 10.1172/JCI106132

PMID: 5822577

## Key Findings

Serum thyrotropin (TSH) was measured in maternal blood, cord blood, and in newborn infants during the early postnatal period.

Mean TSH concentration was approximately:

- 9.5 μU/ml in cord blood
- 60 μU/ml at 10 minutes after birth
- 86 μU/ml at 30 minutes after birth

After approximately 30 minutes, TSH concentrations declined rapidly.

The authors concluded that the rapid rise and fall may represent an acute release of stored pituitary TSH.

Importantly, maintaining newborn infants in a warm environment did not abolish the initial TSH surge, indicating that cooling alone could not account for the acute postnatal response.

## Relevance

The study demonstrates a rapid and powerful activation of the hypothalamic-pituitary-thyroid regulatory system immediately following birth.

The physiological event or combination of events responsible for initiating this neonatal endocrine transition remains more complex than a simple response to environmental cooling.
