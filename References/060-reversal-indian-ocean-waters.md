# Reference 060 — The Reversal Indian Ocean Waters

Liang, L., & Xue, H. (2020).

The Reversal Indian Ocean Waters.

Geophysical Research Letters,
47(14).

DOI:
10.1029/2020GL088269

RESULT:

The study investigates Indian Ocean waters moving in the direction opposite to the Indonesian Throughflow.

The authors identify two sources of these Reversal Indian Ocean Waters:

1. Australian Northwest Shelf Inflow linked with the Leeuwin Undercurrent.

2. South Java Coastal Undercurrent.

At approximately 500 m depth, the Northwest Shelf Inflow joins an equatorward flow supplied by the Leeuwin Undercurrent and continues northward.

Part of this northward-moving water enters the Savu Sea and Indonesian seas.

The South Java Coastal Undercurrent provides another pathway for Indian Ocean water entering the Indonesian seas in the direction opposite to the main Indonesian Throughflow.

After entering the Indonesian seas, these waters mix with surrounding water masses and tend to make them cooler, saltier and denser.

USED IN THE BOOK:

The northward subsurface pathway associated with the Leeuwin Undercurrent can be followed from western Australia toward the Indonesian seas.

Indian Ocean waters are therefore documented moving into the Indonesian seas opposite to the main Indonesian Throughflow.
