# Reference 018 — Magma transport through dykes and lateral propagation

## Primary source

Delaney, P. T., & Gartner, A. E. (1997).
Physical processes of shallow mafic dike emplacement near the
San Rafael Swell, Utah.
*Geological Society of America Bulletin*, 109(9), 1177–1192.

DOI: 10.1130/0016-7606(1997)109<1177:PPOSMD>2.3.CO;2

## Supporting source

Poland, M. P., Fink, J. H., & Tauxe, L. (2004).
Patterns of magma flow in segmented silicic dikes at Summer Coon
volcano, Colorado: AMS and thin section analysis.
*Earth and Planetary Science Letters*, 219(1–2), 155–169.

DOI: 10.1016/S0012-821X(03)00706-4

## Relevant findings

Field evidence shows that magma-filled dykes do not propagate only
vertically.

Delaney and Gartner report that lateral propagation at dike
peripheries can be as important as vertical propagation.

Poland, Fink and Tauxe found evidence of magma moving upward,
laterally away from the volcanic centre, and in some locations
downward along segmented dykes.

## Relevance to the book

These observations support the statement that molten material can
propagate through subsurface fractures in complex directions and can
form interconnected intrusive pathways.

They do not establish the scale or geometry of the subsurface system
proposed by the author.
