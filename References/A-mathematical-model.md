# Reference A — Mathematical Model of Relation, Energy, and Spiral Organization

## 1. Purpose

This reference develops the mathematical framework proposed in the first part of *Measurable Science*.

The central hypothesis is that fundamental units carry unmanifested potential, which becomes expressed as energy through their mutual relations.

Changes in those relations can produce attraction, repulsion, rotational action, organized movement, and the transformation of one form into another.

The model proposes the following sequence:

**UNMANIFESTED POTENTIAL → RELATION → MANIFESTED ENERGY → ATTRACTION / REPULSION / ROTATION → MOTION → ORGANIZATION → TRANSFORMATION.**

The neutron is considered a possible generator of matter, while the atom represents the first complete material form in the proposed sequence.

The objective is not to claim that the complete physical mechanism has already been experimentally demonstrated. It is to establish a mathematically consistent framework, identify its consequences, and make them available for further investigation.

**POTENTIAL IS MANIFESTED IN RELATION. ENERGY IS THE MANIFESTATION OF POTENTIAL.**

## 2. Fundamental Units and the First Relation

The fundamental units in my model are represented by neutrinos.

At this initial level, I propose that an isolated fundamental unit has no manifested energy, mass, or permanently assigned positive or negative polarity. It carries the potential to enter into relations.

For the simplest geometrical example, I consider three neutrinos:

```text
A, B, C
```

Each is assigned the same normalized initial potential:

```text
P_A = 1
P_B = 1
P_C = 1
```

These values are not experimentally measured neutrino potentials. The number 1 represents an equal initial potential and provides a simple basis for calculating relational differences.

The potential is not created by the relation. It already belongs to the fundamental unit.

**THE RELATION DETERMINES HOW THE EXISTING POTENTIAL IS MANIFESTED.**

I represent the relation between units i and j by:

```text
R_ij
```

When the relative phase is relevant, I use:

```text
phi_ij = phi_i - phi_j
```

Here, `phi_i` and `phi_j` represent the phase orientations of the two units.

The angles describe a mathematical relational geometry. They do not require that the initial units already possess a classical spherical shape.

In the geometrical examples, neutrinos are represented as small spheres only to make the proposed relationships easier to visualize.

## 4. Geometry of Rotation

A zero resultant force does not necessarily mean that every rotational contribution vanishes.

Consider a central sphere C acted upon by two equal forces from A and B.

If the forces are equal and opposite, their vector sum can be zero:

```text
F_A + F_B = 0
```

However, if they act along different lines, they can form a force couple.

The magnitude of torque is:

```text
T = r * F * sin(phi)
```

Where:

```text
T    torque
r    lever arm
F    force magnitude
phi  angle between lever arm and force
```

For a normalized illustration:

```text
r = 1

F = 1

phi = 90 degrees
```

Each contribution has magnitude:

```text
T = 1 * 1 * sin(90 degrees)

T = 1
```

If both contributions rotate the central sphere in the same direction:

```text
T_C = 1 + 1

T_C = 2
```

Thus, the central sphere can experience a nonzero torque even when its net translational force is zero.

**ZERO NET FORCE DOES NOT REQUIRE ZERO ROTATIONAL ACTION.**

This classical geometrical illustration assumes noncentral forces or interactions capable of generating a couple.

It does not establish that an actual neutrino is a rigid rotating sphere. In my model, it illustrates a possible organization of relational action.

The next step is to determine whether the same rotational contributions can be obtained from a single mathematical function describing relational energy.

## 5. Relational Energy — The Geometry of Manifestation

The central proposition of my model is that energy is manifested through relations rather than being an independently assigned property of an isolated fundamental unit.

I first describe the angular relation by:

```text
R_ij = cos(phi_i - phi_j)
```

I then construct a normalized relational-energy function by measuring the difference from the aligned reference state:

```text
E_ij = 1 - R_ij
```

Therefore:

```text
E_ij = 1 - cos(phi_i - phi_j)
```

This is the central normalized energy function used in the present model.

The function is dimensionless. Its values describe relative relational energy, not energy already calibrated in joules.

Its values are:

| Phase difference | E_ij |

| 30 degrees | 0.134 |
| 60 degrees | 0.5 |
| 90 degrees | 1 |

The result is straightforward:

**IDENTICAL INITIAL POTENTIALS CAN PRODUCE DIFFERENT RELATIONAL-ENERGY VALUES THROUGH DIFFERENT PHASE RELATIONS.**

At an angular difference of 0 degrees:

```text
E_ij = 1 - cos(0)

E_ij = 0
```

This zero represents the selected reference state of relational energy.

It does not mean that either neutrino has lost its underlying potential.

At 90 degrees:


```text
E_ij = 1 - cos(180)
```

The mathematical form is constructed directly from the geometrical cosine relation.

The additional definition that relational energy equals the difference between the reference value and the angular projection is a proposition of the model.

If physical energy units are required, a positive energy scale `K_ij` can be introduced:

The physical scale has not yet been derived from the fundamental potential or independently measured.

The normalized function is sufficient to examine the geometry of relational energy and its rotational response.

## 6. Energy and Change of Relation

If the phase relation changes, the relational-energy value changes.

For two configurations A and B:

```text
Delta_E_ij = E_ij(B) - E_ij(A)
```

With the normalized energy function:

```text
Delta_E_ij =
[1 - cos(phi_B)] - [1 - cos(phi_A)]
```

Therefore:

```text
Delta_E_ij = cos(phi_A) - cos(phi_B)
```

Consider a change from 180 degrees to 0 degrees.

Initially:

```text
E_ij(180) = 2
```

Finally:

```text
E_ij(0) = 0
```

The change is:

```text
Delta_E_ij = 0 - 2

Delta_E_ij = -2
```

The normalized relational energy has decreased by 2.

In a complete dynamical system, such a decrease can correspond to an increase in another form of energy, including organized motion, provided the system's conservation laws are satisfied.

The reverse transformation increases relational energy:

```text
0 degrees -> 180 degrees

Delta_E_ij = +2
```

Thus, the same underlying potentials can participate in different energetic arrangements without changing their initial assigned values.

**THE POTENTIAL REMAINS. THE RELATION CHANGES. THE MANIFESTATION OF ENERGY CHANGES WITH IT.**

A change of the relational-energy function alone does not yet establish a specific spatial force or physical trajectory.

Those require dynamical equations describing how the relational configuration evolves.

## 7. Three Neutrinos — Relational Energy and Rotational Balance

Two neutrinos establish one pairwise relation.

Three neutrinos permit three pairwise relations:

```text
R_AB
R_BC
R_CA
```

For the normalized model, the total relational-energy function is:

```text
E_odnosa =
    [1 - cos(phi_A - phi_B)]
  + [1 - cos(phi_B - phi_C)]
  + [1 - cos(phi_C - phi_A)]
```

Equivalently:

```text
E_odnosa = 3
         - cos(phi_A - phi_B)
         - cos(phi_B - phi_C)
         - cos(phi_C - phi_A)
```

This expression includes all three mutual relations.

I choose a simple arrangement:

```text
phi_A = 0 degrees

phi_B = 0 degrees

phi_C = -90 degrees
```

The pairwise energies are:

```text

E_AB = 0
```

```text

E_BC = 1
```


Therefore:

```text
E_odnosa = E_AB + E_BC + E_CA

E_odnosa = 0 + 1 + 1

E_odnosa = 2
```

**THREE INITIAL POTENTIALS — THREE RELATIONS — ONE ORGANIZED ENERGY CONFIGURATION.**

### 7.2 Rotational action from relational energy

When the energy of a system depends on a phase angle, the corresponding generalized rotational action is obtained from the negative partial derivative of its energy.

```text
T_i = -partial(E_odnosa)/partial(phi_i)
```

Angular derivatives are evaluated using radians.

For the three-neutrino system:

```text
T_A = -sin(phi_A - phi_B)
      -sin(phi_A - phi_C)
```

```text
T_B = -sin(phi_B - phi_A)
      -sin(phi_B - phi_C)
```

```text
T_C = -sin(phi_C - phi_A)
      -sin(phi_C - phi_B)
```

Substituting the initial phase configuration:

```text
phi_A = 0 degrees

phi_B = 0 degrees

phi_C = -90 degrees
```

For neutrino A:

```text
T_A = -sin(0) - sin(90)

T_A = 0 - 1

T_A = -1
```

For neutrino B:

```text
T_B = -sin(0) - sin(90)

T_B = 0 - 1

T_B = -1
```

For central neutrino C:

```text
T_C = -sin(-90) - sin(-90)

T_C = +1 + 1

T_C = +2
```

The result is:

```text
T_A = -1

T_B = -1

T_C = +2
```

Their total is:

```text
T_A + T_B + T_C = 0
```

Or:

```text
-1 - 1 + 2 = 0
```

**+2 - 1 - 1 = 0**

This is not an arbitrarily assigned distribution.

It follows mathematically from the normalized relational-energy function for the specified phase configuration.

The three units experience different rotational contributions while their total generalized torque is zero.

### 7.3 Balance does not mean absence of motion

The cancellation of the total torque does not imply that the individual torques vanish.

In an idealized rotational model with equal moments of inertia, the initial angular accelerations are proportional to:

```text
alpha_C : alpha_A : alpha_B = +2 : -1 : -1
```

The central unit initially accelerates in one direction, while the other two accelerate in the opposite direction.

If the system is isolated and its dynamics respect rotational symmetry, its total angular momentum is conserved.

Consequently, the organization can contain internal rotational motion without requiring a nonzero total angular momentum.

**A BALANCED WHOLE CAN CONTAIN NONZERO INTERNAL ACTIONS.**

This establishes a geometrical possibility for organized movement.

It does not by itself establish the intrinsic quantum spin of a neutrino or neutron.

## 8. From Rotational Action to Organized Motion

The geometrical example gives a system of interconnected phase relations.

Once movement begins, the angles are no longer fixed.

```text
phi_A = phi_A(t)

phi_B = phi_B(t)

phi_C = phi_C(t)
```

The relational-energy function becomes time-dependent through those angles:

```text
E_odnosa(t) =
    3
  - cos(phi_A(t) - phi_B(t))
  - cos(phi_B(t) - phi_C(t))
  - cos(phi_C(t) - phi_A(t))
```

The changing angles produce changing rotational actions.

The rotational actions, in turn, change the angular motion.

This is the essential feedback mechanism being investigated:

**MOTION CHANGES RELATIONS. CHANGED RELATIONS CHANGE THE ACTIONS THAT INFLUENCE MOTION.**

For an illustrative ideal mechanical realization, the phase dynamics may be written as:

```text
I_i * d2(phi_i)/dt2 = T_i
```

Where:

```text
I_i = assumed effective moment of inertia
```

For equal normalized inertias:

```text
I_A = I_B = I_C = 1
```

The full normalized energy can be written as:

```text
E_total = E_odnosa + E_kinetic
```

With:

```text
E_kinetic = (1/2) * SUM_i[I_i * omega_i^2]
```

And:

```text
omega_i = d(phi_i)/dt
```

For the ideal conservative model:

```text
d(E_total)/dt = 0
```

Therefore, relational energy may be converted into rotational kinetic energy and subsequently returned to relational energy.

This provides a mathematical example of organized internal motion without continuous external energy input.

For the symmetric initial configuration examined above, starting from zero angular velocities, the simple conservative model produces oscillatory relative motion.

Sustained rotation in one direction, spiral compression, or spiral expansion requires appropriate additional initial conditions or spatial dynamics.

The calculation therefore establishes the possibility of a recurring exchange between relational and kinetic energy, not the automatic formation of a stable physical particle.

**ORGANIZED MOTION CAN ARISE THROUGH THE MUTUAL RELATIONS OF THE UNITS.**

## 9. From Motion to Spiral Organization and Transformation

In a larger relational network, several units can interact simultaneously.

Their geometrical contributions need not cancel in every direction.

Once a spatial organization becomes meaningful, the resulting action may be resolved into radial and tangential components:

```text
D_i = D_r * r_hat + D_theta * theta_hat
```

Where:

```text
D_r      radial component

D_theta  tangential component

r_hat    radial direction

theta_hat tangential direction
```

The radial component can be associated with approach or separation.

The tangential component can contribute to turning.

If the two components occur together, a trajectory may have both a changing radius and a changing angle.

Its geometrical representation can be written as:

```text
r = r(theta)
```

When the radius decreases as the angle changes, the motion may describe an inward spiral.

When the radius increases, it may describe an outward spiral.

The spiral is not imposed as an initial objective of the system.

**SPIRAL ORGANIZATION IS ONE POSSIBLE RESULT OF COUPLED RELATIONAL ACTIONS.**

A complete calculation of spiral formation requires a spatial or radial interaction law in addition to the phase-energy function.

The present normalized phase model does not by itself determine radial trajectories.

### 9.1 Organization and persistent form

A spiral is a possible mode of organized movement, but it does not necessarily represent a persistent material form.

In my model, a form arises when relations become organized in a way that permits the structure to retain its identity through internal change.

I denote an organized form by:

```text
Omega
```

Its complete description may include:

```text
Omega = {units, relations, phases, organization}
```

The energy of the organized form is represented generally by:

```text

```

Here, `H` represents the total energetic organization of the form.

It may include contributions beyond the sum of the normalized pairwise relational energies, such as collective dynamics and the physical scale of the interactions.

The precise function `H` remains to be determined.

### 9.2 Transformation

Transformation represents a change in the organization of relations.

In my model, it can have two principal outcomes:

**FORMATION AND MAINTENANCE OF AN ORGANIZED STRUCTURE — OR ITS DISINTEGRATION.**

If the relations continue to support the organization, the form may persist.

If the organization is disrupted, the form may change or disintegrate.

The fundamental units are not assumed to lose their original potential during this process.

Instead, the manner in which that potential is manifested changes.

**TRANSFORMATION IS THE PROCESS THROUGH WHICH CHANGING RELATIONS ALTER THE ORGANIZED MANIFESTATION OF POTENTIAL, ALLOWING FORMS TO EMERGE, PERSIST, CHANGE, OR DISINTEGRATE.**

The model proposes that this general organizational principle can be investigated at multiple levels of nature.






**THE NORMALIZED GEOMETRICAL DISTRIBUTION HAS THE SAME NUMERICAL PROPORTIONS AS THE ELECTRIC CHARGES OF THE NEUTRON'S THREE VALENCE QUARKS.**

In the geometrical model, the original numbers describe normalized rotational contributions.

In the established neutron model, the corresponding fractions describe electric charges.

These are different physical quantities.

Their numerical correspondence motivates the investigation of a possible connection between relational organization and the emergence of charge.

### 10.2 Internal organization and neutrality

An electrically neutral particle need not lack internal electromagnetic properties or internal energy.

The general mathematical possibility explored in this reference is that nonzero internal contributions can form a balanced resultant.

**A NEUTRAL RESULTANT DOES NOT MEAN THE ABSENCE OF INTERNAL ACTION.**

In my model, I investigate whether a related principle of organized internal relations could contribute to the emergence of a neutron-like form.

## 11. Rest Energy and the Emergence of Mass

The neutron has a measured mass of approximately:

```text
m_n = 1.6749275 * 10^(-27) kg
```

Its corresponding rest energy is approximately:

```text
E_n = m_n * c^2

E_n = 939.5654 MeV
```

The mass of the neutron is not simply the sum of the masses of its three valence quarks.

Most of its rest energy is associated with the internal quark-gluon dynamics described by quantum chromodynamics.

This distinction is important for my model.

I propose that mass arises as a property of organized energy, rather than being assigned in advance to the isolated primordial fundamental units.

For an organized form `Omega`, the familiar mass-energy relation is:

```text
E_Omega = m_Omega * c^2
```

Therefore:

```text
m_Omega = E_Omega / c^2
```

The dimensions are consistent:

```text
[E_Omega] = J

[c^2] = m^2/s^2

[m_Omega] = J / (m^2/s^2) = kg
```

I define mass in my model as:

**MASS IS THE STATIC ENERGY OF AN ORGANIZED FORM.**

Here, static energy means the rest energy of the complete form.

It does not imply that the internal constituents or fields lack motion.

A system may possess substantial internal dynamics while having a definite total rest energy.

In the proposed model, no initial mass is assigned to an isolated primordial neutrino.

Mass is considered an emergent property of organized relational energy.

**MASS IS THE RESULT OF ENERGY ORGANIZATION.**

The measured neutron mass provides a quantitative target.

The normalized relational-energy function developed in this reference does not yet calculate the measured value of the neutron mass.

To establish that connection, the physical energy scale and the complete energy of the organized form would have to be determined.

## 13. Conclusions and Directions for Further Verification

The mathematical framework presented here establishes several results within the proposed normalized model.

First, the same initial potential can be associated with different relational-energy values depending on phase geometry.

Second, a system of three pairwise relations produces a definite normalized energy distribution for a specified initial configuration.

Third, the negative partial derivatives of that energy give nonzero individual rotational contributions whose total is zero:

```text
T_C = +2

T_A = -1

T_B = -1

T_A + T_B + T_C = 0
```

Fourth, under an explicitly stated conservative rotational dynamics, relational energy can be exchanged with kinetic energy while total energy and angular momentum remain conserved.

Fifth, the normalized rotational distribution has the same numerical proportions as the electric charges of the neutron's three valence quarks.

That correspondence is a motivation for further investigation, not an established identity between torque and electric charge.

The model still requires a complete physical description of several processes:

- How relational phase dynamics acquire an experimentally measurable energy scale.
- How radial motion and persistent spiral organization emerge from the underlying relations.
- Whether the proposed relational structure can reproduce measured particle properties, including electric charge, spin, magnetic moment, and mass.
- Whether the hypothesized connection between neutrinos and the formation or disintegration of organized matter produces independently testable predictions.

These open questions do not change the mathematical results obtained from the defined normalized function. They identify the additional work needed to connect those results to measurable physical systems.

The purpose of *Measurable Science* is to present the model, its mathematical possibilities, the relevant experimental observations, and an open invitation for independent verification.
