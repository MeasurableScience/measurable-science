## Evidence

The thalamus is a major gray-matter structure of the diencephalon composed of
multiple specialized nuclei.

Thalamic nuclei receive sensory, motor and other neural information and relay
selected information through thalamocortical pathways to the cerebral cortex.

The thalamus also participates in consciousness, alertness, sleep-wake
regulation, learning and memory.

With the exception of olfaction, sensory systems use thalamic nuclei as major
relay and processing stations before information reaches associated cortical
areas.

## Relevance to the text

The established anatomy supports describing the thalamus as a major biological
information junction.

For the simplified computer analogy used in the book:

pineal gland -> receiver
thalamus -> routing junction

"Routing junction" is an engineering analogy used by the author rather than
a neurological term.
