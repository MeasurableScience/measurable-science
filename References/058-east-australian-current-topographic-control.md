# Reference 058 — East Australian Current System and Topographic Control

## Purpose

This reference supports the statements in the book concerning the influence of submarine topography on the East Australian Current system.

Detailed reconstruction of the mean East Australian Current circulation shows that the complex bathymetry of the southwest Pacific strongly affects the organization and pathways of the regional currents.

Observed features include:

- division of incoming zonal flow into individual jets after passage through island chains,
- interaction between the flow and local topography during bifurcation,
- bathymetric control of EAC separation and recirculation within the Tasman Basin,
- interaction of the Tasman Front with submarine ridge systems.

The Tasman Basin is bounded and crossed by major bathymetric structures including the Lord Howe Rise and Norfolk Ridge.

## Relevance to the model

The book asks whether the fourth major warm current being examined also interacts measurably with large submarine structures.

This work establishes that the East Australian Current system is not dynamically independent of the ocean floor.

Its circulation, recirculation and downstream pathways are measurably constrained by bathymetry and submarine ridge systems.

This is evidence of current-topography interaction.

It is not evidence that those geological structures generate or heat the East Australian Current.

## Primary source

Ridgway, K. R., & Dunn, J. R. (2003).

Mesoscale Structure of the Mean East Australian Current System and Its Relationship with Topography.

Progress in Oceanography, 56.

Elsevier.
