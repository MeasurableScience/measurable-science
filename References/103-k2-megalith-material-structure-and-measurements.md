# K2 Megalith: Material, Structure and Reported Measurements

## Purpose

This reference collects published descriptions and reported physical measurements of the K2 megalith in the Ravne tunnel complex near Visoko, Bosnia and Herzegovina.

It separates observations and reported measurements from interpretations concerning the possible function of the object.

## K2 structure and material

K2 is a large megalithic object located inside the Ravne tunnel complex.

Published descriptions associated with the Bosnian Pyramid project report that its material differs substantially from the surrounding natural conglomerate.

The material has been described as sandstone-like and as having structural characteristics comparable to cement concrete.

K2 rests on a separate stone plinth or foundation rather than directly within the surrounding conglomerate.

Material reported between the supporting structure and the megalith has been described as calcium-carbonate based.

Other surveys of the Ravne complex describe K2 as anomalous in shape, dimensions and composition relative to the surrounding conglomerate and estimate its mass at approximately eight tonnes.

## Underground water

Published surveys and descriptions of the Ravne megaliths report underground water flows beneath the structures.

Some reports specifically describe intersecting or divided underground water flows beneath the megalithic locations.

This observation is relevant to the model developed in *Measurable Science*, although the interpretation of these flows as the source of an acoustic standing-wave system is the author's hypothesis.

## Reported electromagnetic and mechanical measurements

Measurements published by researchers associated with investigations of the Bosnian Pyramid complex reported electromagnetic and mechanical/acoustic signals in the tens-of-kilohertz range.

The measurement equipment included:

- TriField Meter Model 100X;
- Teslameter TM40;
- electromagnetic and sound-frequency probes;
- broadband amplification and digital frequency measurement equipment.

For K2, the published measurement table reports approximately:

**Electromagnetic frequency: 30 kHz**

**Mechanical / sound frequency: 28 kHz**

Nearby locations in the Ravne complex and other investigated structures produced reported electromagnetic frequencies broadly within approximately 28–33 kHz.

The authors explicitly state that their frequency measurements have an estimated accuracy of approximately ±15%, because the measured frequency depended on signal strength.

The important observation for the present investigation is therefore not an exact universal value of 28.000 kHz, but the reported presence of electromagnetic and mechanical oscillations within a similar tens-of-kilohertz frequency range.

## Interpretation

The investigators proposed piezoelectricity as a possible mechanism because quartz-bearing material can couple mechanical deformation and electrical polarization.

The following distinction is important:

**Reported observation:**
electromagnetic and mechanical/acoustic signals of similar frequency ranges were measured around K2 and other locations.

**Interpretation proposed by investigators:**
quartz-bearing material and piezoelectric coupling may be responsible.

**Hypothesis proposed in Measurable Science:**
K2 may represent a transformer positioned within a larger standing mechanical/acoustic system associated with underground water flow.

The last interpretation is not established by the cited measurements and is presented as a testable model.
