## Relevance to the text

The thyroid is the first endocrine gland to begin development.

Its development starts near the end of the third week of embryogenesis, approximately day 20–24, as endodermal cells in the primitive pharyngeal floor proliferate and form the thyroid diverticulum.

The developing thyroid subsequently descends through the neck while remaining temporarily connected to the tongue by the thyroglossal duct.

By approximately day 30 it has developed into a bilobed structure, and by about day 40 the original stalk connecting it to the pharyngeal floor atrophies and breaks.

The thyroid later produces hormones including T3 and T4 that have major effects on metabolic activity throughout the body.
