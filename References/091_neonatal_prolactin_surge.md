# Reference 091 — Prolactin and Thyrotropin During the Early Neonatal Period

## Source

**Serum thyrotropin, prolactin, and growth hormone levels during the early neonatal period in the human infant.**

PMID: 940027

## Key Findings

Cord blood and postnatal venous blood were collected from human newborn infants during the first hours after birth.

Mean serum prolactin concentration increased from:

- 165 ± 15 ng/ml in cord blood
- 214 ± 22 ng/ml at 30 minutes
- 204 ± 17 ng/ml at 60 minutes

Prolactin increased after birth in all 15 infants studied.

In the same study, mean TSH increased from approximately 12 μU/ml in cord blood to 67 μU/ml at 30 minutes after birth.

Triiodothyronine (T3) also increased substantially during the following two hours.

## Relevance

Birth is accompanied by a coordinated neuroendocrine transition involving more than the thyroid axis alone.

The simultaneous postnatal changes in prolactin, TSH and thyroid hormones demonstrate that the transition from fetal to independent life involves rapid changes in central endocrine regulation.

The neonatal prolactin increase cannot be interpreted simply in terms of lactation by the newborn and reflects the broader physiological functions of prolactin.
