
# Breast Stimulation: Oxytocin, ACTH and Genital Sensory Cortex

## Purpose

This reference documents three experimental studies relevant to the breast-stimulation stage described in Measurable Science.

The studies measured hormonal responses to mechanical breast stimulation and brain responses to nipple stimulation.

They did not investigate the complete sexual sequence described in the book.

## 1. Oxytocin response to breast stimulation

Pignatti, D., et al. (1992).
Inhibition by ethanol of the oxytocin response to breast stimulation in normal women and the role of endogenous opioids.

Acta Endocrinologica, 126(3), 213–216.

DOI:
https://doi.org/10.1530/acta.0.1260213

Sixteen healthy women were tested on day 22 of four consecutive regular menstrual cycles.

Each participant underwent 10 minutes of mechanical breast stimulation under four experimental conditions involving saline, naloxone, ethanol, or ethanol combined with naloxone.

Under the saline condition, plasma oxytocin increased approximately twofold, reaching its mean peak at 10 minutes.

Ethanol abolished this response, while naloxone partially reversed ethanol's inhibitory effect.

The study demonstrates an acute oxytocin response to mechanical breast stimulation in normally menstruating women.

It does not establish the hormonal response during partnered sexual activity or demonstrate long-term changes in oxytocin.

## 2. Oxytocin and the stress-response system

Chiodera, P., et al. (1991).
Relationship between plasma profiles of oxytocin and adrenocorticotropic hormone during suckling or breast stimulation in women.

DOI:
https://doi.org/10.1159/000181886

PubMed:
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1666892/

The study investigated seven lactating women during suckling and six normally menstruating women during mechanical breast stimulation.

Each participant also underwent a control condition involving sham stimulation.

The researchers measured plasma oxytocin, adrenocorticotropic hormone (ACTH), and cortisol.

Suckling and mechanical breast stimulation significantly increased oxytocin and decreased ACTH.

A significant negative correlation was found between the oxytocin increase and the simultaneous ACTH decrease.

Cortisol concentrations were lower during suckling or breast stimulation than under control conditions.

These findings demonstrate an inverse relationship between oxytocin and ACTH responses under the experimental conditions.

They do not establish that oxytocin directly caused the entire ACTH decrease or that sexual stimulation necessarily produces the same hormonal pattern.

## 3. Nipple stimulation and genital sensory cortex

Komisaruk, B. R., Wise, N., Frangos, E., Liu, W.-C., Allen, K., & Brody, S. (2011).

Women's clitoris, vagina, and cervix mapped on the sensory cortex: fMRI evidence.

The Journal of Sexual Medicine, 8(10), 2822–2830.

DOI:
https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1743-6109.2011.02388.x

Full text:
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3186818/

PMID: 21797981

Eleven healthy women participated in an fMRI experiment involving separate self-stimulation of the clitoris, vagina, cervix, and nipple.

The researchers also used thumb and great-toe stimulation as reference conditions.

Clitoral, vaginal, and cervical stimulation activated distinguishable regions of the medial sensory cortex.

Nipple stimulation activated both the thoracic sensory representation and the genital sensory cortex.

The unexpected genital-cortex response provides a possible neurological explanation for the erotic sensations some women report during nipple stimulation.

However, the experiment did not measure vaginal blood flow, lubrication, uterine contractions, or readiness for penetration.

Activation of the genital sensory cortex should not be interpreted as proof that genital physiological arousal necessarily occurred.

## Evidence classification

MEASUREMENT

- Approximately twofold increase in plasma oxytocin after 10 minutes of mechanical breast stimulation in one experimental condition.
- Increased oxytocin and decreased ACTH during breast stimulation in normally menstruating women.
- Activation of the genital sensory cortex during nipple self-stimulation.

MATHEMATICAL ANALYSIS

- Hormonal concentration comparisons between experimental and control conditions.
- Correlation between changes in oxytocin and ACTH.
- Statistical analysis of fMRI activation patterns.

HYPOTHESIS

The sequence proposed in Measurable Science suggests that breast stimulation may contribute to physiological preparation for subsequent sexual activity.

The three studies provide separate measurements relevant to that hypothesis.

They do not demonstrate that the proposed sequence produces a particular combined hormonal or neural state, or that it leads to the final experience described in the book.

## Conclusion

Breast and nipple stimulation can produce measurable hormonal and neural responses.

These findings provide experimentally documented physiological reference points for the breast-stimulation stage of the proposed method.

The effects of combining this stage with preceding kissing and subsequent genital stimulation remain to be tested.
