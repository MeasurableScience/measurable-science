# Reference 046 — New England Seamount Chain and Gulf Stream Interaction

## Source

Chassignet, E. P., Xu, X., Bozec, A., & Uchida, T. (2023).

**Impact of the New England Seamount Chain on Gulf Stream Pathway and Variability.**

*Journal of Physical Oceanography*, 53(8).

DOI: 10.1175/JPO-D-23-0008.1

Original publication:
https://journals.ametsoc.org/view/journals/phoc/53/8/JPO-D-23-0008.1.xml

---

## What was investigated

The study examined the influence of the New England Seamount Chain on the pathway and variability of the Gulf Stream.

The New England Seamount Chain is a major volcanic seamount system in the northwestern Atlantic located beneath the region crossed by the Gulf Stream.

The authors used numerical ocean simulations to compare the behaviour of the Gulf Stream with realistic bathymetry against experiments in which the New England Seamount Chain was removed from the model topography.

---

## Main result relevant to the book

The experiments show that the New England Seamount Chain has a strong influence on the Gulf Stream pathway and its variability.

When the seamount chain was removed from the model bathymetry, the Gulf Stream remained more coherent farther east.

With realistic bathymetry, interaction with the New England Seamount Chain contributes to the observed downstream evolution and variability of the current.

Therefore, the geographical relationship between the Gulf Stream and the volcanic seamount chain is not simply a visual coincidence on two maps.

The seamount chain physically interacts with the ocean current strongly enough for its influence to appear in oceanographic modelling.

---

## Relevance to the model

The model developed in *Measurable Science* predicts that if heated water returns from a branching deep system into the ocean, traces of that system should be sought beneath major warm ocean currents.

The Gulf Stream provides the first test.

A major volcanic structure exists beneath its pathway:

**New England Seamount Chain**

and published oceanographic research independently demonstrates measurable interaction between the Gulf Stream and this seamount system.

This does not by itself demonstrate that geothermal heat from the seamount chain heats the Gulf Stream.

It establishes the narrower observational point required at this stage:

**a major warm ocean current crosses and physically interacts with a major volcanic seamount chain.**

The origin of any heat associated with deep water in this region must be tested independently.
