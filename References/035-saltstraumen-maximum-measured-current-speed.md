# Reference 035 — Maximum measured current speed at Saltstraumen

## Source

Norwegian Environment Agency / Naturbase

Management plan information for the Saltstraumen Marine Protected Area.

Source:

https://fpnv.naturbase.no/Plan/View?naturvernId=VV00003081


## Relevant measurement

The official description of the Saltstraumen Marine Protected Area
identifies Saltstraumen as one of the world's strongest tidal currents.

The source gives the following physical characteristics:

- sill depth: approximately 26 m;
- surface width at the entrance: approximately 255 m;
- average water transport across the sill: approximately 3,500 m3/s;
- maximum measured water velocity: 20 knots / 37 km/h.

The stated maximum measured velocity therefore corresponds to:

20 knots ≈ 10.3 m/s

or:

37 km/h ≈ 10.3 m/s.


## Independent scientific context

Eliassen et al. (2001) studied the circulation of Saltfjorden,
Saltstraumen and Skjerstadfjorden using a three-dimensional
hydrodynamic model.

The study describes Saltstraumen as a highly energetic tidal
system produced by the exchange of water through the narrow
channel connecting the fjords.

Citation:

Eliassen, I. K., Heggelund, Y., & Berntsen, J. (2001).

"A numerical study of the circulation in Saltfjorden,
Saltstraumen and Skjerstadfjorden."

Continental Shelf Research, 21(15), 1669–1689.

DOI:

https://doi.org/10.1016/S0278-4343(01)00019-X


## Working value used in the book

For the physical model developed in the book, the maximum
measured Saltstraumen velocity is rounded to:

10.3 m/s

This value is used as a real-world upper-end example of the
velocity attainable by a natural tidal current.

It does not establish that water around the hypothetical
Rupes Nigra structure moved at this velocity.

Applying this measured velocity to that proposed system is
an assumption of the author's model.


## Relevance to the book

This reference establishes that natural tidal water flows
can attain measured velocities of approximately 10 m/s.

The book uses this measured natural value as a working
velocity when testing the electromagnetic consequences
of rapidly moving salt water around the proposed central
magnetic structure.
