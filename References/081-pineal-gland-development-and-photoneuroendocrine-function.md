## Evidence

The human pineal gland develops during the second month of gestation as an
evagination of the ependymal/neuroepithelial region associated with the third
ventricle at the diencephalic-mesencephalic border.

The pineal gland is part of the epithalamic/diencephalic region and remains
connected to this region through the pineal stalk.

The mature pineal gland is a neuroendocrine organ whose principal secretory
product is melatonin.

Its activity is regulated by the environmental light-dark cycle and contributes
to the synchronization of circadian biological rhythms.

## Relevance to the text

The anatomical evidence establishes the pineal gland as a centrally located
neuroendocrine structure associated with the diencephalic system.

Its established biological function connects environmental light-dark
information with internal chemical and circadian signaling.

This supports its description in the book as the first "island" associated
with reception and biological rhythm.

The interpretation of the pineal gland as an antenna or receiver connected
to a larger external information system is the author's model.
