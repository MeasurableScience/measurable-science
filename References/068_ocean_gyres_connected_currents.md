# Reference 068 — Ocean gyres and connected boundary currents

Woods Hole Oceanographic Institution (WHOI).

**Currents, gyres, & eddies**

## Link

https://www.whoi.edu/ocean-learning-hub/ocean-topics/how-the-ocean-works/ocean-circulation/currents-gyres-eddies/

## Relevance

Woods Hole Oceanographic Institution describes ocean gyres as large-scale rotating circulation systems thousands of miles across, bounded by major permanent ocean currents.

For the subtropical gyres, WHOI describes four linked currents:

- two boundary currents oriented approximately north–south along the eastern and western sides of the gyre;
- two currents oriented approximately east–west along its northern and southern sides.

WHOI identifies the Gulf Stream, Kuroshio and Agulhas among the major western boundary currents.

These linked currents transport water, heat, salt, nutrients and other materials over large distances and form components of the global ocean circulation.

This reference supports the statement in *Merljiva nauka* that the large north–south current systems discussed in the text are not isolated flows, but are connected across the ocean basins by large-scale gyre circulation.
