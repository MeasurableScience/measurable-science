# Reference 073 — Plasma Dynamic States and Memory Effects

## Purpose

This reference supports the statement in *Merljiva nauka* that plasma can sustain dynamic collective electromagnetic states and that its present response can, under specific physical conditions, depend on previous excitation.

It provides the scientific basis for the author's functional comparison:

**plasma layer → candidate for working memory / dynamic system state**

The comparison with computer RAM is the author's model and is not a claim made by the cited scientific literature.

## Scientific basis

### Collective plasma states

Plasma is a collective system of charged particles whose dynamics are coupled to electric and magnetic fields.

Plasmas can support organized collective oscillations and numerous wave modes.

The state of a plasma can therefore be represented by time-dependent physical quantities including:

- charged-particle distributions;
- particle density and velocity;
- electric fields;
- magnetic fields;
- collective oscillations and wave modes.

Bohm and Gross established the theoretical description of organized electron oscillations in plasma and showed how collective organized behavior emerges from particle dynamics.

### Plasma waves

Plasma supports longitudinal and transverse wave modes whose properties depend on the physical state of the plasma.

Measurements of plasma waves can provide information about plasma density, temperature, particle distribution functions and fluctuation spectra.

This demonstrates that the electromagnetic and collective state of a plasma contains physically measurable information about the system.

### Memory effects

Plasma systems can exhibit memory effects in which the response to a new excitation depends upon physical conditions remaining from previous excitations.

In repetitively pulsed discharges, residual charged particles, surface charges, excited species and other remnants of previous discharge events can influence subsequent discharge development.

Experimental studies have directly observed surface-charge memory effects affecting later ionization-wave dynamics.

### Hysteresis

Plasma discharges can also exhibit hysteresis.

Lee and Chung experimentally and theoretically demonstrated strong hysteresis associated with evolution of the electron energy distribution in plasma.

Hysteresis means that the present state of the physical system can depend upon its previous history rather than solely upon its instantaneous external parameters.

### Plasma echo

An especially important example of information persistence in plasma is the plasma-wave echo.

After an initially organized plasma wave undergoes phase mixing and its macroscopic signal disappears, information associated with that excitation can remain encoded in microscopic particle motion.

Under appropriate subsequent excitation, a macroscopic echo can appear.

This demonstrates that information about a previous plasma excitation can persist even after the original macroscopic wave is no longer directly visible.

## Important limitation

The cited literature does NOT demonstrate that natural atmospheric or ionospheric plasma functions as computer RAM.

It also does not demonstrate digital addressing, binary storage, controlled read/write operations, or the specific information-processing architecture proposed in *Merljiva nauka*.

The literature establishes the narrower physical facts required for the analogy:

1. plasma supports dynamic collective electromagnetic states;
2. those states change in response to excitation;
3. previous excitation can influence later plasma states;
4. physical information concerning previous excitation can persist under particular plasma conditions;
5. plasma states and waves can be physically measured.

The interpretation of a plasma layer as the working memory of the proposed natural-computer architecture is the author's hypothesis.

**magnetized volcanic rock → long-term / non-volatile storage**

**plasma → dynamic / working-state storage**
