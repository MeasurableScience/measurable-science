
# Hyper-Brain Networks During Romantic Kissing

## Primary source

Müller, V., & Lindenberger, U. (2014).
Hyper-Brain Networks Support Romantic Kissing in Humans.
PLOS ONE, 9(11), e112080.

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0112080

Full text:
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4222975/

PMID: 25375132

## Research objective

The study investigated oscillatory coupling within and between the brains of romantic partners during kissing.

The researchers used simultaneous EEG recordings, cross-frequency coupling analysis, and graph-theoretical network measures.

## Participants

Twenty heterosexual couples who reported being in love participated.

Five couples were excluded because of recording artifacts.

The final analysis included 15 couples (30 participants).

Participants were 18–37 years old, with a mean age of 25.4 years.

## Experimental conditions

The study compared three kissing conditions:

1. Kissing one's own hand.
2. Romantic kissing between partners.
3. Kissing one's partner while performing silent mental arithmetic.

Both partners' EEG signals were recorded simultaneously. Lip electromyography (EMG) was also recorded.

## Measurements and findings

The researchers constructed hyper-brain networks incorporating connections within individual brains and between the two partners' brains.

A prominent theta–alpha subnetwork included oscillatory nodes at 5 Hz and 10 Hz.

Network strength was greater and characteristic path length was shorter during partner-oriented kissing than during kissing one's own hand.

Stronger connections involving 5-Hz nodes, particularly inter-brain connections, correlated with greater self-reported partner-oriented kissing satisfaction.

Intra-brain network strength involving 10-Hz nodes correlated with self-reported kissing quality.

These findings concern statistical properties of EEG-derived networks, not the detection of an independent physical field between the partners.

## Methodological limitations

The analyzed sample contained 15 couples.

Five participating couples were excluded because of EEG recording artifacts.

The romantic-kissing condition always preceded the kissing-with-arithmetic condition, so some observed differences may have been influenced by task order.

The study was observational with respect to the association between network measures and subjective satisfaction. Correlation does not establish that a particular oscillation caused pleasure or emotional bonding.

## Relationship to the carrier hypothesis

The book separately reports an author's measurement of a 9-Hz component on what the author calls the carrier.

The Müller and Lindenberger study did not measure that carrier, did not investigate a 9-Hz carrier modulation, and did not test whether EEG coupling causes or corresponds to signals in an external physical medium.

The EEG findings and the author's reported carrier measurement therefore remain separate observations.

A direct test would require simultaneous EEG recordings from both participants and independently recorded carrier data, with synchronized timestamps, suitable controls, predefined signal-processing methods, and blinded analysis.

Only such an experiment could begin to test whether changes in inter-brain coordination are systematically associated with the proposed carrier signal.

## Evidence classification

MEASUREMENT:
Simultaneous EEG and EMG recordings from 15 couples.

MATHEMATICAL ANALYSIS:
Cross-frequency coupling, hyper-brain network construction, graph-theoretical measures, and correlations with reported kissing satisfaction.

HYPOTHESIS:
The author's proposed relationship between inter-brain coordination and a separately observed 9-Hz carrier component.

The hypothesis is not established by the cited experiment.
