# Schumann Discrimination Test

## Purpose

The carrier structures identified in the institutional magnetic datasets occur in the same general ELF frequency region as the first Schumann resonance mode.

For that reason, frequency proximity alone is not sufficient to distinguish the measured carrier structures from ordinary Schumann-resonance activity.

This test therefore does not ask whether a spectral feature exists near 8 Hz.

It asks a different question:

**Does the phase-organized structure identified by the blind carrier analysis behave as a persistent H-D relationship, or can it be adequately described simply by its proximity to the first Schumann-resonance band?**

The central methodological rule remained unchanged:

> The program must not know the result it is expected to find.

---

## Why frequency alone is insufficient

The first Schumann resonance mode occurs in approximately the same frequency region as the carrier structures identified in the institutional datasets.

The representative carrier frequencies obtained independently were:

| Location | Representative frequency |
|---|---:|
| CARISMA, Canada | 7.734 Hz |
| Eskdalemuir, Scotland | 7.748 Hz |
| ISTOK, Russia | 7.667 Hz |
| Kunigami, Japan | 7.896 Hz |

A peak near 7–8 Hz therefore cannot by itself establish the physical identity of the measured structure.

For this reason, the analysis was extended from frequency to simultaneous two-component phase organization.

---

## What was tested

For the institutional datasets, the two simultaneously measured horizontal magnetic components were analyzed through their instantaneous phase relationship.

The principal quantity was the phase-locking value:

PLV = |mean(exp(i × (phi_H - phi_D)))|

The test therefore examined not only where spectral activity occurred, but whether the two magnetic components maintained an organized relationship in time.

Additional diagnostics included:

- phase difference between H and D,
- local polarization geometry,
- signed phase-plane rotation,
- temporal persistence,
- shifted-channel controls,
- and permutation testing of the relationship between phase organization and rotation direction.

---

## ISTOK simultaneity control

ISTOK provided a direct internal control.

For the simultaneous H-D measurements:

- 1,434 windows were analyzed,
- median PLV = 0.477285,
- all 1,434 windows had the same signed rotation direction.

The test was then repeated after shifting one component by five minutes.

After the shift:

- median PLV fell to 0.075219,
- 690 windows were clockwise,
- 744 windows were counter-clockwise,
- the rotation distribution became approximately 48% / 52%.

The organized relationship therefore depended strongly on the simultaneity of the two measured components.

This control does not determine the physical origin of the structure.

It demonstrates that the measured H-D organization is temporal and is not preserved when the simultaneous relationship between the channels is destroyed.

---

## Eskdalemuir long-duration test

Eskdalemuir provided the longest clean temporal test.

Accepted clean windows covered the period from:

**19 June 2012 to 31 December 2012**

A total of 2,502 clean 60-second windows were retained after objective rejection of large raw discontinuities and incomplete records.

Across the complete clean dataset:

- clockwise windows: 2,397
- counter-clockwise windows: 105
- clockwise fraction: 95.80%

The relationship became substantially stronger when only windows with greater H-D phase organization were retained.

| Minimum PLV | Windows | CW | CCW | CW fraction |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.00 | 2,502 | 2,397 | 105 | 95.80% |
| 0.10 | 2,121 | 2,093 | 28 | 98.68% |
| 0.20 | 1,665 | 1,661 | 4 | 99.76% |
| 0.25 | 1,543 | 1,542 | 1 | 99.94% |
| 0.30 | 1,425 | 1,425 | 0 | 100.00% |
| 0.40 | 1,042 | 1,042 | 0 | 100.00% |
| 0.50 | 492 | 492 | 0 | 100.00% |

At PLV >= 0.30, every retained window had the same signed rotation direction:

**1,425 / 1,425 CW**

This was not a single-hour or single-day observation.

It persisted within a dataset extending for more than six months.

---

## Permutation test

A permutation test was used to determine whether the association between high PLV and clockwise rotation could be explained merely by the already high overall clockwise fraction of 95.80%.

The rotation labels were randomly permuted relative to the measured PLV values while preserving:

- the original PLV distribution,
- the total number of CW windows,
- the total number of CCW windows.

For PLV >= 0.30:

- observed result: 1,425 / 1,425 CW
- permutations: 10,000
- mean null result: 1,365.17 / 1,425 CW
- best null result: 1,384 / 1,425 CW
- permutations reproducing the observation: 0

Therefore:

**Monte Carlo p < 10^-4**

This test specifically addresses the statistical association between strong H-D phase organization and rotation direction.

It does not by itself determine the physical origin of that organization.

---

## Result

The carrier analysis did not rely on the existence of a spectral maximum near the first Schumann-resonance frequency.

The measured structures also showed organized relationships between independently measured horizontal magnetic components.

Two particularly strong observations were obtained:

1. At ISTOK, destroying simultaneity between H and D strongly reduced PLV and eliminated the one-directional rotation pattern.

2. At Eskdalemuir, increasing H-D phase organization progressively selected a single rotation direction, reaching 1,425 / 1,425 windows at PLV >= 0.30 across a dataset extending for more than six months.

The permutation test showed that the Eskdalemuir high-PLV result was not explained simply by the overall 95.80% clockwise baseline.

---

## Interpretation

These results are sufficient to show that frequency proximity alone does not describe the measured carrier structures.

The structures contain measurable temporal and phase-geometric organization that must be considered independently of their location near the first Schumann-resonance band.

This analysis does not claim that Schumann resonances do not exist, nor does it attempt to redefine them.

It establishes a narrower result:

**A frequency near 8 Hz is not sufficient to identify the measured carrier structures as Schumann modes.**

For this reason, the structures identified in the carrier analysis are not identified here as Schumann modes solely on the basis of frequency proximity.

Their physical origin remains a separate question.
