# Equipment

This directory documents all measurement equipment used throughout the development of **Measurable Science**.

The research evolved over several years. Different stages of the project required different instruments, beginning with simple analog measurements and progressing toward higher-precision digital sensors.

The purpose of this section is not only to document the equipment used in the project, but also to help other researchers reproduce the measurements.

Each device includes information about:

- its purpose within the project,
- technical specifications,
- where it can be obtained,
- how it should be connected,
- how it should be mounted and used,
- practical recommendations based on field experience,
- photographs where available,
- and links to the recording and analysis software used in this project.

## Equipment Timeline

### 1. Hand-Wound Induction Coil

The earliest experiments used a hand-wound induction coil placed around a quartz crystal and connected to a computer audio interface.

This setup was primarily used for low-frequency signal detection before dedicated digital sensors were introduced.

### 2. WitMotion WT901C

The WT901C became the primary instrument used throughout the project.

It records orientation, acceleration, angular velocity and magnetic field simultaneously and was used for the majority of field measurements.

Official product page:

https://www.wit-motion.com/StandardPrecisionTilt/83.html

### 3. RM3100 Fluxgate Magnetometer

The RM3100 is currently used for high-sensitivity magnetic field measurements.

Its significantly higher magnetic sensitivity makes it suitable for experiments focused on weak magnetic field variations.

Official product page:

https://witmotion-sensor.com/collections/magnetometer/products/witmotion-hwt3100-three-axis-electronic-compass-geomagnetic-sensor
